\title{Interpretable Causal Inference with Optimal Decision Trees\\[2pt]
\large Theory}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We develop inference for the average treatment effect on the treated when both
nuisance functions --- the propensity score and the control-outcome regression
--- are estimated by globally optimal decision trees. The covariate space is
finite with cardinality fixed in the sample size, and the leaf budget is fixed,
so the class of candidate tree partitions is a fixed finite set. Our main result
is that a single tree per nuisance, with both the partition and the leaf values
fitted on all $n$ observations, yields $\sqrt n$-consistent, asymptotically
normal, and locally asymptotically efficient estimation of the target, with
consistent variance estimation by the ordinary empirical influence-function
plug-in. No sample splitting, cross-fitting, or tail condition is used. Two
structural facts drive the argument: the induced estimand is invariant across
every partition rich enough to represent the truth, so data-adaptive selection
of the tree structure cannot move the target; and a positive population risk
margin is a derived consequence of exact global optimisation over a finite
class, rather than an assumption. We further show that the fixed-grid
requirement can be relaxed: a two-stage estimator that recovers the tree
topology on a refining grid and then refines each threshold off-grid attains
threshold accuracy $O_p(n^{-1})$ and inherits the same limit distribution and
variance estimator. We further give a cross-fitted estimator that
dispenses with the structural sparsity condition, and confidence intervals that
remain valid, at a stated and estimable price in width, when sparsity fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and organisation}\label{sec:scope}

This document is the canonical statement of the theory, together with its
proofs. Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes notation and collects every standing
assumption; assumptions are defined there and nowhere else, and are invoked by
label throughout. Section~\ref{sec:eif} records the efficient influence function
and the exact second-order remainder. Section~\ref{sec:structure} develops the
structure of the tree class: sufficiency, invariance, the margin, selection
consistency, and how the selection rule is computed. Section~\ref{sec:main} contains the principal result, full-sample
inference with two optimal trees. Section~\ref{sec:twostage} relaxes the
requirement that the truth align with a fixed grid: a coarse-grid search
recovers the tree topology, an off-grid scan refines each threshold at rate
$n^{-1}$, and the same central limit theorem and variance estimator survive.
Section~\ref{sec:crossfit} treats the
cross-fitted estimator, which requires no sparsity condition and serves as the
anchor for Section~\ref{sec:honest}, where inference remains valid but
inefficient when sparsity fails. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the
results and their relation to adjacent literatures.

Proofs are collected in Appendices~\ref{app:eif}--\ref{app:honest}, keyed to
result numbers; auxiliary lemmas are in Appendix~\ref{app:aux} and the
counterexamples are worked in full in Appendix~\ref{app:counterex}. A proof is
given inline only where the proof is the substance of the result and occupies a
few lines, with one deliberate exception: Section~\ref{sec:twostage}'s proofs
are inline in full, because the section is added self-containedly and its
results are not yet keyed into the appendix numbering. Every numbered
statement in this document has a proof. Claims that
are not proved appear as unnumbered prose in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

Two bodies of theory are deliberately excluded and are developed in a companion
paper: approximation and oracle theory for optimal trees on continuous
covariates, where the leaf budget must grow with $n$ and entropy conditions
become active; and Rashomon sets, together with the structure-intersection
results that support them. Neither is used by any result below.

Appendix~\ref{app:crosswalk} maps each numbered result to its destination in the
submitted paper.

\section{Setup and standing assumptions}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Data, target, and score}

Let $O=(X,A,Y)$ with covariate $X$, binary treatment $A\in\{0,1\}$, and outcome
$Y\in\R$, distributed according to $P$. Let $Y(1),Y(0)$ denote the potential
outcomes. Write
\[
  \pi=\PP(A=1),\qquad
  \ez(x)=\PP(A=1\mid X=x),\qquad
  \mz(x)=\E[Y\mid A=0,X=x],
\]
and let $m_1(x)=\E[Y\mid A=1,X=x]$ and $\sigma_a^2(x)=\Var(Y\mid A=a,X=x)$. The
target is the average treatment effect on the treated,
\[
  \theta_0=\E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid A=1].
\]
The ATT weight is $\wz=\ez/(1-\ez)$, and for a candidate propensity $e$ we write
$w=e/(1-e)$. A candidate nuisance pair is $\eta=(e,\mu)$, with $\eta_0=(\ez,\mz)$
the truth. It is convenient to index the two nuisances by $j\in\{e,\mu\}$ and to
write $\gamma_{0,e}=\ez$, $\gamma_{0,\mu}=\mz$.

We write $\Pn$ for the empirical measure, $\Pn f=n^{-1}\sum_{i\le n}f(O_i)$, and
$\pihat=\Pn A$. Two norms on functions of $x$ are used throughout:
\[
  \Ltwo f^2=\E\bigl[f(X)^2\bigr],
  \qquad
  \Lw f^2=\E\bigl[\{1-\ez(X)\}f(X)^2\bigr],
\]
the second being the control-weighted norm. Convergence in distribution is
denoted $\rightsquigarrow$, and $z_{1-\alpha/2}$ is the standard normal
quantile.

\subsection{Covariate space and tree partitions}

$\cX$ need not itself be finite; it may be any measurable space on which the
covariate law $P_X$ and the nuisances $\ez,\mz$ are well defined (a finite
product $\cX=\prod_{j\le d}\cX_j$ as in earlier drafts of this document is
the special case in which every coordinate's cutpoint family below is its
full value set). What is finite, and fixed in $n$, is not $\cX$ but the
\emph{candidate partition family}, generated by a pre-specified finite
collection of cutpoints on each coordinate --- institutional thresholds such
as age brackets, income bands, or clinical cutoffs, chosen by the analyst
before seeing the data and never updated by it. These cutpoints partition
$\cX$ into a finite family of \emph{grid atoms} $\cA$, $\card\cA<\infty$
fixed in $n$, each atom a product of one inter-cutpoint interval (or
category group) per coordinate; write $P(a)$ for the mass of $a\in\cA$.
When $\cX$ is itself finite, atoms coincide with
the cells $x\in\cX$ of earlier drafts and $P(a)=p_x$.

A \emph{tree partition} $\tau$ of $\cX$ is a partition induced by recursive
binary splits \emph{at the pre-specified cutpoints only}; its blocks are
\emph{leaves}, each leaf a union of atoms, $\card\tau$ is its leaf count, and
$\ell_\tau(x)$ is the leaf containing $x$. Restricting splits to the
pre-specified cutpoints is Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} below, not a
convention: without it the results of this section do not apply, since the
candidate class ceases to be fixed and finite. For a leaf budget $\bar L$,
\[
  \cT_{\bar L}=\bigl\{\tau:\tau\text{ a tree partition of }\cX\text{ built
  from the pre-specified cutpoints},\ \card\tau\le\bar L\bigr\},
\]
a fixed finite set: any $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$ is a partition of the finite
set $\cA$ into at most $\bar L$ blocks realisable by cutpoint splits, so
$\card{\cT_{\bar L}}<\infty$ and does not depend on $n$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}'s proof makes the count explicit). For a strictly
positive weight $\nu$ on $\cX$, let $\Pi^\nu_\tau$ denote the $\nu$-weighted
projection onto functions constant on the leaves of $\tau$; we write
$\Pi_\tau$ for $\Pi^{P}_\tau$ and use the control-weighted projection,
$d\nu=\{1-\ez\}dP$, for the outcome nuisance. Given $\tau$, the
\emph{full-sample leaf refit} sets, on each leaf $\ell$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refit}
  \ehat_\tau\restriction_\ell
  =\frac{\sum_{i:X_i\in\ell}A_i}{\card{\{i:X_i\in\ell\}}},
  \qquad
  \mhat_\tau\restriction_\ell
  =\frac{\sum_{i:X_i\in\ell,\,A_i=0}Y_i}
        {\card{\{i:X_i\in\ell,\,A_i=0\}}} .
\end{equation}

Structure is selected by penalised empirical risk minimisation over the
\emph{feasible} candidates. Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasible}
  \cT^{(j)}_n=\bigl\{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}:\text{every leaf of }\tau\text{ carries
   at least }m_n\text{ observations, control observations for }j=\mu\bigr\},
\end{equation}
and for a penalty $\lambda_n>0$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:select}
  \that_j\in\argmin_{\tau\in\cT^{(j)}_n}
  \bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)+\lambda_n\card\tau\bigr\},
  \qquad j\in\{e,\mu\},
\end{equation}
where $R^{(j)}_n$ is the empirical risk of the leaf-refit fit for nuisance $j$
and $R^{(j)}$ its population counterpart. Both risks are on the unnormalised
scale, so that $R^{(\mu)}$ is the control-weighted risk
$\E[\{1-\ez\}(\mu-\mz)^2]$ matching $\Lw\cdot^2$, not its control-conditional
average. Structure selection uses the \emph{unclipped} leaf refit; clipping is
applied only to the reported $\ehat$. We write $\ehat=\ehat_{\that_e}$,
$\mhat=\mhat_{\that_\mu}$, $\what=\ehat/(1-\ehat)$, and
$L_e=\card{\that_e}$, $L_\mu=\card{\that_\mu}$.

Restricting the argmin to $\cT^{(j)}_n$ is what reconciles selection with the
minimum-leaf-mass requirement of Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d): an
unconstrained argmin over $\cT_{\bar L}$ could return a partition violating it,
so the constraint belongs in the selection rule rather than being asserted of its
output. The restriction is asymptotically inert, since every leaf of a sufficient
partition has population mass bounded below by a fixed positive constant ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} discharges this explicitly, with the leaf-mass floor
made precise there --- while $m_n/n\to0$ follows from $\bar Lm_n\lesssim n$
with $\bar L$ fixed.

\begin{assumption}[Solver fidelity]\label{ass:solver}
The optimisation procedure computing~\eqref{eq:select}, and the uncapped
search of Section~\ref{sec:compute}, consider only tree partitions whose
splits lie at the pre-specified cutpoints of $\cX$: the search space is
$\cT_{\bar L}$ (respectively the uncapped analogue $\cT_\cA$ of
Section~\ref{sec:compute}), never a data-dependent or continuously-refined
set of thresholds. When $\cX$ is itself finite with every value a
pre-specified cutpoint, this holds automatically for any solver, since every
threshold on a finite value set already lies at a cutpoint; it is recorded as
a standing assumption here because it is not automatic once $\cX$ is a
general space and a generic branch-and-bound solver would otherwise consider
splits at observed data values, which are neither fixed nor finite in $n$.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Errors of the fitted nuisances}

Two distinct error notions appear, and they are not interchangeable. The
\emph{realised} errors are properties of the nuisances actually reported,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realised}
  \Dw=\Lw{\what-\wz},\qquad \Dm=\Lw{\mhat-\mz},
\end{equation}
whereas the \emph{approximation} errors are the best available within the
budget,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx}
  \delta_e=\min_{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}}\Ltwo{\Pi_\tau\ez-\ez},
  \qquad
  \delta_\mu=\min_{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}}\Lw{\Pi^{\nu}_\tau\mz-\mz}.
\end{equation}
The realised error is a random quantity attached to a particular fit; the
approximation error is a deterministic functional of $P$ and $\cT_{\bar L}$.
Results stated on one do not transfer to the other.

\subsection{Standing assumptions}

Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} is in force throughout the document and is never
restated as a hypothesis below: every search space appearing in any result,
capped ($\cT_{\bar L}$) or uncapped ($\cT_\cA$ of
Section~\ref{sec:compute}), is by definition built from the pre-specified
cutpoints it fixes, so no theorem needs to cite it separately. Of
Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:finite}, only \ref{ass:iid},
\ref{ass:causal}, \ref{ass:moments}, and the positive-atom-mass clause of
\ref{ass:finite} are restrictions on the sampling model $P$; the remaining
content of \ref{ass:finite} --- that the grid $\cA$ itself is finite and
fixed in $n$ --- is a choice made by the analyst before seeing the data, not
a property of $P$, and is more accurately grouped with \ref{ass:solver} as a
restriction on the estimator's search space. Assumptions
\ref{ass:construct}--\ref{ass:global} restrict the estimator, and
\ref{ass:sparsity}--\ref{ass:pseudo} the structure of the problem. Each
result below states which of \ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:pseudo} it uses, with
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} always additionally in force. Assumptions
\ref{ass:margin-floor} and \ref{ass:pseudo} are local to
Sections~\ref{sec:uniformity} and~\ref{sec:realised} respectively and are
never in force elsewhere.

\begin{assumption}[Sampling]\label{ass:iid}
$O_1,\dots,O_n$ are independent and identically distributed copies of
$O=(X,A,Y)\sim P$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Identification and one-sided positivity]\label{ass:causal}
Consistency, $Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)$; ignorability,
$\{Y(0),Y(1)\}\perp A\mid X$; $\pi>0$; and there exists $c\in(0,1)$ with
\[
  1-\ez(x)\ \ge\ c \qquad\text{for every }x\in\cX .
\]
\end{assumption}

\noindent Positivity is required on one side only. Identification of $\theta_0$
uses no uniform constant: the treated mean $\E[Y(1)\mid A=1]$ is available from
treated units alone, and the region $\{\ez=0\}$ receives weight zero in
$\theta_0=\pi^{-1}\E[\ez\,(m_1-\mz)]$. Inference uses the uniform bound
$1-\ez\ge c$ and nothing further: it is equivalent to boundedness of $\wz$, it
is what makes the second-order remainder of Section~\ref{sec:eif} finite, and it
supplies the density bound $d\nu/dP=1-\ez\ge c$, hence $d\nu\ge c\,dP$, that
the outcome
margin requires. No lower bound on $\ez$ is used anywhere, and $\ez(x)=0$ is
harmless.

\begin{assumption}[Moments and non-degeneracy]\label{ass:moments}
$\E[Y^2\mid A=a,X=x]\le C_Y<\infty$ for all $a\in\{0,1\}$ and $x\in\cX$, and
$V>0$, where $V$ is given in~\eqref{eq:vdecomp}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Finite candidate partition family]\label{ass:finite}
The grid atoms $\cA$ generated by the pre-specified cutpoints are finite in
number, fixed in $n$, and each of positive mass: $\card\cA<\infty$ and
$P(a)>0$ for every $a\in\cA$. ($\cX$ itself is not required to be finite;
see the ``Covariate space and tree partitions'' subsection above.)
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Estimator construction]\label{ass:construct}
The nuisances are fitted as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item \emph{Loss.} Both nuisances are fitted by squared error, so that excess
  risk equals squared $L_2$ distance to the truth.
  \item \emph{Leaf refit.} Leaf values are given by~\eqref{eq:refit}: the
  within-leaf treated fraction for $\ehat$, the within-leaf control mean for
  $\mhat$.
  \item \emph{Clipping.} Fitted propensities are clipped so that
  $1-\ehat(x)\ge c$ for every $x$, with $c$ the constant of
  Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}.
  \item \emph{Leaf mass.} Selection is restricted to the feasible set
  \eqref{eq:feasible}: candidates whose every leaf carries at least $m_n$
  observations, control observations in the case of $\mu$, with
  $\bar Lm_n\lesssim n$. This is a property of the selection rule, not a claim
  about its output. On the null event that a leaf of $\that_\mu$ contains no
  controls, $\mhat$ takes the parent leaf's control mean.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\noindent Three comments on Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}. Under (a) the
excess risk of a candidate $f$ for the propensity is
$\E[(A-f)^2\mid X]-\E[(A-\ez)^2\mid X]=(\ez-f)^2$ exactly, for any
$\ez\in[0,1]$, with no positivity; the loss-to-norm constant is $1$.

\emph{The propensity.} The within-leaf treated fraction minimises both squared
error and log-loss over constants, so (b) is invariant to that choice of loss
and the loss affects only the structure search~\eqref{eq:select}.

\emph{The outcome.} Suppose in addition that $Y\in\{0,1\}$ given $A=0$, so
that cross-entropy is a well-defined alternative to squared error for
$\mhat$; the same invariance holds, by a parallel argument we spell out
because, unlike the propensity case, it is not otherwise used in the proofs
below. Write $H_b(q):=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q)$ and, for $p\in[0,1]$,
$\mathrm{CE}(p,q):=-p\log q-(1-p)\log(1-q)$ for the cross-entropy of $p$
against $q$, both for $q\in[0,1]$ with the convention $0\log0:=0$. This
convention never produces $\log0$ against a disagreeing observation: below,
$q$ reaches an endpoint of $[0,1]$ only on a leaf where $\mz$ is
$\nu$-a.s.\ constant at that same endpoint, so $p=q$ there and
$\mathrm{CE}(p,q)=H_b(p)=0$. Direct expansion gives the exact decomposition
\[
  \mathrm{CE}(p,q)=H_b(p)+\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\Vert
  \mathrm{Bernoulli}(q)\bigr),
\]
with the Kullback--Leibler term non-negative and zero exactly at $p=q$
(Gibbs' inequality). Let
$R^{(\mu),CE}(f):=\E[\{1-\ez\}\,\mathrm{CE}(\mz,f(X))]$ be the
control-weighted cross-entropy risk of a candidate $f:\cX\to[0,1]$, on the
same unnormalised, control-weighted scale as $R^{(\mu)}$ above; a bridge
identical to the one below applied at the population level gives
$\E[(1-A)\,\mathrm{CE}(Y,f(X))]=R^{(\mu),CE}(f)$, so this is also the
population limit of the empirical risk introduced below. Because
$p\mapsto\mathrm{CE}(p,q)$ is affine in $p$ for fixed $q$, it commutes with
convex combinations: its $\nu$-average over a leaf $\ell$ equals
$\mathrm{CE}(\bar\mu_\ell,q)$ exactly, with
$\bar\mu_\ell:=\nu(\ell)^{-1}\sum_{z\in\ell}\nu_z\mz(z)=(\Pi^\nu_\tau\mz)(\ell)$
the $\nu$-weighted leaf mean and $\nu(\ell):=\sum_{z\in\ell}\nu_z$ --- and
with no correction for within-leaf heterogeneity of $\mz$, unlike squared
error, whose leaf average needs the second moment of $\mz$. Consequently, for
the leaf-constant refit $f_\tau\equiv\bar\mu_\ell$ on $\ell\in\tau$,
\[
  R^{(\mu),CE}(\tau)=\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\nu(\ell)\,
  \mathrm{CE}(\bar\mu_\ell,\bar\mu_\ell)
  =\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\nu(\ell)\,H_b(\bar\mu_\ell),
\]
and since $q\mapsto\mathrm{CE}(\bar\mu_\ell,q)$ is strictly convex with
unique minimiser $q=\bar\mu_\ell$ (equivalently, by the decomposition above,
$\mathrm{CE}(\bar\mu_\ell,q)=H_b(\bar\mu_\ell)+\mathrm{KL}(\cdot\Vert\cdot)\ge
H_b(\bar\mu_\ell)$ with equality iff $q=\bar\mu_\ell$), the leaf refit of (b)
is unchanged: the within-leaf control mean minimises both squared error and
cross-entropy over constants, as the within-leaf treated fraction does above.

Subtracting the same display at the finest, sufficient partition and applying
the decomposition above leaf by leaf gives, for every $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$,
\[
  R^{(\mu),CE}(\tau)=\int_\cX H_b(\mz)\,d\nu
  +\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\int_\ell
  \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\mz)\Vert
  \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\bar\mu_\ell)\bigr)\,d\nu,
\]
with $d\nu=\{1-\ez\}dP$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}. The first term is a
functional of $P$ alone, common to every $\tau$; the second is a sum over
leaves of non-negative integrals, each zero iff $\mz=\bar\mu_\ell$
$\nu$-a.s.\ on $\ell$. Since $d\nu/dP=1-\ez\ge c>0$ everywhere by
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, $\nu$ and $P$ are mutually absolutely
continuous, so the second term is zero iff $\mz$ is
$\nu$-a.s.\ constant on every leaf of $\tau$ --- exactly the condition
defining $\cS_\mu$ in Definition~\ref{def:sufficient}, which is loss-free, so
the minimum of $R^{(\mu),CE}$ over $\cT_{\bar L}$ is attained exactly on
$\cS_\mu$, and the excess-risk-zero set under cross-entropy coincides with the
one under squared error. Subtracting the minimum leaves the identity
\[
  R^{(\mu),CE}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'\in\cT_{\bar L}}R^{(\mu),CE}(\tau')
  =\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\int_\ell
  \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\mz)\Vert
  \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\bar\mu_\ell)\bigr)\,d\nu.
\]
Write $\Delta_\mu^{SE}$ for $\Delta_\mu$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} --- a
functional of $P$ and $\cT_{\bar L}$ alone, so its value does not depend on
which loss is used to fit, even though its proof is stated under
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(a) --- shown there strictly positive in the
"outcome, with positivity" step of its proof, and define $\Delta_\mu^{CE}$ by
the same display with $R^{(\mu),CE}$ in place of $R^{(\mu)}$. Pinsker's
inequality, $\mathrm{KL}(P\Vert Q)\ge2\,d_{TV}(P,Q)^2$ in nats with
$d_{TV}(P,Q):=\sup_A\abs{P(A)-Q(A)}=\tfrac12\|P-Q\|_1$, specialises for
$P=\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, $Q=\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q)$ --- where
$d_{TV}(P,Q)=\abs{p-q}$ exactly --- to
$\mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\Vert\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q))\ge2(p-q)^2$.
Applying this pointwise, $\nu$-a.s., inside the displayed identity and
integrating, then comparing with
$R^{(\mu)}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'}R^{(\mu)}(\tau')=\|\Pi^\nu_\tau\mz-\mz\|_\nu^2
=\int_\cX\{\bar\mu_{\ell(x)}-\mz\}^2\,d\nu$, the squared-error excess risk
identity used in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}, gives, for every
$\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$,
\[
  R^{(\mu),CE}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'}R^{(\mu),CE}(\tau')
  \ \ge\ 2\bigl\{R^{(\mu)}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'}R^{(\mu)}(\tau')\bigr\}.
\]
Taking the minimum of both sides over the common index set
$\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}\setminus\cS_\mu$ gives
$\Delta_\mu^{CE}\ge2\Delta_\mu^{SE}>0$, with the positivity of
$\Delta_\mu^{SE}$ taken from Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} and no new probabilistic
argument; positivity of $\Delta_\mu^{CE}$ alone would in fact follow more
directly, from the same finiteness argument as Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}'s own
"Finiteness" step applied to the KL identity above, with Pinsker giving the
quantitative factor of $2$ as a bonus, not a necessity (the constant is sharp
only in the limit $\bar\mu_\ell\to\tfrac12$, since
$\mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\Vert\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q))\sim(p-q)^2/\{2q(1-q)\}$
as $p\to q$ and $q(1-q)\le\tfrac14$).

The empirical risk of the leaf-constant refit, for Step~1 of the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}, is by the same affine-averaging argument applied to
the sample,
$R_n^{(\mu),CE}(\tau)=\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat q_\ell\,H_b(\bar Y_{\ell,0})$,
with no leading term free of $\tau$. This has the same shape as the
squared-error identity of that Step, $R_n^{(\mu)}(\tau)=\Pn[(1-A)Y^2]
-\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat q_\ell\bar Y_{\ell,0}^2$, but the absence of a
leading term here is not a consequence of affineness: affineness gives exact
leaf \emph{aggregation} under either loss (the squared-error leaf average also
aggregates exactly, into a leaf mean plus a within-leaf variance term). The
leading term $\Pn[(1-A)Y^2]$ is instead an artefact of the quadratic impurity,
absent for binary $Y$ specifically: since $Y\in\{0,1\}$ gives $Y^2=Y$,
$\Pn[(1-A)Y^2]=\Pn[(1-A)Y]=\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat q_\ell\bar Y_{\ell,0}$
for every $\tau$, so in this regime the squared-error risk collapses to the
equally leading-term-free Gini form
$R_n^{(\mu)}(\tau)=\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat q_\ell\,\bar Y_{\ell,0}
\{1-\bar Y_{\ell,0}\}$, the direct parallel of the entropy form above ($Y^2$
is a polynomial in $Y$ and separates; $H_b$ is not a polynomial in its
argument and does not need to). Either way, $R_n^{(\mu),CE}(\tau)$ is a fixed
continuous function of the $3\card\cA$ atom statistics
$\{\widehat P(a),\widehat S^0(a),\widehat S^Y(a):a\in\cA\}$ of that Step~1
($H_b$
is continuous on all of $[0,1]$ under the stated convention, so no
Lipschitz property is needed here), so Steps~2--4 there --- uniform
convergence over the fixed finite class $\cT_{\bar L}$, absorption of the
penalty, and the margin comparison --- go through verbatim with
$\Delta_\mu^{CE}$ in place of $\Delta_\mu$, giving
$\PP(\that_\mu\in\cS_\mu)\to1$ under cross-entropy exactly as
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} gives it under squared error. (This requires the
refit $\bar Y_{\ell,0}$ itself to be unclipped; a practical implementation
that clips predicted probabilities away from $\{0,1\}$ to avoid evaluating
$\log0$ against a disagreeing observation perturbs this identity by an amount
vanishing with the clip tolerance, since the identity above never evaluates
$\log0$ on the unclipped refit in the first place.) Nothing in this
document depends on this: Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(a) fixes squared
error, and every downstream result uses the squared-error selection
consistency proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}.

Clipping in (c) is applied on one side
because Assumption~\ref{ass:causal} places $\ez$ in $[0,1-c]$, on which
one-sided clipping is the Euclidean projection and therefore non-expansive:
$\abs{\mathrm{clip}(\ehat)-\ez}\le\abs{\ehat-\ez}$ pointwise, so every $L_2$
bound is preserved. Two-sided clipping forfeits this wherever $\ez<c$. The clip
level must not \emph{exceed} $c$: clipping at $c'\le c$ leaves
$\ez\in[0,1-c]\subseteq[0,1-c']$, so the projection argument goes through
verbatim, $\psi_0$ and $V$ are unchanged, and the only cost is a worse constant,
$c'^{-1}$ in place of $c^{-1}$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:convert}. Clipping at
$c'>c$, by contrast, changes the influence function on cells with $\ez>1-c'$, so
that $\thetahat$ remains consistent by double robustness while $V$ and every
coverage statement below fail. Since $c$ is an unknown functional of $P$ and is
nowhere estimated, this is what makes the procedure implementable: any
conservative choice $c'\le c$ suffices, and only overshooting is fatal.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the cross-entropy comparison]\label{rem:ce-scope}
Two qualifications on the preceding paragraph. First,
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} permits general $Y$ with a bounded second
moment; cross-entropy is defined only for the binary $Y$ assumed there, and
the estimator's \texttt{outcome\_type} argument already restricts
cross-entropy fitting to that case, so this is a documentation point, not a
gap in the proofs. Second, unlike $\ez$, which Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}
bounds away from $1$ by the constant $c$, no assumption bounds $\mz$ away
from $\{0,1\}$. Step~5 of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} obtains its
exponential rate by composing Hoeffding's inequality with a
\emph{Lipschitz} map of the $3M$ cell statistics, and $H_b$ is not
Lipschitz at $0$ or $1$ --- its derivative diverges there --- but it is
H\"older-$\alpha$ on $[0,1]$ for every $\alpha<1$, so composing Hoeffding
with a H\"older (rather than Lipschitz) modulus still gives an exponential
rate for $\that_\mu$ under cross-entropy, of the form
$\card{\cT_{\bar L}}\exp\{-c_1n(\Delta_\mu^{CE})^{2/\alpha}\}$ for any
$\alpha<1$, with no new assumption --- merely a degraded exponent relative
to Step~5's $\Delta_j^2$. Recovering the clean quadratic exponent would need
an assumption bounding $\mz$ away from $\{0,1\}$, symmetric to
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}; this refinement is not attempted here, and
either way is orthogonal to $\PP(\that_\mu\in\cS_\mu)\to1$, which by Step~6
of that proof rests on Steps~1--4 alone and does not use Step~5. Third,
$R^{(\mu)}$ and $R_n^{(\mu)}$ are unnormalised and control-weighted, fixed
following eq.~\eqref{eq:select} and reiterated in Step~1 of the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}; any log-loss implementation of the outcome
tree's risk must match that same convention, since a
control-conditional-average normalisation would rescale the effective
leaf-budget penalty $\lambda_n\card\tau$ relative to $R_n^{(\mu),CE}$
exactly as it would under squared error.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Fixed leaf budget]\label{ass:budget}
$\bar L$ is a constant, fixed in $n$, with $\bar L\le\card\cA$; consequently
$\cT_{\bar L}$ is a fixed finite set and $\card{\cT_{\bar L}}$ does not depend
on $n$.
\end{assumption}

\noindent Assumptions~\ref{ass:finite} and~\ref{ass:budget} together are what
make the analysis below elementary:
$\log\card{\cT_{\bar L}}\asymp\bar L\log(dJ)=O(1)$, so a union bound over the
candidate class costs a constant, no exponential tail inequality is needed at
any point, and Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} suffices with no sub-Gaussian
condition. The results of this document should not be motivated as escaping an
entropy cost, because under \ref{ass:finite}--\ref{ass:budget} no such cost
exists.

\begin{assumption}[Exact global optimisation]\label{ass:global}
For each $j$, $\that_j$ attains the global minimum in~\eqref{eq:select} over the
feasible set $\cT^{(j)}_n$ of~\eqref{eq:feasible}, and $\lambda_n\to0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Sufficient class]\label{def:sufficient}
For $j\in\{e,\mu\}$,
\[
  \cS_j=\bigl\{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}:
  \Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_{0,j}=\gamma_{0,j}\ \ P\text{-a.s.}\bigr\},
\]
equivalently the set of $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$ on whose leaves $\gamma_{0,j}$ is
$P$-a.s.\ constant. The characterisation is independent of the strictly positive
weight $\nu$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Structural sparsity]\label{ass:sparsity}
$\cS_e\ne\emptyset$ and $\cS_\mu\ne\emptyset$; equivalently
$\delta_e=\delta_\mu=0$.
\end{assumption}

\noindent Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} is a sparsity condition, not a
correct-specification condition. When $\cX$ itself is finite, so that every
cell of $\cX$ is a singleton grid atom, every function of $X$ is piecewise
constant, and the partition into singletons is tree-realisable using
$\card\cX-1$ splits; at $\bar L=\card\cX$ the assumption therefore holds
automatically and carries no content, and its content at $\bar L<\card\cX$
is only that a small tree suffices, that is, that the nuisances are
structurally sparse and hence displayable.

That escape hatch is special to a genuinely finite $\cX$, and does not
survive Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}'s relaxation to a general covariate
space. There, $e_0$ and $\mu_0$ are merely measurable functions on $\cX$;
they need not be constant within a grid atom, let alone within a leaf.
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} demands exact leaf-constancy on some
$\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$, and no choice of budget forces this: enlarging
$\bar L$ only permits finer unions of the \emph{fixed} atoms in $\cA$, so it
cannot make an otherwise-varying $e_0$ or $\mu_0$ exactly constant within a
single atom. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}, Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity}
is therefore always substantive, at every fixed budget --- not merely a
statement that a small tree suffices, but that some tree, small or not, is
exactly right. This is the central way in which Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}
relocates rather than removes the burden: fixing $\cA$ in advance buys back
the finite candidate class $\cT_{\bar L}$ that Sections~\ref{sec:main}
and~\ref{sec:crossfit} rely on, at the cost of requiring the truth to align with
that fixed grid exactly, not just approximately.

The cost of Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} is not symmetric in the type of
structure present. With $s$ active coordinates in a region, a tree needs at most
$\prod_{j\le s}J_j$ leaves, and far fewer when dependence is
\emph{hierarchical}, in the sense that the active coordinate set differs across
regions: as few as $s+1$ leaves. The assumption is expensive precisely for
\emph{additive} structure, where a leaf is required for each configuration of
the active coordinates, and cheap precisely for region-varying active sets. That
asymmetry, rather than flexibility, is the reason to use trees here; sparsity is
local, so $\gamma_{0,j}$ may depend on all $d$ coordinates globally while
remaining cheap to represent.

\begin{assumption}[Margin floor]\label{ass:margin-floor}
$\min_{j\in\{e,\mu\}}\Delta_j\ge\Delta_{\min}>0$, with $\Delta_j$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Pseudo-true margin]\label{ass:pseudo}
There is a unique pair $(\tau_e^\dagger,\tau_\mu^\dagger)\in\cT_{\bar L}^2$
minimising the population risks $R^{(e)}$ and $R^{(\mu)}$ over
$\cT_{\bar L}$, and
\[
  \Delta^\dagger_j:=\min_{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}\setminus\{\tau_j^\dagger\}}
  \bigl\{R^{(j)}(\tau)-R^{(j)}(\tau^\dagger_j)\bigr\}>0,
  \qquad j\in\{e,\mu\}.
\]
Write $\eta_*=(e_*,\mu_*)$ for the corresponding population leaf-refit
nuisances, $w_*=e_*/(1-e_*)$, and $V_*$ for the asymptotic variance of the
estimator at $\eta_*$.
\end{assumption}

\noindent Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo} is strictly weaker than
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity}: it requires a unique population-risk minimiser
within the budget and a gap to the nearest competitor, but not that the
minimiser represent the truth. Under \ref{ass:sparsity} it holds with
$\tau^\dagger_j$ the finest member of $\cS_j$ and $\eta_*=\eta_0$.

\section{The influence function and the exact remainder}\label{sec:eif}

\subsection{Score and estimator}

The efficient influence function for $\theta_0$ in the nonparametric model is
\citep{hahn1998}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
  \psi(O;\theta,\eta)
  =\frac1\pi\Bigl[A\bigl\{Y-\mu(X)-\theta\bigr\}
   -(1-A)\,w(X)\bigl\{Y-\mu(X)\bigr\}\Bigr],
  \qquad w=\frac e{1-e},
\end{equation}
and we write $\psi_0=\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_0)$. Solving
$\Pn\psi(\cdot;\theta,\widehat\eta)=0$ gives the estimator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:estimator}
  \thetahat=\frac1{n\pihat}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \Bigl[A_i\bigl\{Y_i-\mhat(X_i)\bigr\}
   -(1-A_i)\,\what(X_i)\bigl\{Y_i-\mhat(X_i)\bigr\}\Bigr].
\end{equation}
The control-arm term carries a minus sign, as solving $\Pn\psi=0$ requires. The
treated share is not substituted into~\eqref{eq:estimator} but emerges from the
coefficient of $\theta$ in~\eqref{eq:score}: with $\phi:=\pi\psi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-pi}
  \pihat\,(\thetahat-\theta_0)
  =\Pn\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\widehat\eta)
\end{equation}
holds exactly, with no expansion, so that no further correction for the
estimation of $\pi$ is required. The same algebra at the population level gives an
identity used repeatedly below: since the coefficient of $\theta$ in
\eqref{eq:score} is $-A/\pi$ and $\E A=\pi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pop-identity}
  \theta(\eta)-\theta_0=P\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta)
  \qquad\text{for every }\eta\text{ with }1-e\ge c,
\end{equation}
where $\theta(\eta)$ solves $\E[\psi(O;\theta,\eta)]=0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound}
therefore bounds $\theta(\eta)-\theta_0$ as well as $P\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta)$.

\subsection{The second-order remainder}

\begin{proposition}[Exact bilinear remainder]\label{prop:bilinear}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:causal}, for any $\eta=(e,\mu)$ with
$1-e\ge c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilinear}
  \E\bigl[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)\bigr]
  =\frac1\pi\,\E\Bigl[\bigl\{1-\ez(X)\bigr\}
   \bigl\{\wz(X)-w(X)\bigr\}\bigl\{\mz(X)-\mu(X)\bigr\}\Bigr].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:bilinear} is the classical doubly robust computation
\citep{robins1995,vanderlaan2003,kennedy2024}; it is recorded here because the
exact form of its weighting determines the constants in
Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound} and Corollary~\ref{cor:convert}, which differ, and
because Section~\ref{sec:honest} requires it at the fitted rather than the
population nuisances.

\begin{lemma}[Bias bound in the control-weighted norm]\label{lem:biasbound}
Write $P\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta)=\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)]$ for the population
expectation at a \emph{fixed} $\eta$, and evaluate it at $\eta=\widehat\eta$; both
sides below are then random through $\widehat\eta$ alone. Under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:causal} and
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c),
\[
  \bigl\lvert P\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta)\bigr\rvert_{\eta=\widehat\eta}
  \ \le\ \frac1\pi\,\Dw\,\Dm .
\]
\end{lemma}

\noindent The distinction matters: an unconditional expectation over
$\widehat\eta$ would yield $\E[\Dw\Dm]$ on the right and would not follow from
Cauchy--Schwarz. The quantity bounded here is the conditional bias given the
fitted nuisances, which is what Sections~\ref{sec:honest} treats as a random
interval width.

\noindent The constant is exactly $1/\pi$: Cauchy--Schwarz with respect to
$\{1-\ez\}\,dP$ places $\sqrt{1-\ez}$ on each factor, and no power of $c$
appears. Converting to the conventional unweighted propensity error is not free.

\begin{corollary}[Unweighted forms]\label{cor:convert}
Under the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound}, with the same convention,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convert-sym}
  \bigl\lvert P\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta)\bigr\rvert_{\eta=\widehat\eta}
  \ \le\ \frac1{\pi c}\,\Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}\,\Ltwo{\mhat-\mz},
\end{equation}
and consequently also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convert}
  \bigl\lvert P\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta)\bigr\rvert_{\eta=\widehat\eta}
  \ \le\ \frac1{\pi c^{3/2}}\,\Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}\,\Lw{\mhat-\mz}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\noindent Of the two, \eqref{eq:convert-sym} is the sharper: by
Lemma~\ref{lem:normequiv}, $\Lw{\mhat-\mz}\ge\sqrt c\,\Ltwo{\mhat-\mz}$, so the
right side of~\eqref{eq:convert} is never smaller than that
of~\eqref{eq:convert-sym}, and is strictly larger unless $1-\ez$ equals $c$
almost surely. The asymmetric form~\eqref{eq:convert} is therefore a matter of
convenience rather than necessity: what \emph{is} forced by the factor structure
is that the propensity error emerges unweighted, since the identity
$\{1-\ez\}\abs{\wz-w}=\abs{\ez-e}/(1-e)$ consumes the entire weight on that side;
retaining the outcome factor in the control-weighted norm is a choice, paid for
at $c^{-1/2}$, and it is made because Section~\ref{sec:honest} supplies bounds on
$\Dm$ rather than on $\Ltwo{\mhat-\mz}$. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal} the two norms are equivalent
with constants fixed in $n$, so no asymptotic rate statement distinguishes them;
the distinction has content only where the product enters an interval width
without vanishing, as in Section~\ref{sec:realised}.

\subsection{The efficiency bound}

\begin{lemma}[Local saturation and the variance bound]\label{lem:tangent}
Let $\cP_{c,C_Y}$ be the set of observed-data laws of $(X,A,Y)$ on
$\cX\times\{0,1\}\times\R$, where $\cX$ is an arbitrary measurable space
(finite or not), satisfying $\pi>0$, $1-\ez\ge c$ $P$-a.s.,
$\E[Y^2\mid A=a,X=x]\le C_Y$ for $P_X$-a.e.\ $x$ and both $a$, and $V>0$, on
which $\theta$ is defined as the identified functional
$\theta(P)=\pi^{-1}\E[\ez(m_1-\mz)]$. Let $P_0$ be \emph{interior} to
$\cP_{c,C_Y}$, in the sense that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior}
  \ez>0\quad P_0\text{-a.s.},\qquad
  \operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{P_0}\,(1-\ez)>c .
\end{equation}
When $\cX$ is finite with $p_x>0$ for every $x$, the essential infimum in
\eqref{eq:interior} is a minimum over finitely many cells, so
\eqref{eq:interior} reduces to the pointwise requirement $\ez(x)>0$ and
$1-\ez(x)>c$ for every $x$, matching earlier drafts of this document. Then
the tangent space
of $\cP_{c,C_Y}$ at $P_0$ is the \emph{entire} nonparametric tangent space
$\{h\in L^2(P_0):\E h=0\}$ --- the model is locally saturated at $P_0$ --- and
$\theta$ is pathwise differentiable at $P_0$ with canonical gradient $\psi_0$, so
that the semiparametric efficiency bound is $V=\E[\psi_0^2]$, given explicitly by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vdecomp}
  V=\pi^{-2}\,\E\Bigl[\ez\sigma_1^2
   +\frac{\ez^2\sigma_0^2}{1-\ez}
   +\ez\,(m_1-\mz-\theta_0)^2\Bigr].
\end{equation}
The tangent space is infinite-dimensional if and only if $P_0$ is not
concentrated on finitely many points of $\cX\times\{0,1\}\times\R$; this
happens, in particular, whenever $X$ has a nondegenerate continuous
component, and, when $\cX$ is finite, exactly when some conditional law
$P_{Y\mid A=a,X=x}$ has infinite support.
\end{lemma}

\noindent The model imposes no local restriction at an interior $P_0$: it is
cut out by the inequalities $\pi>0$, $1-\ez\ge c$, $\E[Y^2\mid A=a,X=x]\le C_Y$
and $V>0$, each of which holds with slack at $P_0$ under \eqref{eq:interior},
while the moment condition leaves the conditional laws of $Y$ given $(A,X)$
and the marginal law of $X$ otherwise unrestricted. Hence $V$ is a genuine
semiparametric bound rather than a parametric information bound, and this is
so whether or not $\cX$ is finite. It would be an error to argue instead
that the tangent space is infinite-dimensional. When $\cX$ \emph{is} finite,
with $M=\card\cX$, and $Y$ is binary --- a case the accompanying software
supports --- the whole space $L^2(P_0)$ has dimension at most $4M$, so every
tangent space is finite-dimensional, while $V=\E[\psi_0^2]$ remains the
bound. Dimension is a property of $P_0$; saturation is the property that
matters.

The interiority requirement is not cosmetic. At a law with $\ez(x_0)=0$ the
event $\{(A,X)=(1,x_0)\}$ is null (or, on a continuum, $\{A=1\}$ is null on
the set where $\ez=0$), so $m_1$ and $\sigma_1^2$ are undefined there and the
propensity admits perturbation in one direction only; the set of admissible
perturbations is then a cone rather than a subspace and the canonical-gradient
apparatus does not apply as stated. Such laws remain harmless for identification,
for estimation, and for the value of $V$, since the affected terms
of~\eqref{eq:vdecomp} carry weight $\ez(x_0)=0$; they are excluded here only for
the efficiency statement. Throughout, $m_1$ and $\sigma_1^2$ are defined
arbitrarily on $\{\ez=0\}$, where the products in~\eqref{eq:vdecomp} vanish.

\section{Structure of the tree class}\label{sec:structure}

Throughout this section $j\in\{e,\mu\}$ and $\cS_j$ is as in
Definition~\ref{def:sufficient}. The order of results is deliberate:
invariance of the estimand (Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}) precedes the margin
(Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}), which precedes the failure of the margin under greedy
splitting (Proposition~\ref{prop:greedy}), which precedes selection consistency
(Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}); the computational subsection that follows depends
on all four.

\subsection{Box complexity is not tree complexity}

Definition~\ref{def:sufficient} characterises sufficiency intrinsically, by the
requirement that the projection of the truth onto $\tau$ return the truth.
Equivalently, and this is worth recording because it is what the proofs use,
$\tau\in\cS_j$ if and only if every leaf of $\tau$ lies inside a level set of
$\gamma_{0,j}$, that is, if and only if $\tau$ refines the level-set partition of
$\gamma_{0,j}$ --- the unique coarsest partition on which $\gamma_{0,j}$ is
constant. The refinement reading of sufficiency is therefore correct.

What is \emph{not} correct is the corresponding reading of the sparsity
condition, namely that it suffices for $\bar L$ to be at least the cardinality of
a minimal partition into axis-aligned \emph{boxes} on which $\gamma_{0,j}$ is
constant. Minimal box complexity can be strictly smaller than minimal tree-leaf
complexity, because a minimal partition into boxes need not be realisable as a
tree.

\begin{proposition}[Minimal box partitions need not be tree-realisable]
\label{prop:pinwheel}
There exist a finite product space $\cX$ with all cells of positive
probability, a function $\gamma_0$ on $\cX$, and a budget $\bar L$ equal to the
cardinality of a minimal partition of $\cX$ into axis-aligned boxes on which
$\gamma_0$ is constant, such that no tree partition in $\cT_{\bar L}$ has
$\gamma_0$ constant on its leaves. That is, $\cS=\emptyset$ while
$\bar L$ equals the minimal box complexity of $\gamma_0$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent A five-box witness on $\cX=\{0,1,2\}^2$ requiring six leaves is
constructed in Appendix~\ref{app:counterex}. The consequence is that the
refinement formulation can render $\cS_j$ empty exactly when the budget appears
to be adequate, in which case Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} has probability zero and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has no valid route. Definition~\ref{def:sufficient}
avoids the distinction between boxes and trees, and also avoids a support
qualifier, since it is stated $P$-a.s.

\subsection{Invariance of the estimand}

\begin{lemma}[Invariance over the sufficient class]\label{lem:invariance}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:causal} and
\ref{ass:sparsity}, for every $(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$ the
population leaf-refit nuisances $\eta_\tau=(e_{\tau_e},\mu_{\tau_\mu})$ satisfy
$\theta(\eta_\tau)=\theta_0$, where $\theta(\eta)$ solves
$\E[\psi(O;\theta,\eta)]=0$.
\end{lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} is the load-bearing structural fact. The target of
inference does not move as the selected structure ranges over the sufficient
class; consequently $\that_j$ need never recover a particular partition, and no
\emph{rate} for structure selection is required. Membership $\that_j\in\cS_j$
with probability tending to one suffices. It also disposes of a distinction that
would otherwise require attention: $\mhat$ estimates the control-weighted
within-leaf mean of $\mz$ rather than the unweighted mean, but by
Definition~\ref{def:sufficient} the two coincide on the sufficient class.

The relation between Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} and the impossibility results
for post-selection inference \citep{leeb2005} is direct. Those results concern
inference on a parameter whose meaning changes with the selected model. Here it
does not: the estimand is constant on $\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$.

\subsection{The margin is derived}

\begin{lemma}[Positive population margin]\label{lem:margin}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}, Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:construct}(a),
and \ref{ass:budget}--\ref{ass:sparsity},
\[
  \Delta_j:=\min_{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}\setminus\cS_j}
  \Bigl\{R^{(j)}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'\in\cT_{\bar L}}R^{(j)}(\tau')\Bigr\}
  \ >\ 0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

The margin is a consequence of exact global optimisation over a finite class,
not a hypothesis: every $\tau\notin\cS_j$ has a leaf on which $\gamma_{0,j}$ is
non-constant with positive probability, hence strictly positive excess risk, and
a minimum of finitely many strictly positive numbers is strictly positive. The
two nuisances draw on different assumptions here. The propensity margin
$\Delta_e$ requires no positivity, since its excess risk is
$\Ltwo{\Pi_\tau\ez-\ez}^2$, strictly positive as soon as a leaf mixes
positive-probability regions with distinct $\ez$. The outcome margin $\Delta_\mu$
requires Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, because the outcome risk is
control-weighted and the relevant density bound is $d\nu/dP=1-\ez\ge c$.

\begin{proposition}[Greedy splitting has no first-split signal]\label{prop:greedy}
There exist a finite product space $\cX$ and a propensity $\ez$ on it, with all
cells of positive probability and $\cS_e\ne\emptyset$ at $\bar L=4$, such that the
reduction in population risk achieved by every admissible first split is exactly
zero, while $\Delta_e>0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}. Consequently a greedy
recursive procedure that splits only on strictly positive immediate risk
reduction halts at the root and never reaches $\cS_e$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Proposition~\ref{prop:greedy} is what gives Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}
content as a statement about the solver rather than merely about a finite class:
the margin is available to an exact optimiser and invisible to a procedure that
evaluates one split at a time. The witness, $\ez$ depending on $X$ only through
$\mathbf1\{x_1=x_2\}$, is worked in Appendix~\ref{app:counterex}.

The scope of the conclusion should be stated precisely, since the natural
stronger reading is false. On this witness the first split is a \emph{tie} rather
than a barrier, so a greedy procedure that breaks ties arbitrarily and grows to
the leaf budget does reach the singleton partition: splitting on $x_1$ at the root
for zero gain, then on $x_2$ in each child for strictly positive gain, recovers
$\ez$ exactly. What the witness establishes is therefore that greedy growth is not
\emph{guaranteed} to reach the sufficient class, and that the standard
positive-improvement stopping rule provably fails --- not that every greedy
variant fails. Assumption~\ref{ass:global} accordingly cannot be weakened to
greedy recursive splitting \emph{with a positive-gain stopping rule}; whether some
tie-breaking variant grown to the budget always suffices is not settled here.

\begin{lemma}[Selection lands in the sufficient class]\label{lem:selection}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}, Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:global},
and \ref{ass:sparsity}, for
each $j\in\{e,\mu\}$,
\[
  \PP\bigl(\that_j\in\cS_j\bigr)\longrightarrow1 .
\]
If in addition $Y$ is bounded, then
$\PP(\that_j\notin\cS_j)\le\card{\cT_{\bar L}}\,e^{-c_1n\Delta_j^2}$ for a
constant $c_1>0$ depending only on the outcome bound, on $c$, and on
$q_{\min}$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent Only the exponential rate uses a tail condition. The convergence
$\PP(\that_j\in\cS_j)\to1$ holds under Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} alone,
because for each fixed $\tau$ the empirical risk converges by the law of large
numbers and uniformity over a fixed finite candidate set is automatic.

The penalty $\lambda_n$ in~\eqref{eq:select} is asymptotically inert for
inference: by Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} every member of $\cS_j$ induces the
same estimand, so the penalty's role is to select the smallest sufficient tree,
that is, the most readily displayed one. Regularisation here serves
interpretability rather than statistical validity.
Proposition~\ref{prop:parsimony} makes this precise and shows that the budget
is then met without being enforced.

\subsection{Computing the selection rule}\label{sec:compute}

Equation~\eqref{eq:select} presumes a solver that searches $\cT^{(j)}_n$ itself.
The branch-and-bound solver used in practice searches
$\cG^{(j)}_n\supseteq\cT^{(j)}_n$, obtained by dropping the leaf-count bound
$\card\tau\le\bar L$ while retaining the minimum-leaf requirement, and returns
for a supplied $\lambda>0$ an exact global minimiser $t_j(\lambda)$ of
$R^{(j)}_n(\tau)+\lambda\card\tau$ over $\cG^{(j)}_n$; it has no native cap on
$\card\tau$. Proposition~\ref{prop:parsimony} shows that at $\lambda=\lambda_n$
the uncapped search already respects the budget with probability tending to
one; the two lemmas that follow certify the budget at the realised $n$ when it
does not.

\begin{proposition}[The budget binds asymptotically]\label{prop:parsimony}
Let
\[
  L^{\min}_j=\min_{\tau\in\cS_j}\card\tau,
  \qquad
  \cS^{\min}_j=\bigl\{\tau\in\cS_j:\card\tau=L^{\min}_j\bigr\},
\]
so that $L^{\min}_j\le\bar L$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity}. Let
$\mathcal{C}_n$ satisfy $\cT^{(j)}_n\subseteq\mathcal{C}_n\subseteq\cG^{(j)}_n$
and let $t$ minimise $R^{(j)}_n(\cdot)+\lambda_n\card{\cdot}$ over
$\mathcal{C}_n$. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:global} and
\ref{ass:sparsity}, and if in addition $n\lambda_n\to\infty$,
\[
  \PP\bigl(t\in\cS^{\min}_j\bigr)\longrightarrow1 .
\]
Two choices of $\mathcal{C}_n$ are of interest. With $\mathcal{C}_n=\cT^{(j)}_n$
the conclusion is that $\that_j$ of~\eqref{eq:select} is a sufficient tree of
minimal leaf count. With $\mathcal{C}_n=\cG^{(j)}_n$ it is that $t_j(\lambda_n)$,
the output of the uncapped solver at the analyst's own $\lambda_n$, satisfies
$\card{t_j(\lambda_n)}=L^{\min}_j\le\bar L$ and $t_j(\lambda_n)\in\cT^{(j)}_n$,
and hence solves~\eqref{eq:select}, with probability tending to one: the leaf
budget is met without being imposed. The minimiser need not be unique, and no
claim of convergence to a particular partition is made; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} the estimand does not distinguish the members of
$\cS^{\min}_j$, and only the leaf count is at issue for display.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\cT_\cA$ for the set of all partitions of the grid atoms $\cA$ into
leaves, with no cap on leaf count, so that $\cG^{(j)}_n\subseteq\cT_\cA$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} and $\cT_\cA$ is fixed and finite since $\cA$ is
finite by Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}, and put
$\cS^\cA_j=\{\tau\in\cT_\cA:\Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_{0,j}=\gamma_{0,j}\ P\text{-a.s.}\}$.
Since $\cS_j=\cS^\cA_j\cap\cT_{\bar L}$ and $\cS_j\ne\emptyset$, minimising
$\card\cdot$ over $\cS^\cA_j$ and over $\cS_j$ gives the same value $L^{\min}_j$
and the same minimising set $\cS^{\min}_j$. The proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} uses only $P(a)>0$ for every atom
(Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}), the lower bound $1-\ez\ge c$
(Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}), and finiteness of the candidate class, so it
applies verbatim with $\cT_{\bar L}$ replaced by $\cT_\cA$ and yields
\[
  \Delta_{j,\cA}:=\min_{\tau\in\cT_\cA\setminus\cS^\cA_j}
  \Bigl\{R^{(j)}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'\in\cT_\cA}R^{(j)}(\tau')\Bigr\}>0 ;
\]
necessarily $\Delta_{j,\cA}\le\Delta_j$, the minimum being over a larger class.

\emph{An exact decomposition.} Let $\tau_{\mathrm{sat}}$ be the partition of
$\cA$ into its individual atoms; it serves only as a reference point and is
not required to be a candidate in $\cT_{\bar L}$ or even in $\cG^{(j)}_n$. In
the notation of Step~1 of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection},
$R^{(j)}_n(\tau)=C_n-\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat\omega_\ell\bar Z_\ell^2$ with
$C_n$ free of $\tau$, $\widehat\omega_\ell=\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}\widehat\omega_a$
and
$\bar Z_\ell=\widehat\omega_\ell^{-1}\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}\widehat\omega_a\bar Z_a$,
this atom-level reduction holding for every $\tau\in\cT_\cA$ regardless of
leaf count, since Step~1 places no cap on $\card\tau$. Expanding the square
gives, for every $\tau\in\cT_\cA$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anova}
  R^{(j)}_n(\tau)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau_{\mathrm{sat}})
  =\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}
   \widehat\omega_a\bigl(\bar Z_a-\bar Z_\ell\bigr)^2\ \ge\ 0 ,
\end{equation}
on the event that every atom mean is defined, of probability tending to one
since every atom has positive mass by Assumption~\ref{ass:finite} and atom
occupancy counts are $\asymp n$ by the same binomial argument as the
occupancy step of Lemma~\ref{lem:findim}'s proof. If $\tau\in\cS^\cA_j$ then
$\gamma_{0,j}$ is constant on each leaf, so every $\bar Z_a$ with
$a\subseteq\ell$ and the convex combination $\bar Z_\ell$ estimate one common
value; each is within $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ of it by Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}
and the atom-mass lower bound just cited, while $\widehat\omega_a\le1$ and
there are at most $\card\cA$ summands. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anova-rate}
  \max_{\tau\in\cS^\cA_j}
  \bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau_{\mathrm{sat}})\bigr\}=O_p(n^{-1}).
\end{equation}

\emph{Availability.} Fix $\sigma\in\cS^{\min}_j$. The first paragraph of Step~4
of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} gives
$\PP(\sigma\in\cT^{(j)}_n)\to1$, and $\cT^{(j)}_n\subseteq\mathcal{C}_n$.

\emph{Competitors with more leaves.} Let $\tau\in\mathcal{C}_n$ with
$\card\tau>L^{\min}_j$, so that $\card\tau-L^{\min}_j\ge1$. By~\eqref{eq:anova}
applied to $\tau$ and~\eqref{eq:anova-rate} applied to $\sigma$,
\[
  \bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\sigma)+\lambda_nL^{\min}_j\bigr\}
  -\bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)+\lambda_n\card\tau\bigr\}
  \ \le\ \bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\sigma)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau_{\mathrm{sat}})\bigr\}-\lambda_n
  \ =\ O_p(n^{-1})-\lambda_n ,
\]
strictly negative with probability tending to one because $n\lambda_n\to\infty$.
The bound is free of $\tau$, so the conclusion is uniform over such $\tau$ with
no union bound: no candidate with more than $L^{\min}_j$ leaves is a minimiser.

\emph{Insufficient competitors with no more leaves.} Let
$\tau\in\cT_\cA\setminus\cS^\cA_j$ with $\card\tau\le L^{\min}_j$. The
atom-level reduction of Step~1 of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}
applies to every $\tau\in\cT_\cA$ without a leaf-count cap, and Steps~2 and~3
of that proof, whose only use of Assumption~\ref{ass:budget} is that the
candidate class be fixed and finite --- which $\cT_\cA$ is, $\cA$ itself
being finite --- give both
$\max_{\tau,\tau'\in\cT_\cA}\lvert\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau')\}
-\{R^{(j)}(\tau)-R^{(j)}(\tau')\}\rvert=o_p(1)$ and
$\lambda_n\card\cdot\le\lambda_n\card\cA=o(1)$. Since $\sigma\in\cS^\cA_j$
attains $\min_{\cT_\cA}R^{(j)}$ while $R^{(j)}(\tau)$ exceeds it by at least
$\Delta_{j,\cA}$,
\[
  \bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)+\lambda_n\card\tau\bigr\}
  -\bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\sigma)+\lambda_nL^{\min}_j\bigr\}
  \ \ge\ \Delta_{j,\cA}-o_p(1)\ >\ 0
\]
with probability tending to one, so no such $\tau$ is a minimiser.

\emph{Conclusion.} On the intersection of these events a minimiser $t$ over
$\mathcal{C}_n$ satisfies $\card t\le L^{\min}_j$ and $t\in\cS^\cA_j$, hence
$\card t=L^{\min}_j$ and $t\in\cS^{\min}_j$. For the second stated consequence,
$t\in\cG^{(j)}_n$ with $\card t\le\bar L$ gives $t\in\cT^{(j)}_n$, and a
minimiser over $\mathcal{C}_n\supseteq\cT^{(j)}_n$ lying in $\cT^{(j)}_n$
minimises over $\cT^{(j)}_n$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Proposition~\ref{prop:parsimony} is what reconciles the penalty of
\eqref{eq:select} with the budget of Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}: there is one
penalty, not two. The condition $n\lambda_n\to\infty$ is compatible with
$\lambda_n\to0$ of Assumption~\ref{ass:global} --- $\lambda_n\asymp n^{-1/2}$
and $\lambda_n\asymp n^{-1}\log n$ both qualify --- and is what separates the
$O_p(n^{-1})$ empirical fluctuation among sufficient trees from the $\lambda_n$
cost of one extra leaf. It is not imposed as a standing assumption because no
other result uses it. What the proposition does not supply is a check at the
realised $n$: the event it describes has probability tending to one but is not
verifiable from data, and it is vacuous when $\cS_j=\emptyset$, in which case
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} fails as well. The two facts below are the
finite-sample instruments for that complementary event.

\begin{lemma}[Monotone leaf count]\label{lem:lambda-mono}
Let $0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2$ and let $t_i$ be a global minimiser of
$R^{(j)}_n(\cdot)+\lambda_i\card{\cdot}$ over $\cG^{(j)}_n$. Then
$\card{t_2}\le\card{t_1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Optimality of $t_1$ at $\lambda_1$ and of $t_2$ at $\lambda_2$ gives
$R^{(j)}_n(t_1)+\lambda_1\card{t_1}\le R^{(j)}_n(t_2)+\lambda_1\card{t_2}$ and
$R^{(j)}_n(t_2)+\lambda_2\card{t_2}\le R^{(j)}_n(t_1)+\lambda_2\card{t_1}$.
Adding cancels both risks and leaves
$(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)(\card{t_2}-\card{t_1})\le0$; divide by
$\lambda_2-\lambda_1>0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda-mono} is what makes a bisection on $\lambda$
against the realised leaf count well posed: the count is a weakly decreasing
integer-valued correspondence, so a search for the smallest $\lambda$ attaining
$\card{t_j(\lambda)}\le\bar L$ terminates.

\begin{lemma}[Certificate for the computed selector]\label{lem:lambda-exact}
Let $\lambda\ge\lambda_n$, let $t=t_j(\lambda)$, and suppose $t$ is feasible,
i.e.\ every leaf of $t$ carries at least $m_n$ observations, control
observations for $j=\mu$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] if $\card t=\bar L$, then $t$ solves~\eqref{eq:select}, and moreover
  $t\in\argmin_{\tau\in\cT^{(j)}_n}R^{(j)}_n(\tau)$, so the returned tree does
  not depend on $\lambda$ beyond the leaf count it induces;
\item[(ii)] if $\card t\le\bar L$ and $\lambda=\lambda_n$, then $t$
  solves~\eqref{eq:select};
\item[(iii)] if $\card t=L<\bar L$, then for every $\tau\in\cT^{(j)}_n$
  \[
    R^{(j)}_n(t)+\lambda_nL
    \ \le\ R^{(j)}_n(\tau)+\lambda_n\card\tau
      +(\lambda-\lambda_n)(\bar L-L),
  \]
  so $t$ is optimal in~\eqref{eq:select} up to the computable slack
  $(\lambda-\lambda_n)(\bar L-L)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Feasibility of $t$ together with $\card t\le\bar L$ gives $t\in\cT^{(j)}_n$, and
$\cT^{(j)}_n\subseteq\cG^{(j)}_n$, so $t$ minimises
$R^{(j)}_n+\lambda\card{\cdot}$ over $\cT^{(j)}_n$; this is (ii) when
$\lambda=\lambda_n$. For (i) and (iii), that optimality reads
$R^{(j)}_n(t)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau)\le\lambda(\card\tau-\card t)$ for
$\tau\in\cT^{(j)}_n$. In case (i), $\card\tau-\bar L\le0$ and
$\lambda\ge\lambda_n\ge0$ give
$\lambda(\card\tau-\bar L)\le\lambda_n(\card\tau-\bar L)\le0$; the first
inequality is~\eqref{eq:select} and the second is unpenalised optimality. In
case (iii), write
$\lambda(\card\tau-L)=\lambda_n(\card\tau-L)+(\lambda-\lambda_n)(\card\tau-L)$
and bound $\card\tau-L\le\bar L-L$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Cases (i) and (ii) are the operative ones, and on either of them the
global-optimality clause of Assumption~\ref{ass:global} is \emph{verified rather
than assumed}: the returned tree is an exact global minimiser
in~\eqref{eq:select}, certified by a finite-sample inequality, at the same
$\lambda_n$ that appears in the assumption. The clause $\lambda_n\to0$ concerns
the pre-specified $\lambda_n$ and is untouched; the certifying $\lambda$ is not
that sequence and need not vanish in $n$. Case (iii) arises when the integer
leaf count jumps past $\bar L$, and it should be reported together with its
slack rather than presented as a solution of~\eqref{eq:select}; note also that a
realised $L<\bar L$ narrows the candidate class, so sufficiency
(Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity}) must be read at $L$ rather than at $\bar L$
before Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} is invoked; under
Proposition~\ref{prop:parsimony} this does not arise at $\lambda=\lambda_n$.

\begin{remark}[Why $\lambda$ is solved for and not chosen]\label{rem:lambda-closed-form}
The budget can also be enforced in closed form: no tree reduces the empirical
risk below zero, so $\lambda\ge R^{(j)}_n(\tau_{\mathrm{root}})/(\bar L-1)$
makes every tree with more than $\bar L$ leaves suboptimal. The bound is
correct, and the estimator it defines is not the one analysed here. Provided the
root is not itself sufficient, such a $\lambda$ satisfies
$\lambda\bar L>R^{(j)}_n(\tau_{\mathrm{root}})
=R^{(j)}(\tau_{\mathrm{root}})+o_p(1)\ge\Delta_j+o_p(1)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}, whereas Step~3 of the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} requires $\lambda_n\bar L<\delta<\Delta_j/2$. A
penalty large enough to \emph{enforce} the budget is therefore never small
enough to be inferentially inert, and the resulting estimator targets a
$\lambda$-dependent limit. Lemma~\ref{lem:lambda-exact} avoids the dilemma by
certifying the budget at whatever $\lambda$ attains it while leaving the
objective in~\eqref{eq:select} unchanged.
\end{remark}

\section{Full-sample inference with two optimal trees}\label{sec:main}

Both partitions and both sets of leaf values are computed from all $n$
observations. There is no sample splitting, no cross-fitting, no intersection of
candidate sets, and no averaging over fits.

\subsection{An exact decomposition}

Let $\varepsilon_i=Y_i-\mz(X_i)$, $\Delta\mu=\mhat-\mz$, $\Delta w=\what-\wz$,
and $\phi(O;\eta)=A\{Y-\mu(X)-\theta_0\}-(1-A)w(X)\{Y-\mu(X)\}$, so that
$\phi(\cdot;\eta_0)=\pi\psi_0$.

\begin{lemma}[Skeleton identity]\label{lem:skeleton}
On the event $\{\pihat>0\}$, and with no expansion or approximation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:skeleton}
  \pihat\,(\thetahat-\theta_0)=\Pn\phi(\cdot;\eta_0)+U-T,
\end{equation}
where
\[
  U:=\Pn\bigl[h(O)\,\Delta\mu(X)\bigr],\quad
  h(O):=\wz(X)(1-A)-A,
  \qquad
  T:=\Pn\bigl[(1-A)\,\Delta w(X)\{Y-\mhat(X)\}\bigr],
\]
and $\E[h\mid X]=\wz(1-\ez)-\ez=0$ pointwise.
\end{lemma}

The two remainder terms have different characters and are controlled by
different arguments. Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} removes the first-order
approximation error; what survives in $U$ and $T$ is driven by
\emph{estimation} error of the leaf values, and is controlled by
Assumptions~\ref{ass:construct}--\ref{ass:budget}. Of the two, $U$ is the
binding constraint.

\begin{lemma}[Leaf-value term]\label{lem:U}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:budget}, for every fixed
$\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$, $\abs U=O_p(\bar L/n)=o_p(n^{-1/2})$; the same bound holds
for the maximum over $\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$. The bound does not involve
$\tau_e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Cross term]\label{lem:cross}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:budget} and \ref{ass:sparsity},
\[
  \max_{(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu}
  \bigl\lvert T(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\bigr\rvert
  =O_p\bigl(\sqrt{\bar L}/n\bigr)=o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\]
\end{lemma}

\noindent Three features of Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} deserve emphasis, because they
determine the shape of its proof. First, the index set
$\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$ is tight: sufficiency of $\tau_\mu$ makes the substitution
$Y-\mhat\to\varepsilon$ exact, and sufficiency of $\tau_e$ is what permits
$\ez(X_i)$ to be collapsed to a single leaf value before the binomial variance
bound. Were $\tau_e\notin\cS_e$, then $\Delta w$ would converge to a non-zero
limit, centring within the leaves of $\tau_\mu$ would leave
$\sum_ia_i^2=\Theta_p(n)$, and $\sqrt nT$ would be $O_p(1)$ rather than
$o_p(1)$. Second, no minimum-leaf-mass condition is used for this bound, although
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d) remains in force: each
leaf contributes at most a constant to the relevant sum irrespective of its
size, because the ratio of control count to total count in a leaf is at most one
deterministically. Third, clipping preserves the bound, being a projection onto
an interval containing the leaf value of the truth.

The cross term is not of product form. It pairs the propensity error with
outcome \emph{noise}, so the argument must exploit conditional mean-zeroness and
cannot exploit smallness. Cauchy--Schwarz is not merely lossy here but fatal:
its second factor converges to the control-arm residual standard deviation
$\E[\{1-\ez\}\sigma_0^2]^{1/2}$, which is bounded away from zero, leaving a
bound of order $\sqrt{L_e/n}\ge n^{-1/2}$ for every $L_e\ge1$. No leaf budget
rescues that, since the failure occurs at the scale of the central limit
theorem.

\subsection{Uniform asymptotic linearity}

For $(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cT_{\bar L}^2$ let $\thetahat(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)$ denote
the estimator~\eqref{eq:estimator} formed from the leaf refits on those
partitions.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform asymptotic linearity on the sufficient class]
\label{lem:uniform}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:budget} and \ref{ass:sparsity},
\[
  \max_{(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu}
  \Bigl\lvert\sqrt n\bigl\{\thetahat(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)-\theta_0\bigr\}
   -\sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0\Bigr\rvert=o_p(1).
\]
\end{lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} is what licenses the passage from fixed partitions to
the selected ones, and the form of the statement matters. Three routes that
appear adequate are not. Invariance of the \emph{estimand}
(Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}) is invariance of first-order bias, whereas a
central limit theorem requires invariance of the \emph{influence function}. A
finite mixture $\sum_\tau\mathbf1\{\that=\tau\}\sqrt n\{\thetahat(\tau)-\theta_0\}$
is not asymptotically normal merely because each component is, since the
selection indicators are correlated with the components; finiteness of the
candidate set is not by itself sufficient, and what is needed is asymptotic
equivalence to a \emph{fixed} statistic. Conditioning on $\{\that_\mu=\tau\}$ is
not a repair, because leaf membership is determined by $X$ while the merging of
cells is determined by $Y$, so $\mhat$ remains conditionally biased given the
selection event.

Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} conditions on nothing. It bounds a maximum over a family
that does not depend on the data, and a maximum of finitely many $o_p(n^{-1/2})$
terms is $o_p(n^{-1/2})$. It requires Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c) but
neither Assumption~\ref{ass:global} nor Assumption~\ref{ass:margin-floor}.

It is worth recording alongside that neither tree uses treated outcomes:
$\that_e$ and $\ehat$ are $\sigma(X_{1:n},A_{1:n})$-measurable, and $\that_\mu$
and $\mhat$ are $\sigma(X_{1:n},A_{1:n},\{Y_i:A_i=0\})$-measurable. The only
double use of the outcome is of the control arm, and the double use of the
treatment indicator is harmless because the propensity error enters
\eqref{eq:skeleton} only multiplied by a residual whose within-leaf control sum
is exactly zero.

\subsection{The main result}

\begin{theorem}[Full-sample two-tree central limit theorem]\label{thm:main}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}, Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:global},
and \ref{ass:sparsity}, with
$\that_e,\that_\mu$ selected by~\eqref{eq:select} on all $n$ observations and
leaf values refitted on all $n$ observations,
\[
  \sqrt n\bigl(\thetahat-\theta_0\bigr)\rightsquigarrow N(0,V),
\]
with $V$ as in~\eqref{eq:vdecomp}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Variance estimation and confidence intervals]
\label{cor:variance}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vhat}
  \Vhat=\frac1{n\pihat^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \Bigl[A_i\bigl\{Y_i-\mhat(X_i)-\thetahat\bigr\}
   -(1-A_i)\what(X_i)\bigl\{Y_i-\mhat(X_i)\bigr\}\Bigr]^2 .
\end{equation}
Then $\Vhat\to_pV$, and $\thetahat\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\Vhat/n}$ has
asymptotic coverage $1-\alpha$ for $\theta_0$, pointwise in $P$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent The route to Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} is a union bound over the
finite sufficient class, and it requires three inputs:
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, so that every $\tau\in\cS_j$ has the same limit and
$\plim\Vhat$ is not a selection-weighted mixture of distinct numbers;
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}, to discard the complementary event; and
Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} below, for consistency at fixed $\tau$. Notably it
requires only $\Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}=o_p(1)$, and neither the rate
condition of Section~\ref{sec:crossfit} nor Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional finite-dimensionality]\label{lem:findim}
Fix any measurable partition $\tau$ with at most $\bar L$ leaves --- in
particular any $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$, and also any continuum partition in
$\cTcont$ --- all of whose leaves have positive probability under
$P$, and, for $j=\mu$, positive control mass. Then the leaf refit for nuisance
$j$ is determined by at most $\card\tau$ real numbers, and
\[
  \max_{\ell\in\tau}
  \bigl\lvert\widehat\gamma_{j}\restriction_\ell
   -(\Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_{0,j})\restriction_\ell\bigr\rvert=O_p(n^{-1/2}),
\]
with $\nu=P$ for $j=e$ and $d\nu=\{1-\ez\}\,dP$ for $j=\mu$ --- no
membership of $\tau$ in $\cS_j$ is assumed. If in addition $\tau\in\cS_j$,
the projection $\Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_{0,j}$ equals $\gamma_{0,j}$ exactly, and
the display recovers
$\max_\ell\lvert\widehat\gamma_j\restriction_\ell-\gamma_{0,j}\restriction_\ell\rvert=O_p(n^{-1/2})$.
\end{lemma}

Two finite-sample properties of~\eqref{eq:vhat} are worth stating. Its summands
have exactly zero sample mean by construction, so it is simultaneously the
uncentred second moment and the centred sample variance, and no centring
ambiguity arises. Because structure and leaf values are both fitted on all $n$
observations, the summands are in-sample residuals, and~\eqref{eq:vhat} is
downward biased by a relative factor of order $(2\bar L+1)/n$; a
degrees-of-freedom divisor $n-(\card{\that_e}+\card{\that_\mu}+1)$ is a
reasonable finite-sample convention. All statements are on $\{\pihat>0\}$, whose
probability tends to one.

\subsection{Uniformity in the underlying law}\label{sec:uniformity}

Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} gives
$\PP(\that_j\notin\cS_j)\le\card{\cT_{\bar L}}e^{-c_1n\Delta_j^2}$, which is
$o(1)$ for each fixed $P$ but not uniformly over
$\{P:\delta_e=\delta_\mu=0\}$, since $\Delta_j$ may be arbitrarily small in that
set. Coverage in Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} is therefore asserted pointwise.
Uniformity is recovered on a class that fixes the margin together with the
moment, positivity, and non-degeneracy constants.

\begin{definition}[Uniformity class]\label{def:uniformity}
For $\Delta_{\min},c,v_0>0$ and $C_4<\infty$, let
\[
  \cP(\Delta_{\min},c,C_4,v_0,p_0,\pi_0)
  =\Bigl\{P:\ \delta_e=\delta_\mu=0,\ \min_j\Delta_j\ge\Delta_{\min},\
  1-\ez\ge c,\ \abs Y\le C_4^{1/4},\ V\ge v_0,\ \min_{a\in\cA}P(a)\ge p_0,\
  \pi\ge\pi_0\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform coverage]\label{thm:uniform-cover}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:global} hold, and suppose in addition
that the linearisation remainder is uniformly negligible on the class, that is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unif-rem}
  \sup_{P\in\cP(\Delta_{\min},c,C_4,v_0,p_0,\pi_0)}
  \PP_P\bigl(\abs{R_n}>\epsilon_n\bigr)\longrightarrow0
  \quad\text{for some }\epsilon_n\to0,\qquad
  R_n=\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)-\sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0 .
\end{equation}
Then $\thetahat\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\Vhat/n}$ has coverage converging to
$1-\alpha$ uniformly over $\cP(\Delta_{\min},c,C_4,v_0,p_0,\pi_0)$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent Every constant in the class is consumed. The margin floor
$\Delta_{\min}$ uniformises selection; $c$ bounds the weights; the outcome bound
controls the third and fourth moments of $\psi_0$; $v_0$ keeps the standardisation
non-degenerate; $p_0$ floors the leaf denominators; and $\pi_0$ is required
because $\psi_0$ carries a factor $\pi^{-1}$, so that without it neither
$\E[\psi_0^4]$ nor $\theta_0$ is bounded on the class --- and $V\ge v_0$ does not
help, since $V\asymp\pi^{-2}$ grows as $\pi$ shrinks. The outcome is required to be
bounded rather than merely to have four moments, because the only exponential
selection bound available is the one Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} supplies under
boundedness.

Condition~\eqref{eq:unif-rem} is stated as a hypothesis rather than derived.
Establishing it requires uniform-in-$P$ versions of Lemmas~\ref{lem:U},
\ref{lem:cross} and~\ref{lem:uniform}, whose constants involve $\bar Lm_n$, $p_0$,
$\pi_0$ and the outcome bound; that is not carried out here, and until it is, the
uniform statement is conditional. The pointwise results of
Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} need none of this.

\subsection{Regularity and efficiency}\label{sec:regularity}

\begin{theorem}[Regularity and local asymptotic efficiency]\label{thm:regular}
Suppose the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} hold and, in addition, that
$P_0$ satisfies the interiority condition~\eqref{eq:interior} of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tangent}. Then $\thetahat$ is regular at $P_0$
relative to $\cP_{c,C_Y}$: for every path $\{P_{n,h}\}$ in $\cP_{c,C_Y}$ that is differentiable
in quadratic mean at $P_0$ with score $h$,
\[
  \sqrt n\bigl\{\thetahat-\theta(P_{n,h})\bigr\}\rightsquigarrow N(0,V)
  \qquad\text{under }P_{n,h}^n,
\]
with the limit free of $h$. Consequently, by the convolution theorem,
$\thetahat$ is locally asymptotically efficient in $\cP_{c,C_Y}$.
\end{theorem}

The efficiency claim is asserted in $\cP$ and must not be asserted in the
sparsity-restricted submodel, where the bound is strictly smaller.

\begin{proposition}[The bound in the sparse submodel]\label{prop:sparse-bound}
For $\tau\in\cS_e$, let $\cP_\tau\subset\cP$ impose that $\ez$ be constant on
the leaves of $\tau$. The efficiency bound for $\theta_0$ in $\cP_\tau$ is
\[
  V_\tau=V-\pi^{-2}\,\E\Bigl[\ez(1-\ez)\bigl\{\Delta m
   -\Pi^{v}_\tau\Delta m\bigr\}^2\Bigr]\ \le\ V,
  \qquad \Delta m=m_1-\mz-\theta_0,\quad dv=\ez(1-\ez)\,dP,
\]
with strict inequality whenever, on the region where $\ez>0$, the
treated--control contrast varies within a
leaf of $\tau$. Moreover $V^{\mathrm{known}\text{-}e}\le V_\tau\le V$, where the
first is the bound when $\ez$ is known.
\end{proposition}

\noindent For \emph{known} $\tau$ the bound $V_\tau$ is attainable by a regular
estimator; this is the classical asymmetry that for the ATT, knowledge of the
propensity lowers the bound \citep{hahn1998}. The obstruction is that $\tau$ is
unknown. The relevant quantity across the union of sparse submodels is
$V_{\mathrm{sparse}}=\max_{\tau\in\cS_e}V_\tau$, governed by the finest
sufficient partition within the budget, and hence by $\bar L$ as well as by $P$;
it depends discontinuously on $P$, because $\cS_e$ can empty out as $P$ leaves
the sparse class. Accordingly no estimator attains $V_{\tau(P)}$ at every
$P\in\bigcup_\tau\cP_\tau$ while remaining regular. The estimator studied here
is regular, is uniformly valid over the class of
Definition~\ref{def:uniformity}, and attains $V$.

\section{A two-stage estimator with off-grid threshold refinement}\label{sec:twostage}


The two-stage algorithm sketched above -- Stage~A, a coarse-resolution
grid-restricted fit at an inflated leaf/depth budget, followed by a collapse
map and Stage~B's exact one-threshold scans -- and its topology-recovery and
threshold-superconsistency guarantees are developed and proved in full in a
companion paper on continuum-threshold recovery for optimal decision trees
(the results are non-causal: they concern recovery of a single regression
function's partition, not the ATT). What follows restates, without proof,
exactly the results this section's central limit theorem uses as hypotheses,
applied separately to each nuisance $j\in\{e,\mu\}$; proofs are cited to the
companion paper throughout.

\begin{assumption}[Continuum tree sparsity and jump regularity]
\label{ass:sparsity-jump}
For each nuisance \(\gamma_{0,j}\), \(j\in\{e,\mu\}\), the following conditions
hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*),leftmargin=*]
  \item[(S)] \emph{Tree realisability.}
  The function \(\gamma_{0,j}\) is piecewise constant on a continuum
  partition realised by an axis-aligned tree with at most \(\bar L\) leaves
  and depth at most \(d\), where \(\bar L\) and \(d\) are fixed in \(n\).

  \item[(J)] \emph{Genuine jumps.}
  Every true split separates regions on which the relevant population values
  differ by an absolute amount between fixed constants
  \(0<\kappa_{\min}\le\kappa_{\max}<\infty\).

  \item[(Dens)] \emph{Local connected density.}
  Let \(R_N\) be the closed axis-aligned box associated with a true internal
  node \(N\), and let
  \[
    R_N^+
    :=
    \bigl\{x\in\cX:
      \operatorname{dist}_\infty(x,R_N)\le\varsigma/2
    \bigr\}.
  \]
  On \(R_N^+\), the covariate law under the measure \(\nu\) admits a density
  \(f_{\nu,N}\), with respect to Lebesgue measure or the appropriate product
  base measure, satisfying
  \[
    0<f_{\min}
    \le f_{\nu,N}(x)
    \le f_{\max}<\infty
    \qquad\text{for every }x\in R_N^+,
  \]
  where \(f_{\min}\) and \(f_{\max}\) are fixed uniformly over true nodes.
  The sets \(R_N^+\) are fixed compact rectangles whose coordinate diameters
  are bounded uniformly over true nodes by a fixed constant
  \(D_{\max}<\infty\).

  \item[(Sep)] \emph{Separation and interiority.}
  Distinct true boundaries on the same coordinate are separated by at least
  \(\varsigma>0\), and every true boundary is at least \(\varsigma\) from the
  relevant support boundary.

  \item[(Node)] \emph{Positive node mass.}
  Every true node and leaf has \(\nu\)-mass at least \(q_{\min}>0\), fixed in
  \(n\).

  \item[(Local-Jump)] \emph{Jumps across every active face cell.}
  Fix an internal node \(N\), splitting coordinate \(q\) at \(c_{0,N}\). For
  every pair of leaves adjacent across this boundary, the corresponding
  population values \(\gamma^-_{N,r}\) and \(\gamma^+_{N,r}\) satisfy
  \(\kappa_{\min}\le\abs{\gamma^+_{N,r}-\gamma^-_{N,r}}\le\kappa_{\max}\).

  \item[(Joint)] \emph{Ancestor--descendant joint density.}
  If an ancestor split uses coordinate \(q_a\), a descendant split uses
  \(q_\ell\), and \(q_a\ne q_\ell\), the pair \((X_{q_a},X_{q_\ell})\) has a
  joint density bounded above by a fixed constant \(\bar f_2<\infty\) on a
  neighbourhood of \((c_{0,a},c_{0,\ell})\).
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Restated from the companion paper}, which states it for a single
regression function; applied here to each of the two nuisances separately.
\end{assumption}

For each resolution \(r\), let \(\cA_r\) be the full product grid generated by
\(r\) cutpoints per coordinate, with mesh \(O(r^{-1})\) and
\(N_r:=\card{\cA_r}\asymp r^d\). Stage~A globally minimises the penalised
empirical risk over trees built from \(\cA_r\), subject to the inflated leaf
budget \(\bar L_A:=\min\{2\bar L-1,N_r\}\) and depth bound \(2d\). Only the
collapsed topology \(\topolcoll(\hat t^A_j)\) is retained from Stage~A, via
the collapse map defined in the companion paper. In Stage~B, each recovered
collapsed split is refined by an exact scan over the observed values of its
splitting coordinate, restricted to the bracket bounded by the nearest
\emph{other} same-coordinate split in the recovered topology (or by the
support boundary), with the topology, descendants, and all other split
coordinates held fixed. Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage} below makes
both stages precise.

\begin{proposition}[Sequential recovery of continuum boundaries -- restated
from the companion paper]
\label{prop:boundary-recovery}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}, \ref{ass:causal},
\ref{ass:moments}, \ref{ass:construct}, \ref{ass:budget},
\ref{ass:global}, and \ref{ass:sparsity-jump}, let
\(r_n\ge r_0\) and \(\lambda_n\to0\) satisfy
\[
  \frac{N_{r_n}}{n}\ll\lambda_n\ll\frac1{r_n},
  \qquad
  N_{r_n}\asymp r_n^d,
  \qquad
  m_n=o(n/r_n)
\]
when Stage~A retains the minimum-leaf constraint. Equivalently, the first
two conditions require \(r_n^{d+1}=o(n)\). Under the inflated Stage-A
budgets \(\bar L_A=\min\{2\bar L-1,N_{r_n}\}\) and \(2d\),
\[
  \PP\left(
    \begin{array}{c}
    \topolcoll(\hat t^A_j)\text{ is in bijection with the true boundaries,}\\
    \text{and each recovered boundary is within }C/r_n
    \text{ of its match}
    \end{array}
  \right)
  \longrightarrow1
\]
for each \(j\in\{e,\mu\}\). Proof: companion paper.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Transition leaves inflate the grid-optimal leaf count --
restated from the companion paper]
\label{prop:transition-leaves}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity-jump} and for all $r\ge r_0$ (so that no
grid atom contains more than one true boundary), let $L^{\mathrm{cont}}_j$ be
the leaf count of the true continuum partition and let $B_{j,r}$ be the
number of its boundaries not lying at a cutpoint of $\cA_r$. Then
\[
  L^{\mathrm{cont}}_j\ \le\ L^{\min}_{j,r}
  \ \le\ L^{\mathrm{cont}}_j+B_{j,r}
  \ \le\ 2L^{\mathrm{cont}}_j-1 ,
\]
where $L^{\min}_{j,r}:=\min\{\card\tau:\tau\in\cS^{\cA_r}_j\}$, and the middle
inequality is an equality whenever the jump values on the two sides of each
such boundary differ from the atom-averaged value between them. Proof:
companion paper.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous superconsistency of all refined boundaries
-- restated from the companion paper]
\label{prop:twostage-superconsistency}
Suppose the standing sampling and conditional second-moment conditions hold.
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:sparsity-jump}, including (Local-Jump) and (Joint),
and~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}, and on the topology-recovery conclusion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-recovery}, every Stage-B-refined boundary
satisfies
\[
  \widehat c_k-c_{0,k}=O_p(n^{-1}),
  \qquad k=1,\ldots,K,
  \qquad K\le\bar L-1.
\]
Equivalently, the result holds simultaneously:
\[
  \max_{1\le k\le K}
  n\abs{\widehat c_k-c_{0,k}}
  =
  O_p(1).
\]
Thus the conclusion applies to an arbitrary fixed-\(\bar L\), fixed-depth
tree, not merely to a two- or three-leaf special case. Proof: companion
paper.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Full-sample inference after topology recovery}

\begin{assumption}[Two-stage solver fidelity]\label{ass:solver-twostage}
For each nuisance $j\in\{e,\mu\}$, the two-stage estimator satisfies the
following conditions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item \emph{Stage-A optimisation.} Stage A computes an exact global minimiser
  of the penalised empirical risk over the grid $\cA_r$, using the inflated leaf
  and depth budgets. Every Stage-A split lies at a pre-specified cutpoint in
  $\cA_r$; thus the pre-specified-cutpoint requirement of
  Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} remains in force for Stage A itself.
  \item \emph{Stage-B optimisation.} Conditional on the topology returned by
  Stage A, Stage B refines thresholds one node at a time, in a fixed
  root-first order: at each node, it computes an exact global minimiser of
  the empirical risk over the observed covariate values lying in that
  node's own bracket (the interval bounded by the nearest other
  same-coordinate split in the recovered topology), holding every other
  threshold in the tree fixed --- already refined for every ancestor (the
  root-first order visits every ancestor before any descendant), still at
  its Stage-A grid value for every descendant and for any not-yet-visited
  node. Stage B is therefore a sequence of single-threshold exact scans, not
  a joint minimisation over the Cartesian product of all thresholds at
  once. Only Stage B may evaluate continuous or observed-value thresholds.
  \item \emph{Full-sample construction.} Stages A and B both use all $n$
  observations. There is no sample splitting or cross-fitting between them.
  \item \emph{Topology preservation.} Stage B refines threshold locations only:
  no split is added or deleted, no pair of splits is reordered, and no split is
  moved to a different covariate coordinate.
  \item \emph{Valid brackets.} For every recovered split, the bracket bounded by
  the nearest other split on the same coordinate on each side, as determined
  by the recovered topology, contains the corresponding true threshold with
  probability tending to one.
  \item \emph{Measurability.} Both stages use deterministic, measurable
  tie-breaking rules. Consequently the refined threshold vector $\hat c$ is a
  well-defined random vector.
  \item \emph{Refined leaf mass.} There exists a constant $\rho>0$, independent
  of $n$, such that, with probability tending to one, every leaf of the refined
  propensity partition contains at least $\rho n$ observations and every leaf
  of the refined outcome partition contains at least $\rho n$ control
  observations. The same bounds hold at the true partition $c_0$. Thus the
  finite-dimensional leaf-refit control underlying
  Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} is available at the anchor and throughout the
  topology-preserving perturbation from $c_0$ to $\hat c$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}
Clause~(e) is a consequence of Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-recovery}.
Indeed, on
\[
  E_{j,n}:=
  \Bigl\{
    \topolcoll(\hat t^A_j)\text{ is in bijection with the true boundaries of }
    \gamma_{0,j}\text{, each within }C/r_n\text{ of its match}
  \Bigr\},
  \qquad \PP(E_{j,n})\to1,
\]
the recovered split corresponding to each true boundary lies within
$C/r_n$ of that boundary, while boundary separation and interiority from
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity-jump} prevent adjacent true boundaries from
entering the same bracket. No rate condition linking $r_n$ to $n$ is needed
below beyond the conditions already required by
Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-recovery}. Stage A supplies only the topology
and brackets; every threshold value in the final estimator is supplied by the
off-grid Stage-B scan. In particular, imposing an additional condition that
ties $r_n$ to either $n^{-1/2}$ or $n^{-1}$ would be false and unnecessary for
the expansion below.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Anchor-and-perturbation expansion]\label{lem:anchor-perturb}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}, \ref{ass:causal},
\ref{ass:moments}, \ref{ass:construct}(a)--(c),
\ref{ass:sparsity-jump}, and \ref{ass:solver-twostage} hold. Suppose also that
the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-recovery} hold for both
nuisances and that Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage-superconsistency} gives,
for each $j\in\{e,\mu\}$,
\[
  \hat c_j-c_{0,j}=O_p(n^{-1})
\]
in Euclidean threshold-location distance conditional on $E_{j,n}$. Let
$E_n=E_{e,n}\cap E_{\mu,n}$, let
$c_0=(c_{0,e},c_{0,\mu})$, and let
$\hat c=(\hat c_e,\hat c_\mu)$. On $E_n$, each $c_{0,j}$ and $\hat c_j$ has
fixed dimension $K_j\le\bar L-1$. Then the full-sample estimator formed from
the refined partitions satisfies
\[
  \sqrt n\bigl\{\thetahat(\hat c)-\theta_0\bigr\}
  =
  \sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0+o_p(1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Proof strategy.}
We anchor the estimator at the fixed true partition $c_0$, apply the existing
fixed-partition expansion there, and then show that moving the thresholds from
$c_0$ to $\hat c$ reassigns only $O_p(1)$ observations. A deterministic
worst-case bound then makes the resulting perturbation
$o_p(n^{-1/2})$ without conditioning on the selected thresholds.

On $E_n$, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-decomp}
  \thetahat(\hat c)-\theta_0
  =
  \underbrace{\bigl\{\thetahat(c_0)-\theta_0\bigr\}}_{\mathrm{(i)}}
  +
  \underbrace{\bigl\{\thetahat(\hat c)-\thetahat(c_0)\bigr\}}_{\mathrm{(ii)}}.
\end{equation}

For term $\mathrm{(i)}$, $c_0$ is fixed and its leaves are sufficient for the
corresponding population nuisances by
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity-jump}. Lemmas~\ref{lem:skeleton},
\ref{lem:U}, and \ref{lem:cross} therefore apply verbatim after specialising
the maximum over $\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$ to the singleton $\{c_0\}$. Their
assumptions hold because the true partition has at most $\bar L$ leaves,
positive leaf mass, and exact leaf constancy. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-fixed-expansion}
  \sqrt n\bigl\{\thetahat(c_0)-\theta_0\bigr\}
  =
  \sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0+o_p(1).
\end{equation}

We next control term $\mathrm{(ii)}$. Let $S_n$ be the set of observations
whose leaf assignment under $\hat c_e$ or $\hat c_\mu$ differs from their
assignment under $c_{0,e}$ or $c_{0,\mu}$. We first prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-reassigned-count}
  \card{S_n}=O_p(1).
\end{equation}
Fix one true boundary $c_{0,k}$ lying on coordinate $q_k$. By the
\emph{(Dens)} clause of Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity-jump}, there are constants
$0<\underline f\le\overline f<\infty$ and a neighbourhood of $c_{0,k}$ on
which the density of $X_{q_k}$ is bounded between these constants. The lower
bound implies the standard order-statistic spacing fact that the gap
containing $c_{0,k}$ has width $O_p(n^{-1})$: for every fixed $t>0$ and all
sufficiently large $n$,
\[
  \PP\!\left(
    \min_{1\le i\le n}\abs{X_{i,q_k}-c_{0,k}}>\frac{t}{n}
  \right)
  \le
  \left(1-\frac{2\underline f t}{n}\right)^n
  \le e^{-2\underline f t}.
\]
Thus the distances from $c_{0,k}$ to the nearest order statistics on either
side are $O_p(n^{-1})$.

More directly, for fixed $M>0$ define
\[
  N_{n,k}(M)
  :=
  \sum_{i=1}^n
  \mathbf 1\!\left\{
    \abs{X_{i,q_k}-c_{0,k}}\le\frac{M}{n}
  \right\}.
\]
The upper density bound gives
\[
  \E\{N_{n,k}(M)\}
  \le
  n\left(\frac{2\overline f M}{n}\right)
  =
  2\overline f M.
\]
Markov's inequality therefore yields
\[
  \PP\{N_{n,k}(M)>B\}
  \le \frac{2\overline f M}{B},
\]
so $N_{n,k}(M)=O_p(1)$ for every fixed $M$. Because
$n\abs{\hat c_k-c_{0,k}}=O_p(1)$, for every $\epsilon>0$ one may choose
$M<\infty$ so that
\[
  \PP\!\left(
    \abs{\hat c_k-c_{0,k}}>\frac{M}{n},\,E_n
  \right)<\epsilon
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$. On the complementary event, every observation
separated from its original leaf by this boundary is among the
$N_{n,k}(M)$ order statistics lying within $M/n$ of $c_{0,k}$. Since the
number of boundaries is fixed, summing over them proves
\eqref{eq:anchor-reassigned-count}.

Set
\[
  M_n:=\max_{i\in S_n}\abs{Y_i},
\]
with $M_n=0$ when $S_n=\emptyset$. Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} implies
$\E Y^2<\infty$. The standard maximal-moment bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-maximal-moment}
  \max_{1\le i\le n}\abs{Y_i}=o_p(\sqrt n),
  \qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
  M_n=o_p(\sqrt n).
\end{equation}
For completeness, finite second moment implies
$t^2\PP(\abs Y>t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$. Therefore, for every $\epsilon>0$,
\[
  \PP\!\left(\max_{1\le i\le n}\abs{Y_i}>
  \epsilon\sqrt n\right)
  \le
  n\,\PP(\abs Y>\epsilon\sqrt n)
  =
  \epsilon^{-2}
  \bigl\{(\epsilon\sqrt n)^2
  \PP(\abs Y>\epsilon\sqrt n)\bigr\}
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
This argument bounds the maximum over the entire sample and therefore remains
valid for any criterion-dependent or otherwise adversarial choice of $S_n$.

We now propagate the reassignment bound through the leaf refits. By
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(g), every relevant leaf denominator is
at least $\rho n$ with probability tending to one. Moving
$s_n:=\card{S_n}$ observations changes a propensity leaf numerator and
denominator by at most $s_n$. For the outcome refit, it changes a control
count by at most $s_n$ and a control-outcome sum by at most $s_nM_n$.
The elementary identity
\[
  \frac{a+\Delta a}{b+\Delta b}-\frac ab
  =
  \frac{b\,\Delta a-a\,\Delta b}
       {b(b+\Delta b)}
\]
and the lower bounds $b,b+\Delta b\ge\rho n$ consequently give, over leaves
whose membership is unchanged away from $S_n$,
\begin{align}
  \max_\ell
  \abs{\widehat e_\ell(\hat c)-\widehat e_\ell(c_0)}
  &\le C\,\frac{s_n}{n}, \label{eq:anchor-leaf-e}\\
  \max_\ell
  \abs{\widehat\mu_\ell(\hat c)-\widehat\mu_\ell(c_0)}
  &\le C\,\frac{s_n(1+M_n)}{n}, \label{eq:anchor-leaf-mu}
\end{align}
for a constant $C<\infty$ depending only on $\rho$. The anchor outcome-leaf
means are $O_p(1)$ because there are finitely many positive-control-mass
leaves and $\E Y^2<\infty$; this accounts for the additive $1$ in
\eqref{eq:anchor-leaf-mu}. Equations
\eqref{eq:anchor-reassigned-count} and \eqref{eq:anchor-maximal-moment} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-leaf-rate}
  \frac{s_n(1+M_n)}{n}=o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}

Clipping from Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c), together with
$1-\ez\ge c$ from Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, keeps every fitted propensity
in $[0,1-c]$. On this interval the map $e\mapsto e/(1-e)$ is Lipschitz because
\[
  \left\lvert
    \frac{d}{de}\frac{e}{1-e}
  \right\rvert
  =
  \frac{1}{(1-e)^2}
  \le c^{-2}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:anchor-leaf-e} propagates to the fitted weights with the same
order.

Let $U(c)$ and $T(c)$ denote the two remainder terms in
Lemma~\ref{lem:skeleton} for the partition indexed by $c$. On observations
outside $S_n$, the change in each fitted nuisance is bounded by
\eqref{eq:anchor-leaf-e}--\eqref{eq:anchor-leaf-mu}. On $S_n$, fitted leaf
values may differ by a constant order amount, but there are only $s_n$
such observations. Since $h$ and all clipped weights are bounded, and since
$\Pn\abs Y=O_p(1)$ by the law of large numbers and $\E Y^2<\infty$, the
triangle inequality gives
\begin{align}
  \abs{U(\hat c)-U(c_0)}
  &\le C\,\frac{s_n(1+M_n)}{n}
    =o_p(n^{-1/2}), \label{eq:anchor-U-perturb}\\
  \abs{T(\hat c)-T(c_0)}
  &\le
    C\left\{
      \frac{s_n(1+M_n)}{n}
      +\frac{s_n}{n}\Pn(1+\abs Y)
    \right\}
    =o_p(n^{-1/2}). \label{eq:anchor-T-perturb}
\end{align}
These are worst-case bounds: they do not average over the identities of the
reassigned observations and do not require independence between $S_n$ and
the data.

Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:skeleton} at $\hat c$ and $c_0$ and subtracting the
two identities cancels the common empirical influence-function term:
\[
  \pihat\bigl\{\thetahat(\hat c)-\thetahat(c_0)\bigr\}
  =
  \{U(\hat c)-U(c_0)\}
  -
  \{T(\hat c)-T(c_0)\}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:anchor-U-perturb}--\eqref{eq:anchor-T-perturb} and
$\pihat\to_p\pi>0$,
\[
  \thetahat(\hat c)-\thetahat(c_0)=o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Combining this display with \eqref{eq:anchor-decomp} and
\eqref{eq:anchor-fixed-expansion} proves the asserted expansion on $E_n$.
Finally, $\PP(E_n)\to1$, so the same expansion holds unconditionally.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
All regularity used above is explicit: topology recovery and the Stage-B
$n^{-1}$ threshold rate control the number of reassignments; the local
\emph{upper} density bound (only; the lower bound is used solely for the
discarded order-statistic-spacing display, not for the count itself)
justifies the order-statistic count; the refined
leaf-mass floor controls the refit denominators; the second moment controls
the sample maximum; and overlap plus clipping supplies the Lipschitz weight
map. The dependencies are Lemmas~\ref{lem:skeleton}, \ref{lem:U}, and
\ref{lem:cross} only at the fixed sufficient anchor. The rates combine as
$O_p(1)o_p(\sqrt n)/n=o_p(n^{-1/2})$. The argument can fail under insufficient
outcome moments, vanishing refined leaf mass, loss of overlap, or failure of
local density control. The $o_p(n^{-1/2})$ perturbation rate is sufficient and
is not strengthened to $O_p(n^{-1})$ under a bare second moment.

\begin{remark}
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} is not load-bearing here. The expansion is anchored
at the true sufficient partition $c_0$ rather than made uniform over
$\cS_e^{\mathrm{cont}}\times\cS_\mu^{\mathrm{cont}}$; the fitted estimator is
then compared directly with that anchor through the observations whose leaf
assignments change. Invariance remains useful as interpretation, but it does
not control the perturbation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Cross-fitting the split-selection step is unnecessary. The bound
$\card{S_n}M_n/n=o_p(n^{-1/2})$ is valid under adversarial,
criterion-dependent selection of the reassigned observations and therefore
covers the full-sample construction directly. Such a fallback would become
necessary if any of three conditions failed: Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}
were weaker than a second moment for $Y$; the Stage-B rate were available
only in Euclidean threshold distance while the covariate density were
unbounded at a true boundary; or -- the binding case in practice, since it
is the one a weaker abstraction could plausibly invite -- the Stage-B rate
were only $\hat c-c_0=o_p(n^{-1/2})$ rather than the sharp
$O_p(n^{-1})$ Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage-superconsistency} actually
delivers. Under $o_p(n^{-1/2})$ alone, $\card{S_n}$ need not stay bounded,
and the product $\card{S_n}M_n/n$ can then exceed $o_p(n^{-1/2})$: a bounded
outcome or a cross-fitted selection step would each independently repair
this, but neither is assumed, and neither is needed, because Stage B in fact
delivers the sharp rate. None of the three conditions occurs here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Under the two-stage estimator, both realised nuisance errors are
$O_p(n^{-1/2})$. Consequently the width conclusion of
Corollary~\ref{cor:width} automatically reduces to $O_p(n^{-1/2})$; no
modification of that corollary is required.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Full-sample two-stage central limit theorem]
\label{thm:main-twostage}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}, \ref{ass:causal},
\ref{ass:moments}, \ref{ass:construct}(a)--(c),
\ref{ass:sparsity-jump}, and \ref{ass:solver-twostage} hold, with
$\cS_e^{\mathrm{cont}}\ne\emptyset$ and
$\cS_\mu^{\mathrm{cont}}\ne\emptyset$. Suppose the conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-recovery} and of
Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage-superconsistency} hold for both
nuisances. Let the Stage-A
partitions, Stage-B refined thresholds, and leaf values all be fitted using
the same $n$ observations, and write $\thetahat:=\thetahat(\hat c)$ for the
resulting estimator (the two-stage analogue of the grid-fitted $\thetahat$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). Then
\[
  \sqrt n\bigl(\thetahat-\theta_0\bigr)
  \rightsquigarrow N(0,V),
\]
where $V$ is the same variance as in~\eqref{eq:vdecomp}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Proof strategy.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-perturb} supplies the asymptotic linear representation;
the central limit theorem is then applied to its fixed influence-function
term exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-perturb},
\[
  \sqrt n\bigl(\thetahat-\theta_0\bigr)
  =
  \sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0+o_p(1).
\]
The remainder of the argument is identical to the concluding step of the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} once this linear expansion is established:
the classical i.i.d.\ central limit theorem applies to $\psi_0$, and Slutsky's
theorem gives the asserted limit.

The variance is unchanged for a population, rather than an
estimator-provenance, reason. It depends only on the population nuisances
$\eta_0$ and $\pi$ through the influence function $\psi_0$. Sufficiency of the
true continuum partition makes its population leaf-refit projections equal
$\gamma_{0,j}$ exactly, so the $\psi_0$ appearing above is literally the same
function as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. No cross-fitting is introduced.
Therefore its variance remains the $V$ in~\eqref{eq:vdecomp}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-perturb} verifies the only new step, namely replacement
of finite-class uniformity by a fixed-anchor perturbation argument. The
remaining dependencies, moment conditions, convergence mode, and central
limit theorem are exactly those used in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. No additional
grid-resolution rate enters the expansion. Failure is localised to failure of
topology recovery, the Stage-B $n^{-1}$ threshold rate, refined leaf mass,
local density bounds, overlap, or the second-moment condition. The limiting
variance cannot change because the population influence function does not
change.

\noindent Theorem~\ref{thm:main-twostage} is simpler than
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} at the final expansion step: no maximum over a
sufficient class remains after anchoring at the true partition.

\begin{corollary}[Variance estimation and confidence intervals, two-stage]
\label{cor:variance-twostage}
Assume the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-twostage}, and write
$\thetahat=\thetahat(\hat c)$, $\ehat=\ehat(\cdot;\hat c)$ and
$\mhat=\mhat(\cdot;\hat c)$ for the estimator and the fitted nuisances formed
from the Stage-B refined partitions. Let $\Vhat$ be the plug-in of
\eqref{eq:vhat} evaluated at these two-stage fits. Then $\Vhat\to_pV$, with
$V$ as in \eqref{eq:vdecomp}, and
$\thetahat\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\Vhat/n}$ has asymptotic coverage
$1-\alpha$ for $\theta_0$, pointwise in $P$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Proof strategy.} Step~2 of the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:variance}
requires only the \emph{empirical} $L_2$ statement
$\Pn[(\widehat g-g_0)^2]=o_p(1)$, where $\widehat g_i$ is the bracketed
summand of \eqref{eq:vhat} and $g_0=\pi\psi_0$. We verify that statement
directly at the two-stage fits, splitting the sample at the reassignment
set $S_n$ of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-perturb}. Step~1 of that
proof, which established $\Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}=o_p(1)$ by a union
bound over the finite sufficient class, is neither available nor needed
here: on the slab reassigned between $c_0$ and $\hat c$, the refined fit
takes the neighbouring leaf's value, which by (Local-Jump) differs from the
truth by at least $\kappa_{\min}$, so $\Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}$ does
\emph{not} tend to zero. See the remark after this proof.

Work on the event $E_n$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-perturb}'s proof, which has
$\PP(E_n)\to1$. Because $\mhat$ and $\what$ are bounded --- the anchor
outcome-leaf means are $O_p(1)$ and the clipped propensity fits lie in
$[0,1-c]$ by Assumptions~\ref{ass:causal} and~\ref{ass:construct}(c) --- there
is a constant $C<\infty$ with
\[
  \abs{\widehat g_i-g_{0,i}}
  \le
  C\Bigl\{
    \bigl(1+\abs{Y_i}\bigr)
    \max_{j\in\{e,\mu\}}
      \abs{\widehat\gamma_j(X_i)-\gamma_{0,j}(X_i)}
    +\abs{\thetahat-\theta_0}
  \Bigr\}
\]
for every $i$, and $\abs{\widehat g_i-g_{0,i}}\le C(1+\abs{Y_i})$
unconditionally.

\emph{Observations outside $S_n$.} For $i\notin S_n$, the leaf of $X_i$ is
the same under $\hat c$ and under $c_0$. The true continuum partition $c_0$
is sufficient by clause (S) of Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity-jump}, and its
leaves carry mass at least $\rho$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(g),
so Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} applies at the fixed partition $c_0$ (using the
generalisation to any partition with at most $\bar L$ leaves, not only
$\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$) and gives
$\max_\ell\abs{\widehat\gamma_j\restriction_\ell(c_0)
 -\gamma_{0,j}\restriction_\ell}=O_p(n^{-1/2})$. Combining with
\eqref{eq:anchor-leaf-e}--\eqref{eq:anchor-leaf-rate},
\[
  \max_{i\notin S_n}
  \abs{\widehat\gamma_j(X_i)-\gamma_{0,j}(X_i)}
  \le
  \max_\ell\abs{\widehat\gamma_{j,\ell}(\hat c)-\widehat\gamma_{j,\ell}(c_0)}
  +\max_\ell\abs{\widehat\gamma_{j,\ell}(c_0)
    -\gamma_{0,j}\restriction_\ell}
  =O_p(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Since $\Pn(1+\abs Y)^2=O_p(1)$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} and the weak
law of large numbers, and $\thetahat-\theta_0=o_p(1)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-twostage},
\[
  \frac1n\sum_{i\notin S_n}(\widehat g_i-g_{0,i})^2
  =
  O_p(n^{-1})+o_p(1)
  =
  o_p(1).
\]

\emph{Observations in $S_n$.} Using the unconditional bound and
$s_n:=\card{S_n}$, $M_n:=\max_{i\in S_n}\abs{Y_i}$,
\[
  \frac1n\sum_{i\in S_n}(\widehat g_i-g_{0,i})^2
  \le
  C^2\,\frac{s_n(1+M_n)^2}{n}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:anchor-reassigned-count} $s_n=O_p(1)$, and by
\eqref{eq:anchor-maximal-moment} $M_n=o_p(\sqrt n)$, so $M_n^2=o_p(n)$ and
the right-hand side is $O_p(1)\,o_p(n)/n=o_p(1)$. This is where the
sup-norm route fails and the empirical $L_2$ route succeeds: on $S_n$ the
fitted and true nuisances differ by a fixed jump, but $S_n$ carries only
$O_p(1)$ of the $n$ observations.

\emph{Conclusion.} Adding the two displays gives
$\Pn[(\widehat g-g_0)^2]=o_p(1)$. Cauchy--Schwarz exactly as in Step~2 of
the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} then gives
$\Pn[\widehat g^2]=\Pn[g_0^2]+o_p(1)$, while
$\Pn[g_0^2]\to_p\E[g_0^2]=\pi^2V$ by the weak law of large numbers and
$\pihat\to_p\pi>0$; Slutsky yields $\Vhat\to_pV$. Coverage follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-twostage} and $V/\Vhat\to_p1$ by Slutsky. All
statements hold on $E_n\cap\{\pihat>0\}$, whose probability tends to one.
\end{proof}

\noindent Three differences from Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} are worth
recording. First, $\Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}$ does \emph{not} tend to zero
here, as noted above; the proof replaces the sup-norm route by an empirical
$L_2$ bound, which is all Step~2 of Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} ever
required. Second, Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} is not used: the expansion is
anchored at the single fixed partition $c_0$, so no selection-weighted
mixture of distinct limits can arise, and Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} is
likewise replaced by $\PP(E_n)\to1$. Third, the finite-sample downward bias
of $\Vhat$ is of relative order $(2\bar L+1)/n$ as before, since the
refined trees carry $\bar L$ leaves each by
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(d) and
Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-recovery}; the $\bar L_A$ leaves searched in
Stage A do not enter, because Stage A contributes only the collapsed
topology.

\begin{remark}[Grid schedule in practice]\label{rem:grid-practice}
The mechanism the theory requires is fixed, and is implemented. A
grid-misaligned true boundary creates a transition leaf and therefore costs
one additional leaf (Proposition~\ref{prop:transition-leaves}), so the
Stage-A search budget must accommodate up to $2\bar L-1$ leaves and depth
$2d$; the collapse map $\topolcoll$ then recovers the topology, and the
Stage-B scan supplies every threshold value. The collapse map and the
single-node threshold refinement of Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(b)
are available as tested routines in the companion \texttt{optimaltrees}
package. What is \emph{not} yet available is a single orchestrating entry
point that composes a Stage-A search, the collapse, and the Stage-B
refinement into one call; assembling the two stages is currently the
user's responsibility, and no such wrapper should be assumed in reading the
results above.

Two practical questions are deliberately left open. First, no default grid
resolution is asserted to be safe. The rate condition $m_n=o(n/r_n)$ of
\S\ref{sec:twostage}'s setup, together with $r_n^{d+1}=o(n)$, is asymptotic
and pins down only the growth rate; it does not identify a finite-sample
$r_n$ adequate at the leaf budgets of interest here, and the resolutions
exercised to date do not span that range. Second, this document establishes
no data-computable substitute for the population optimality conditions used
in the proofs: the Stage-B output is exactly locally optimal over the
observed values in its own bracket by construction
(Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(b)), but that is a property of the
algorithm, not a diagnostic for the conditions of
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity-jump}. A diagnostic adapted to the refined
thresholds would be a natural addition and is not attempted here.
\end{remark}

\section{Cross-fitted inference with optimal-tree nuisances}\label{sec:crossfit}

The estimator of this section drops Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} and imposes no
leaf budget, at the cost of $K$ trees per nuisance rather than one. It is
included for two reasons: it is what remains available when structural sparsity
is untenable, and it is the anchor for Section~\ref{sec:honest}.

Partition $\{1,\dots,n\}$ into $K$ folds of size $n_k$ with $K$ fixed. For each
$k$, fit $\widehat\eta^{(-k)}$ by~\eqref{eq:select} and~\eqref{eq:refit} on the
observations outside fold $k$, and let $\thetahat_{\mathrm{cf}}$ solve
$\sum_k\sum_{i\in\text{fold }k}\psi(O_i;\theta,\widehat\eta^{(-k)})=0$.

\begin{assumption}[Rate condition]\label{ass:rate}
$\Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}\cdot\Lw{\mhat-\mz}=o_p(n^{-1/2})$, where the norms are
evaluated at the fold-wise fits.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Cross-fitted central limit theorem]\label{thm:crossfit}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}, Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:moments}
and~\ref{ass:construct}, \ref{ass:global}, and~\ref{ass:rate}, with $K$-fold
cross-fitting and $K$ fixed,
\[
  \sqrt n\bigl(\thetahat_{\mathrm{cf}}-\theta_0\bigr)\rightsquigarrow N(0,V).
\]
\end{theorem}

\noindent Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit} is an application of the general theory of
debiased machine learning \citep{chernozhukov2018}: Neyman orthogonality of
\eqref{eq:score} is immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear}, and the
product structure of~\eqref{eq:convert} is what Assumption~\ref{ass:rate}
addresses. The pairing in Assumption~\ref{ass:rate} is unweighted for the
propensity and control-weighted for the outcome, exactly as
Corollary~\ref{cor:convert} delivers.

Assumption~\ref{ass:rate} is verifiable, rather than merely assumable, given
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} --- which is exactly the assumption
Section~\ref{sec:honest}'s cross-fitted estimator exists to avoid needing, so
the verification below is a conditional guarantee, not an unconditional one.

\begin{proposition}[The rate condition holds under structural sparsity]
\label{prop:rate-verify}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:global} and \ref{ass:sparsity},
with $\bar L$ fixed,
Assumption~\ref{ass:rate} is satisfied with
$\Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}\cdot\Lw{\mhat-\mz}=O_p(n^{-1})$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The argument is short: Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} applies, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} places $\that_j$ in $\cS_j$ with probability
tending to one; on that event $\gamma_{0,j}$ is leaf-constant, so each
fitted nuisance is a leaf mean of a leaf-constant truth and converges at
$O_p(\sqrt{\bar L/n})=O_p(n^{-1/2})$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:findim}; the product
is $O_p(n^{-1})$. This holds at \emph{any} fixed budget $\bar L$ for which
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} holds --- the saturated budget $\bar L=\card\cA$
plays no special role in the argument itself; its only role, in a document
where $\cX$ is literally finite and every value is a pre-specified cutpoint,
was to make Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} automatic. That role does not
transfer to a general $\cX$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}: sparsity
there asserts that $\ez,\mz$ are exactly step functions of the pre-specified
cutpoints, which no budget choice can force. Assumption~\ref{ass:rate} is
therefore verified only under the assumption Section~\ref{sec:honest} exists
to drop, not unconditionally.

The appeal to Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} in Proposition~\ref{prop:rate-verify} is
not dispensable. The alternative route, which uses only finiteness of the
candidate class and a union bound without a margin, yields excess risk
$O_p(n^{-1/2})$ per nuisance, hence $O_p(n^{-1/4})$ in norm, hence a product of
exactly $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ --- which fails Assumption~\ref{ass:rate}, since the
requirement is $o_p(n^{-1/2})$. Finiteness of the candidate set is not by itself
enough.

\section{Inference when sparsity fails}\label{sec:honest}

Without Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity}, Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} fails and
Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound} becomes the object to control. Validity survives;
efficiency does not; and the price is estimable. Two constructions are given.
The first requires no assumption beyond a valid anchor and is the recommended
default. The second is sharper but requires
Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo} and a conservative variance estimator.

\subsection{The anchor interval}

\begin{theorem}[Anchor interval]\label{thm:anchor}
Let $\thetatil$ satisfy
$\sqrt n(\thetatil-\theta_0)\rightsquigarrow N(0,\widetilde V)$ with
$\widetilde V>0$ and $\widetilde\sigma^2\to_p\widetilde V$, and let
$\widehat\delta=\thetahat-\thetatil$. Then
\[
  C_n=\Bigl[\thetahat\pm\bigl(\abs{\widehat\delta}
   +z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\widetilde\sigma/\sqrt n\bigr)\Bigr]
\]
satisfies $\liminf_n\PP(\theta_0\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha$ on $\{\pihat>0\}$, whose
probability tends to one, with no condition on
$\delta_e$, $\delta_\mu$, $\Dw$, or $\Dm$, and no smoothness condition on the
nuisances.
\end{theorem}

\noindent Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit} supplies the anchor. The interval $C_n$
contains the anchor's own interval recentred at $\thetahat$ and is therefore
never narrower than the cross-fitted interval; what it buys is that the point
estimate remains the one the displayed trees produce. The construction is
deliberately crude, requiring nothing beyond the triangle inequality; a
bias-aware critical value \citep{armstrong2018}, which exploits the fact that a
bias of known magnitude $B$ costs less than $B$ in half-width, is preferable in
practice.

The difference $\widehat\delta$ must be used to widen the interval and never to
correct the point estimate, since $\thetahat-\widehat\delta=\thetatil$
identically: subtracting it returns the cross-fitted estimator exactly and
forfeits the displayed-tree estimate for nothing.

\begin{corollary}[Width]\label{cor:width}
Suppose the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:anchor} hold, together with
Assumptions~\ref{ass:moments}--\ref{ass:construct} and~\ref{ass:budget} and the
conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit} for the anchor. Then
\[
  \abs{\widehat\delta}=O_p\bigl(\Dw\Dm+n^{-1/2}\bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{width}(C_n)=O_p\bigl(\max\{n^{-1/2},\ \Dw\Dm\}\bigr).
\]
In particular the width is $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ whenever $\Dw\Dm=O(n^{-1/2})$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent Three qualifications. The bound is stated in the \emph{realised} errors
of~\eqref{eq:realised}, not the approximation errors of~\eqref{eq:approx}: the two
agree only up to the estimation error of the leaf values \emph{and} the excess risk
of the selected tree, and away from Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} the latter is
only $O_p(n^{-1/2})$, so substituting $\delta_e\delta_\mu$ requires an argument
this corollary does not supply. The two notions also live in different norms ---
$\delta_e$ measures $e$ in $\Ltwo\cdot$ while $\Dw$ measures $w=e/(1-e)$ in
$\Lw\cdot$ --- so any conversion carries the Lipschitz constant $c^{-2}$ and
Lemma~\ref{lem:normequiv}. And the conclusion is an upper bound only: a matching
lower bound, which is what a genuine plateau would assert, does not hold in
general, since the bilinear remainder can vanish with both factors large.

\noindent Corollary~\ref{cor:width} quantifies the trade-off. The correct
statement is not that a bounded-complexity tree cannot deliver $\sqrt n$
inference for $\theta_0$ --- it can, by Theorem~\ref{thm:main} --- but that the
cost of insisting on a displayable tree is an interval whose width contracts at
the parametric rate only while the tree is rich enough to track the nuisances,
and plateaus thereafter at a height equal to the product of the approximation
errors. With $\bar L$ fixed that height does not shrink.

\subsection{The fidelity diagnostic}

\begin{proposition}[Fidelity diagnostic]\label{prop:spectest}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:global}, \ref{ass:sparsity},
and~\ref{ass:rate}, $\sqrt n\,\widehat\delta=o_p(1)$. Consequently, for any
$\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}\asymp n^{-1/2}$, the test that rejects
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} when
$\abs{\widehat\delta}>z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}$ has size tending to
zero. If in addition Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo} holds, so that
$\widehat\eta\to_p\eta_*$, the test is consistent against any alternative with
non-vanishing bias, $b:=P\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_*)\ne0$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Consistency must be indexed by $b\ne0$ and not by
$\delta_e\delta_\mu>0$. The remainder of Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear} is a
\emph{bilinear} form, which vanishes whenever the two error functions are
orthogonal under $\{1-\ez\}\,dP$ --- the very mechanism of double robustness --- so
$\delta_e\delta_\mu>0$ bounds $\abs b$ from above, through
Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound}, and never from below. The diagnostic is accordingly
blind to orthogonal misspecification: the trees can be badly wrong in both
nuisances and the test still fail to reject, because the estimator is then not
biased. Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo} is required because without a population
margin the minimiser may oscillate among near-ties and $\widehat\eta$ has no
limit, so $b$ is undefined.

\noindent Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} both estimators are
asymptotically linear with the \emph{same} influence function $\psi_0$, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit}, so the leading terms of
$\widehat\delta$ cancel identically. The diagnostic is therefore a conservative
test rather than an asymptotically exact one, and it admits no non-degenerate
limiting variance. It cannot serve as a widening term on its own: it is
$O_p(n^{-1/2})$ even when the bias is exactly zero, so it neither centres on nor
dominates the bias.

\subsection{The realised-error refinement}\label{sec:realised}

The anchor interval pays the anchor's full width. A sharper interval is
available under Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo}, which supplies the limits that
misspecification otherwise destroys: without a population margin the minimiser
in~\eqref{eq:select} may oscillate among near-ties, in which case
$\widehat\eta$ has no limit and $V_*$ is not defined.

The anchor interval pays the anchor's full width, and a sharper interval would be
worth having. The natural route measures the realised errors directly, against a
saturated atom-wise reference fit: with
$\widehat D_\mu^2=\sum_a\widehat P(a)\{1-\ehat(a)\}\{\mhat(a)-\mhat_{\mathrm{sat}}(a)\}^2
-\sum_a\widehat P(a)\{1-\ehat(a)\}\widehat\sigma^2(a)/n_{0,a}$
and an analogue $\widehat D_w^2$, one inflates to one-sided upper bounds
$U_j^2=(\widehat D_j^2)_++\kappa_na_{n,j}$ and reports
$\thetahat\pm\{z\sqrt{\Vhat^{+}/n}+U_wU_\mu/\pi\}$, where $\Vhat^{+}$ dominates the
asymptotic variance at the pseudo-true nuisances of
Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo}.

This route is \emph{not} established here, and is stated as prose rather than as a
numbered result because four obstacles remain open. First, the natural statistic
weights by $1-\ehat$ while the realised error $\Dm$ is defined in the
$\{1-\ez\}$-weighted norm; away from sparsity $\ehat\to_pe_*\ne\ez$, so the two
differ at first order and the one-sided guarantee $\PP(U_\mu\ge\Dm)\to1$ can fail
by a constant factor that no vanishing slack repairs. Either the propensity must be
correctly specified, or the norm in Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound} must be recast in
$\{1-\ehat\}$, which is not Cauchy--Schwarz-compatible as that lemma stands.
Second, the degrees-of-freedom correction is unbiased to first order only under a
hold-out for the saturated fit, and a hold-out is in direct tension with the
full-sample construction of Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}: the audited object then
ceases to be the reported tree's fit. Third, the comparison variance $V_*$ is not
well defined by Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo} alone --- under misspecification
$\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)$ diverges, so $V_*$ must be defined by centring at
$\theta(\eta_*)$, and no formula for it is available here, which leaves the
requirement on $\Vhat^{+}$ uncheckable and no $\Vhat^{+}$ exhibited. Fourth, and
most consequentially, asymptotic linearity at $\eta_*$ is not available: the only
routes to it, Lemmas~\ref{lem:U} and~\ref{lem:cross}, are proved over
$\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} is accompanied by an argument that
off $\cS_e$ one has $\sqrt nT=O_p(1)$ rather than $o_p(1)$. Supplying an
off-sparsity linearity lemma is the substantive prerequisite.

What survives is worth stating plainly. Theorem~\ref{thm:anchor} is the paper's
only established inference statement away from sparsity; it needs no condition on
the errors, and its price is the anchor's width. The realised-error refinement is a
direction for the companion paper, not a result of this one. One qualitative
conclusion from the attempt does stand on its own, because it is proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:honest}: the plug-in~\eqref{eq:vhat} cannot serve as a
conservative variance estimator away from sparsity, since with $a=\mu_*-\mz$ and
$b=w_*-\wz$,
\[
  \plim\Vhat=V+2\pi^{-2}\bigl\{\E[\{1-\ez\}\wz b\,\sigma_0^2]
   -\E[\ez\,a\,\Delta m]\bigr\}+O\bigl(\Ltwo a^2+\Ltwo b^2\bigr),
\]
whose first-order term is not sign-definite: taking $b\equiv0$ and
$a=\epsilon\{1-\ez\}\Delta m$ makes it $-\epsilon\E[\ez\{1-\ez\}\Delta m^2]<0$
against a remainder of order $\epsilon^2$, so $\plim\Vhat<V$ for small $\epsilon>0$
whenever $\Delta m\not\equiv0$. That is a defect distinct from the bias, and no
bias allowance repairs it.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results establish.}
Three claims are available, and they answer different questions. Inside the
sparse class, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} give
efficient $\sqrt n$ inference for $\theta_0$ from the displayed trees, with the
ordinary plug-in variance and no sample splitting. Outside it with the bias
bounded, Theorem~\ref{thm:anchor} and Corollary~\ref{cor:width} give valid but
inefficient inference at width $\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_e\delta_\mu\}$, the point
estimate still being the one the trees produce. Outside it with the bias
unbounded, the trees remain an audit object reported alongside the cross-fitted
estimate, with $\widehat\delta$ as a diagnostic and no inferential claim
attached to the trees.

\paragraph{Interpretability.}
The displayed model is the estimator. It is neither a post hoc surrogate for a
black-box nuisance nor a collection of $K$ fold-specific trees: one propensity
tree and one outcome tree, fitted on every observation, are simultaneously the
audit object and the inferential object. The regularisation that makes them
small is statistically inert by Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, so
interpretability is obtained at no cost in validity.

\paragraph{A shared partition degenerates.}
On the common refinement $\that_e\wedge\that_\mu$ both $\what$ and $\mhat$ are
leaf-constant, so the identity $\sum_{i\in\ell,A_i=0}(Y_i-\mhat_\ell)=0$ makes
the control-arm term of~\eqref{eq:estimator} identically zero and the estimator
reduces to the treated-weighted stratified difference in means
$\sum_\ell(n_{1,\ell}/n_1)(\bar Y_{1,\ell}-\bar Y_{0,\ell})$, an exact
finite-sample identity unaffected by clipping. That estimator is consistent and
asymptotically normal with $T=0$ identically, so its analysis is easier. The
reasons not to prefer it are not reasons of validity: a single displayed tree
would carry no fitted propensity values, defeating the purpose of displaying it;
and $\card{\that_e\wedge\that_\mu}$ can be as large as $L_eL_\mu$, so the
refinement may violate Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d) and
Assumption~\ref{ass:budget} even when each tree satisfies them. The propensity's
fitted values are annihilated in the common refinement, but its structure still
selects the stratification and so determines both the point estimate and the
variance.

\paragraph{Boundaries.}
The results above hold at a fixed grid $\cA$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}) and
a fixed leaf budget $\bar L$. Three further extensions, along three distinct
axes, are outside the scope of this document.

\emph{Growing the leaf budget.} Letting $\bar L_n\to\infty$ with the grid
$\cA$ fixed makes $\cT_{\bar L}$ non-fixed, so that uniformity is no longer
free and the entropy $\bar L_n\log(dJ_n)$ becomes active together with a tail
condition; the order in which the present arguments fail as $\bar L_n$ grows
is $U=O_p(\bar L_n/n)$ first, at $\bar L_n\asymp\sqrt n$, then
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}, then Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}, which merely reinstates
a logarithmic factor, and Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} last, which would require
genuine localisation. A sufficient set of conditions for that regime is
$\bar L_n=o(\sqrt n)$, $\bar L_n\log(d_nJ_n)=o(n\Delta_{\min,n}^2)$, and a
bounded or sub-Gaussian outcome. This is a different failure from anything a
continuous covariate space causes on its own: Assumption~\ref{ass:solver}
already keeps $\cT_{\bar L}$ fixed and finite for a general, possibly
continuous $\cX$ by fixing the grid $\cA$ in advance, at the cost --- made
precise in the discussion following Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} in
Section~\ref{sec:setup} --- that structural sparsity becomes a substantive
restriction on $\ez,\mz$ rather than an automatic consequence of finiteness.
What genuinely requires an oracle inequality for optimal trees is letting
$\bar L_n$ itself grow without bound; that reopens exactly the
uniform-in-$\cT_{\bar L}$ argument this document is designed to avoid, and is
developed in the companion paper rather than here.

\emph{Refining the grid.} A second, distinct axis fixes $\bar L$ but lets the
grid itself grow finer, $\card{\cA_n}\to\infty$, so that the pre-specified
cutpoints of Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} approximate an arbitrarily fine
discretisation of a continuous $\cX$ rather than being fixed once and for
all. This is not the same failure mode as growing $\bar L_n$, and the two
axes affect different parts of the present arguments differently: the
per-fixed-$\tau$, $\bar L$-indexed bounds --- Lemma~\ref{lem:U}'s
$O_p(\bar L/n)$, the leaf-value union bound inside Lemma~\ref{lem:findim},
Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}'s per-pair variance, and~\eqref{eq:D-vs-delta} --- are
governed by $\bar L$ alone and remain untouched by refining the grid at any
fixed $n$. The \emph{class}-indexed objects are not: $\log\card{\cT_{\bar
L,n}}=O(\bar L\log(dJ_{n,\max}))$ grows without bound as $J_{n,\max}\to\infty$
under refinement, so the union bounds underlying Lemma~\ref{lem:selection},
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:width} would need an explicit
$\sqrt{\log\card{\cT_{\bar L,n}}/n}$ rate rather than being treated as
unchanged; the atom-mass floor in Assumption~\ref{ass:finite} likewise
degrades, since $\min_{a\in\cA_n}P(a)\to0$ under refinement; and, most
consequentially, Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} fails at every finite $n$
along the sequence, not merely in a limit, because the discretised truth
cannot in general be made exactly leaf-constant on cutpoints that are fixed
in advance while the truth's breakpoints need not fall on them. In
particular the population margin $\Delta_j$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}
degrades at the same rate as the mesh, $\Delta_{j,n}=O(h_n)$, so exact
selection consistency (Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}) cannot be rescued by any
uniform margin floor and must instead be replaced by a finite-class oracle
inequality; see the \texttt{\%~TODO(refining-grid)} markers on
Lemmas~\ref{lem:margin} and~\ref{lem:selection} above, which this paragraph
now confirms rather than merely flags. What a refining grid buys, at the
population level only, is that the mesh-alignment bias between a continuous
truth and its nearest grid-discretised version can be shown to vanish as the
mesh width $h_n\to0$, at a rate governed by the modulus of continuity of
$\ez,\mz$; this is a purely approximation-theoretic fact about the sequence
of grids, not an inferential claim, and the inferential consequences
sketched above are not developed as part of the present document. It is
complementary to Remark~\ref{rem:pshab-fixed-budget} below rather than a
special case of it: the remark bounds the best achievable error of the
\emph{continuum} tree class, not the document's own fixed-grid quantities
$\delta_e,\delta_\mu$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:pshab-bridge} below relates the two,
at a cost), whereas grid refinement asks what happens to the grid-alignment
component of that gap across a sequence of ever-finer grids, a question
neither the remark nor the bridging lemma addresses.

\emph{Continuous covariates without a fixed grid.} Dropping
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} altogether, so that the tree search is not
confined to a pre-specified set of cutpoints, requires approximation theory
and an oracle inequality for optimal trees, and Rashomon sets require
structure-intersection results; both are developed in the companion paper
and neither is used above. The realised-error refinement of
Section~\ref{sec:realised} is left as prose: it requires a conservative
variance estimator dominating the asymptotic variance at the pseudo-true
nuisances, a definition of that variance by centring at the pseudo-true
estimand, an off-sparsity asymptotic-linearity lemma, and a reconciliation of
its hold-out with the full-sample design. None of the four is supplied here.

\paragraph{Assumed decay as an alternative to measurement.}
The alternative to estimating the approximation errors is to assume their decay,
$\delta_j(L)\le C_jL^{-q_j}$ with $q_e+q_\mu>1$, the discrete counterpart of
assuming a Hölder or Besov ball. Weak-$\ell_r$ decay of the tree coefficients
gives $q=1/r-1/2$. No decay rate follows from finiteness alone: parity on $d$
binary coordinates has $\delta(L)$ bounded away from zero for every
axis-aligned tree with $L<2^{d-1}$ leaves and zero thereafter, so decay may be a
step function of $L$. Decay of low-order interactions is not an appropriate
sufficient condition here, since such functions are well approximated by
additive models, which are the least favourable case for trees.

The following observation upgrades an assumed approximation-decay condition to
a consequence of a named smoothness class, while remaining purely
approximation-theoretic.

\begin{remark}[Fixed-budget PSHAB approximation]\label{rem:pshab-fixed-budget}
Let $\cT_{\bar L}^{\circ}$ denote the class of axis-aligned, leaf-constant tree
functions on $[0,1]^d$ with at most $\bar L$ leaves -- the continuum analogue
of the finite-grid class $\cT_{\bar L}$ used above. Define the population
oracle errors
\[
  \delta_e^*(\bar L)
  :=\inf_{f\in\cT_{\bar L}^{\circ}}\Ltwo{f-\ez},
  \qquad
  \delta_\mu^*(\bar L)
  :=\inf_{f\in\cT_{\bar L}^{\circ}}\Lw{f-\mz}.
\]

For each $j\in\{e,\mu\}$, suppose $\gamma_{0,j}$ (where $\gamma_{0,e}=\ez$ and
$\gamma_{0,\mu}=\mz$) belongs to the piecewise sparse anisotropic Besov
class of \citet[Definition~4.5]{xu2026}, with sparsity $s_j$, anisotropic
smoothness indices at most one, and harmonic-mean active-coordinate smoothness
$\bar\alpha_j$. \citet[Theorems~5.5 and~6.1]{xu2026} give an explicit
anisotropic dyadic-grid tree construction achieving, for squared-error loss
and \emph{every} integer $L\ge1$ (their achievability bound is uniform in $L$;
the constant depends on the smoothness-class parameters but not on $L$ or
$n$), squared population approximation error of order $L^{-2\bar\alpha_j/s_j}$
over the active coordinates. Evaluating this achievability bound at the fixed
budget $\bar L$ gives, for every integer $\bar L\ge1$,
\[
  \delta_j^*(\bar L)
  =O\!\left(\bar L^{-\bar\alpha_j/s_j}\right),
  \qquad j\in\{e,\mu\},
\]
and consequently
\[
  \delta_e^*(\bar L)\,\delta_\mu^*(\bar L)
  \;\lesssim\;
  \bar L^{-\bar\alpha_e/s_e-\bar\alpha_\mu/s_\mu}.
\]
Here the constants are independent of $\bar L$ and $n$.  In particular, these
inequalities may be evaluated at the paper's \emph{fixed} budget $\bar L$;
the order notation records the deterministic envelope across possible budgets
and does not introduce a sequence $\bar L_n\to\infty$.

\textbf{Oracle scope.}
This statement bounds only the best possible population approximation error:
it describes what is attainable \emph{if one could choose the best
$\bar L$-leaf tree}.  It makes no claim that the finite-sample penalized-ERM
tree $\that_j$ attains, or nearly attains, this bound.  Relating this population
ceiling to the realized bias of the fitted nuisance is a separate
estimation-and-inference problem not addressed here.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Proof strategy.}
Apply the squared-error achievability bound of \citet[Thms.~5.5,
6.1]{xu2026} separately to the two nuisances, evaluate it at $L=\bar L$, pass
from squared error to norm error by taking square roots, and multiply the
resulting bounds.

By the cited achievability result, for each $j\in\{e,\mu\}$ there is a
constant $C_j<\infty$, depending on the PSHAB-class parameters but
\emph{not on $L$ or $n$}, such that
\[
  \inf_{f\in\cT_L^\circ}
  \Ltwo{f-\gamma_{0,j}}^2
  \le C_jL^{-2\bar\alpha_j/s_j}.
\]
This is precisely the clause permitting evaluation at a fixed leaf budget.
Setting $L=\bar L$ gives
\[
  \bigl\{\delta_e^*(\bar L)\bigr\}^2
  \le C_e\bar L^{-2\bar\alpha_e/s_e},
  \qquad
  \delta_e^*(\bar L)
  \le C_e^{1/2}\bar L^{-\bar\alpha_e/s_e}.
\]
For the outcome nuisance, $0\le1-\ez(X)\le1$ implies, for every
$f\in\cT_{\bar L}^{\circ}$,
\[
  \Lw{f-\mz}^2
  =\E\!\left[\{1-\ez(X)\}\{f(X)-\mz(X)\}^2\right]
  \le\E\!\left[\{f(X)-\mz(X)\}^2\right]
  =\Ltwo{f-\mz}^2.
\]
Taking infima and applying the achievability bound to the right-hand side
therefore yields
\[
  \bigl\{\delta_\mu^*(\bar L)\bigr\}^2
  \le C_\mu\bar L^{-2\bar\alpha_\mu/s_\mu},
  \qquad
  \delta_\mu^*(\bar L)
  \le C_\mu^{1/2}\bar L^{-\bar\alpha_\mu/s_\mu}.
\]
Multiplication gives
\[
  \delta_e^*(\bar L)\delta_\mu^*(\bar L)
  \le (C_eC_\mu)^{1/2}
     \bar L^{-\bar\alpha_e/s_e-\bar\alpha_\mu/s_\mu},
\]
which is the claimed product bound.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
\emph{Assumptions:} the PSHAB hypotheses are stated separately for both
nuisances.  \emph{Dependency:} only the cited external achievability result
and the elementary inequality $\Lw{\cdot}\le\Ltwo{\cdot}$ are used.
\emph{Rates:} taking square roots halves each squared-error exponent, and
multiplication adds the resulting exponents.
\emph{Edge cases:} weak overlap and outcome tails do not affect this
population upper bound; failure occurs if either nuisance is outside the stated
PSHAB class or if the admissible continuum tree class does not contain the
approximants certified by the cited construction.
\emph{Tightness:} the approximation order is inherited from that external
achievability result; no matching lower bound is asserted here.
\emph{Failure localization:} the unproved bridge is deliberately excluded:
nothing above transfers the oracle bound to the fitted tree $\that_j$.
\end{remark}

\noindent Remark~\ref{rem:pshab-fixed-budget} bounds the best achievable
error of the \emph{continuum} class $\cT_{\bar L}^\circ$, whose cutpoints are
unrestricted. It does \emph{not}, by itself, bound $\delta_e,\delta_\mu$
themselves, since $\cT_{\bar L}\subseteq\cT_{\bar L}^\circ$ (every
grid-restricted tree is a continuum tree, but not conversely) gives only
$\delta_j\ge\delta_j^*(\bar L)$: the continuum oracle can always do at least
as well as the grid-restricted one, so its error is a floor under
$\delta_j$, not a ceiling on it. The following bridges the two, at the cost
of an explicit, non-asymptotic penalty for the analyst's own cutpoint mesh.

\begin{assumption}[Rectangular covariate space with cutpoint mesh]
\label{ass:bridge-mesh}
This assumption is local to Lemma~\ref{lem:pshab-bridge} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:pshab-bridge} below and is never in force elsewhere.
$\cX=\prod_{r=1}^d[a_r,b_r]\subset\R^d$ with $d$ fixed. On coordinate $r$ the
pre-specified cutpoints of Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} are
$a_r=t_{r,0}<\cdots<t_{r,J_r}=b_r$; write
$h:=\max_{r\le d}\max_{k\le J_r}(t_{r,k}-t_{r,k-1})$ for the cutpoint mesh.
Call coordinate $r$ \emph{exhaustive} if every value in $[a_r,b_r]$ is itself
a cutpoint (so that no rounding of a split on that coordinate is ever
needed), and \emph{non-exhaustive} otherwise. There is $C_X<\infty$ such
that
\[
  \PP\{\abs{X_r-t}\le u\}\le C_Xu
  \qquad\text{for every non-exhaustive }r,\ \text{every }t\in[a_r,b_r],
  \ \text{and every }u\in(0,h] .
\]
This holds, with $C_X=2\bar c$, whenever $P_X$ has a Lebesgue density bounded
above by $\bar c<\infty$ on the non-exhaustive coordinates (a slab
$\{\abs{X_r-t}\le u\}$ then has Lebesgue measure at most $2u$). The same
density bound is very likely what is needed to transfer
Remark~\ref{rem:pshab-fixed-budget}'s achievability bound from the Lebesgue
measure in which \citet{xu2026} state it into $L_2(P_X)$ in the first place,
since that remark does not itself specify a reference measure; this should
be checked against \citet{xu2026}'s exact statement (arXiv:2603.05340)
before the constants below are treated as sharp.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Grid-restricted approximation bridge]\label{lem:pshab-bridge}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:causal}, \ref{ass:moments}, \ref{ass:solver}, and
\ref{ass:bridge-mesh}, for every $f\in\cT_{\bar L}^\circ$,
\[
  \delta_e\ \le\ \Ltwo{f-\ez}+\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh},
  \qquad
  \delta_\mu\ \le\ \Ltwo{f-\mz}+2\sqrt{C_Y}\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh},
\]
and hence, taking infima over $f\in\cT_{\bar L}^\circ$,
\[
  \delta_e\ \le\ \delta_e^*(\bar L)+\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh},
  \qquad
  \delta_\mu\ \le\ \delta_\mu^{*,L_2}(\bar L)+2\sqrt{C_Y}\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh},
\]
where $\delta_\mu^{*,L_2}(\bar L):=\inf_{f\in\cT_{\bar L}^\circ}\Ltwo{f-\mz}$
satisfies, by Lemma~\ref{lem:normequiv},
$\delta_\mu^*(\bar L)\le\delta_\mu^{*,L_2}(\bar L)\le c^{-1/2}\delta_\mu^*(\bar L)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $f\in\cT_{\bar L}^\circ$ with at most $\bar L-1$ splits. For the
propensity, let $\tilde f=\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(f)$, the leafwise clipping of
Lemma~\ref{lem:clip}; since $\ez(x)\in[0,1-c]$ for every $x$
(Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}), $\tilde f$ is leaf-constant on the same tree
and $\Ltwo{\tilde f-\ez}\le\Ltwo{f-\ez}$ pointwise, hence in norm. For the
outcome, let $\tilde f=\Pi_{[-\sqrt{C_Y},\sqrt{C_Y}]}(f)$; since
$\abs{\mz(x)}\le\sqrt{C_Y}$ for every $x$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} and
Jensen's inequality applied to $\mz(x)=\E[Y\mid A=0,X=x]$), the identical
projection argument gives $\Ltwo{\tilde f-\mz}\le\Ltwo{f-\mz}$.

Round each of $\tilde f$'s at most $\bar L-1$ splits to the nearest
pre-specified cutpoint on its coordinate: on an exhaustive coordinate every
value is already a cutpoint and no rounding is needed; on a non-exhaustive
coordinate the nearest cutpoint is within $h$ of the true split location by
definition of the mesh. Deleting any resulting empty leaves gives a
grid-restricted tree $g\in\cT_{\bar L}$ --- a feasible comparator for
$\delta_e,\delta_\mu$, which are minima over exactly this class. Let $B$
denote the set on which $g$ and $\tilde f$ disagree; a disagreement can occur
only if $X$ lies within $h$ of one of the at most $\bar L-1$ rounded
thresholds, so by a union bound and Assumption~\ref{ass:bridge-mesh},
\[
  \PP(X\in B)\ \le\ (\bar L-1)C_Xh .
\]
Since $g$ and $\tilde f$ agree off $B$ and $\abs{g-\tilde f}$ is bounded by
the range of $\tilde f$ on $B$ ($1-c$ for the propensity, $2\sqrt{C_Y}$ for
the outcome),
\[
  \Ltwo{g-\tilde f}\ \le\ (1-c)\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh}
  \quad\text{(propensity)},
  \qquad
  \Ltwo{g-\tilde f}\ \le\ 2\sqrt{C_Y}\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh}
  \quad\text{(outcome)}.
\]
For the propensity, the displayed bound uses the coarser range-$1$
projection distance rather than the sharper $(1-c)$ one, matching the
statement above; either is valid, and the sharper constant may be
substituted without changing the argument. By the triangle inequality and
$g\in\cT_{\bar L}$,
\[
  \delta_e\ \le\ \Ltwo{g-\ez}
  \ \le\ \Ltwo{\tilde f-\ez}+\Ltwo{g-\tilde f}
  \ \le\ \Ltwo{f-\ez}+\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh} .
\]
For the outcome, the same triangle inequality gives
$\Ltwo{g-\mz}\le\Ltwo{f-\mz}+2\sqrt{C_Y}\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh}$, and since
$1-\ez\le1$,
\[
  \delta_\mu\ \le\ \Lw{g-\mz}\ \le\ \Ltwo{g-\mz}
  \ \le\ \Ltwo{f-\mz}+2\sqrt{C_Y}\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh} .
\]
Taking infima over $f\in\cT_{\bar L}^\circ$ on the right of both displayed
inequalities gives the stated corollaries.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bridged PSHAB bound]\label{cor:pshab-bridge}
Under the hypotheses of Remark~\ref{rem:pshab-fixed-budget} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:pshab-bridge}, for $j\in\{e,\mu\}$,
\[
  \delta_j\ \le\ C_j^{1/2}\bar L^{-\bar\alpha_j/s_j}+K_j\sqrt{(\bar L-1)C_Xh},
  \qquad K_e=1,\ K_\mu=2\sqrt{C_Y}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\noindent\textbf{Scope: this bound is vacuous at the grids this document
motivates.} A single-leaf tree is always feasible, so
$\delta_e\le\Ltwo{\ez-\E\ez}=\mathrm{sd}(\ez(X))\le(1-c)/2$ trivially, with no
argument at all. The bridge above is informative only when its own rounding
term is smaller than this trivial ceiling, i.e.\ only when
$h<1/\{4(\bar L-1)C_X\}$. With $\bar L=8$ and a roughly uniform design
($C_X\approx2$), this requires $h<1/56$ --- more than fifty cutpoints per
coordinate. The cutpoints this document motivates throughout are
\emph{institutional} ones (age brackets, income bands, clinical cutoffs),
typically a handful per coordinate: five age bands give $h\approx0.2$ and a
rounding term of order $\sqrt{7\cdot2\cdot0.2}\approx1.7$, which exceeds the
trivial ceiling of $0.5$ by a factor of roughly three. Corollary~\ref{cor:pshab-bridge} is therefore an honest sense of scale for
\emph{fine} grids, not a claim that the trees this paper's examples
motivate --- displayed on an index card, split at a handful of clinically or
administratively meaningful thresholds --- enjoy a non-vacuous approximation
guarantee. Sharpening the rounding term to depend on the \emph{local jump}
of the approximating tree across each threshold, rather than its global
range, is the only route visible to a non-vacuous bound at a coarse grid,
and would require jump control on \citet{xu2026}'s dyadic construction that
is not extracted here.

\begin{remark}[Relation to grid refinement]

\section{Auxiliary results}\label{app:aux}

\begin{lemma}[Norm equivalence]\label{lem:normequiv}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, for every $f$ with $\Ltwo f<\infty$,
\[
  c\,\Ltwo f^2\le\Lw f^2\le\Ltwo f^2,
  \qquad
  \sqrt c\,\Ltwo f\le\Lw f\le\Ltwo f .
\]
The equivalence constant is $1/c$ for squared norms and $c^{-1/2}$ for norms.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[One-sided clipping is non-expansive]\label{lem:clip}
Let $\Pi_{[0,1-c]}$ denote the Euclidean projection onto $[0,1-c]$. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, $\ez(x)\in[0,1-c]$ for every $x$, and consequently
\[
  \bigl\lvert\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(u)-\ez(x)\bigr\rvert\le\abs{u-\ez(x)}
  \qquad\text{for every }u\in\R .
\]
In particular the clipping prescribed by Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c) can
only decrease the error of a fitted propensity, pointwise and hence in every
$L_p$ norm.
\end{lemma}

\noindent The leaf-mean moment bounds used repeatedly in
Appendices~\ref{app:structure} and~\ref{app:main} are supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:findim}, which is stated in the main text and proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:main}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:normequiv}]
By Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, $1-\ez(x)\ge c$ for every $x$, and since
$\ez(x)\ge0$ we also have $1-\ez(x)\le1$. Hence pointwise
\[
  c\ \le\ 1-\ez(x)\ \le\ 1 .
\]
Multiplying by $f(x)^2\ge0$ preserves both inequalities, giving
$c\,f(x)^2\le\{1-\ez(x)\}f(x)^2\le f(x)^2$, and taking expectations over $X$,
which is monotone on non-negative quantities,
\[
  c\,\E[f(X)^2]\ \le\ \E\bigl[\{1-\ez(X)\}f(X)^2\bigr]\ \le\ \E[f(X)^2],
\]
that is, $c\,\Ltwo f^2\le\Lw f^2\le\Ltwo f^2$. All three quantities are
non-negative, so taking square roots, monotone on $[0,\infty)$, gives
$\sqrt c\,\Ltwo f\le\Lw f\le\Ltwo f$. Rearranging the squared inequality gives
$\Ltwo f^2\le c^{-1}\Lw f^2$, and rearranging the unsquared one gives
$\Ltwo f\le c^{-1/2}\Lw f$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The equivalence constant is $c^{-1}$ at the level of squared norms but
only $c^{-1/2}$ at the level of norms. The two are not interchangeable, and
substituting one for the other produces an incorrect power of $c$ in every
bound that passes through this lemma.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:clip}]
Fix $x$. By Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, $\ez(x)\in[0,1-c]$, so $\ez(x)$ is its
own projection. Let $u\in\R$ and consider three cases.

If $u<0$ then $\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(u)=0$ and $u<0\le\ez(x)$, so
$\abs{u-\ez(x)}=\ez(x)+\abs u\ge\ez(x)=\abs{\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(u)-\ez(x)}$.
If $u\in[0,1-c]$ then $\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(u)=u$ and the two sides are equal.
If $u>1-c$ then $\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(u)=1-c$ and $\ez(x)\le1-c<u$, so
$\abs{u-\ez(x)}=u-\ez(x)\ge(1-c)-\ez(x)=\abs{\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(u)-\ez(x)}$.
In all three cases the displayed inequality holds.

Taking $u=\ehat(x)$ for each $x$ gives the pointwise bound
$\abs{\Pi_{[0,1-c]}(\ehat(x))-\ez(x)}\le\abs{\ehat(x)-\ez(x)}$. Raising to the
$p$-th power preserves it, both sides being non-negative, and integrating against
$P$ preserves it by monotonicity, giving the claim for every $L_p(P)$ norm.
\end{proof}

\noindent One-sidedness is essential rather than cosmetic. Since
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal} permits $\ez(x)<c$, consider clipping instead to
$[c,1-c]$ and take $u=\ez(x)$, a perfectly fitted value. Then
$\ez(x)\notin[c,1-c]$, so $\Pi_{[c,1-c]}(\ez(x))=c$ and
\[
  \abs{\Pi_{[c,1-c]}(u)-\ez(x)}=c-\ez(x)>0=\abs{u-\ez(x)} :
\]
the two-sided clip strictly increases the error at such a cell, even under a
perfect fit. The case analysis above requires $\ez(x)$ to lie in the clipping
interval, which two-sided clipping violates precisely where one-sided positivity
allows $\ez$ to be small.

\section{Proofs for Section~\ref{sec:eif}}\label{app:eif}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear}]
Expand $\pi\,\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)]$ by the tower property, splitting on
$A\in\{0,1\}$ and using $m_1,\mz$ as the conditional means of $Y$ given $(A,X)$;
substitute the identification identity to cancel the $\E[\ez m_1]$ terms; then
factor the remainder using $\ez=(1-\ez)\wz$. Every step is an equality, so no
expansion or remainder is introduced.

By the definition~\eqref{eq:score} of $\psi$,
\[
  \pi\,\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)]
  =\E\bigl[A\{Y-\mu(X)-\theta_0\}\bigr]
  -\E\bigl[(1-A)\,w(X)\{Y-\mu(X)\}\bigr].
\]
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:iid} the tower property applies pointwise in $X$. For
the first term, condition on $X$: given $X$, $A$ is Bernoulli with parameter
$\ez(X)$, and $\E[Y\mid A=1,X]=m_1(X)$ by definition, so
\[
  \E\bigl[A\{Y-\mu(X)-\theta_0\}\bigr]
  =\E\bigl[\ez(X)\{m_1(X)-\mu(X)-\theta_0\}\bigr]
  =\E[\ez m_1]-\E[\ez\mu]-\pi\theta_0,
\]
using $\E[\ez(X)]=\PP(A=1)=\pi$. For the second term,
$\E[Y\mid A=0,X]=\mz(X)$ by definition and $\PP(A=0\mid X)=1-\ez(X)$, so
\[
  \E\bigl[(1-A)\,w(X)\{Y-\mu(X)\}\bigr]
  =\E\bigl[\{1-\ez(X)\}\,w(X)\{\mz(X)-\mu(X)\}\bigr].
\]
Consistency and ignorability, from Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, identify the
target as $\pi\theta_0=\E[\ez(m_1-\mz)]=\E[\ez m_1]-\E[\ez\mz]$. Substituting,
\[
  \pi\,\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)]
  =\E[\ez m_1]-\E[\ez\mu]-\bigl(\E[\ez m_1]-\E[\ez\mz]\bigr)
  -\E\bigl[(1-\ez)w(\mz-\mu)\bigr].
\]
The $\E[\ez m_1]$ terms cancel, leaving
\[
  \pi\,\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)]
  =\E[\ez\mz]-\E[\ez\mu]-\E\bigl[(1-\ez)w(\mz-\mu)\bigr]
  =\E\bigl[(\mz-\mu)\{\ez-(1-\ez)w\}\bigr].
\]
Since $1-\ez(x)\ge c>0$ for every $x$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, the weight
$\wz(x)=\ez(x)/\{1-\ez(x)\}$ is well defined at every $x$, and rearranging gives
$\ez=(1-\ez)\wz$ pointwise --- including at $\ez(x)=0$, where both sides vanish.
Hence
\[
  \ez-(1-\ez)w=(1-\ez)\wz-(1-\ez)w=(1-\ez)(\wz-w).
\]
Substituting and dividing by $\pi>0$ gives~\eqref{eq:bilinear}.
\end{proof}

\noindent Three features of the argument are worth isolating. Assumption
\ref{ass:iid} is used only to justify conditioning under a single fixed law.
The causal content enters exactly once, through the identification identity
$\pi\theta_0=\E[\ez(m_1-\mz)]$; the two conditioning steps are statements about
conditional means and require no causal assumption. And one-sided positivity is
used only to make $\wz$ and $w$ well defined; no step divides by $\ez$ or by $e$,
which is why $\ez(x)=0$ is harmless. Were the candidate to violate $1-e\ge c$,
the weight $w$ would be undefined on a set of positive probability and the
statement ill-posed.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound}]
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c) gives $1-\ehat(x)\ge c$ for every $x$, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear} applies at $\eta=\widehat\eta$:
\[
  \E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\widehat\eta)]
  =\frac1\pi\,\E\Bigl[\{1-\ez(X)\}\{\wz(X)-\what(X)\}\{\mz(X)-\mhat(X)\}\Bigr].
\]
Split the weight as $\{1-\ez\}=\sqrt{1-\ez}\cdot\sqrt{1-\ez}$ and attach one
factor to each bracket:
\[
  \{1-\ez\}\{\wz-\what\}\{\mz-\mhat\}
  =\Bigl[\sqrt{1-\ez}\,\{\wz-\what\}\Bigr]
   \Bigl[\sqrt{1-\ez}\,\{\mz-\mhat\}\Bigr].
\]
Taking absolute values inside the expectation and applying Cauchy--Schwarz,
$\E\abs{UV}\le\{\E U^2\}^{1/2}\{\E V^2\}^{1/2}$, to
$U=\sqrt{1-\ez}\{\wz-\what\}$ and $V=\sqrt{1-\ez}\{\mz-\mhat\}$,
\[
  \Bigl\lvert\E\bigl[\{1-\ez\}\{\wz-\what\}\{\mz-\mhat\}\bigr]\Bigr\rvert
  \le\Lw{\what-\wz}\cdot\Lw{\mhat-\mz}=\Dw\,\Dm ,
\]
since $\E[\{1-\ez\}\{\wz-\what\}^2]=\Lw{\what-\wz}^2$ and likewise for the
outcome factor. Dividing by $\pi>0$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\noindent No power of $c$ appears because the weight $\{1-\ez\}$ is split evenly
and absorbed entirely, one factor $\sqrt{1-\ez}$ into each norm, leaving neither
factor unweighted. This is precisely why $\Lw\cdot$, and not $\Ltwo\cdot$, is the
natural norm for this bound.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:convert}]
Fix $x$ and write $e=\ehat(x)$, $w=\what(x)$. From the definitions
$\wz(x)=\ez(x)/\{1-\ez(x)\}$ and $w=e/(1-e)$,
\[
  \wz(x)-w=\frac{\ez(x)}{1-\ez(x)}-\frac{e}{1-e}
  =\frac{\ez(x)(1-e)-e\{1-\ez(x)\}}{\{1-\ez(x)\}(1-e)} .
\]
The numerator is $\ez(x)-\ez(x)e-e+e\,\ez(x)=\ez(x)-e$, so
\[
  \wz(x)-w=\frac{\ez(x)-e}{\{1-\ez(x)\}(1-e)},
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  \{1-\ez(x)\}\,\abs{\wz(x)-w}=\frac{\abs{\ez(x)-e}}{1-e},
\]
the factor $\{1-\ez(x)\}$ cancelling exactly; it is positive by
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}. By Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c), $1-e\ge c$,
so $1/(1-e)\le c^{-1}$ and
\[
  \{1-\ez(x)\}\,\abs{\wz(x)-\what(x)}\le c^{-1}\abs{\ez(x)-\ehat(x)} .
\]
Substituting into Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear} and bounding termwise,
\[
  \bigl\lvert\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\widehat\eta)]\bigr\rvert
  \le\frac1{\pi c}\,\E\bigl[\abs{\ez-\ehat}\,\abs{\mz-\mhat}\bigr]
  \le\frac1{\pi c}\,\Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}\,\Ltwo{\mhat-\mz},
\]
by unweighted Cauchy--Schwarz. Converting the outcome factor with
Lemma~\ref{lem:normequiv}, $\Ltwo{\mhat-\mz}\le c^{-1/2}\Lw{\mhat-\mz}$, yields
the constant $c^{-1}\cdot c^{-1/2}=c^{-3/2}$ and the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\noindent The asymmetry of~\eqref{eq:convert} is forced rather than chosen. The
identity above consumes the entire factor $\{1-\ez\}$ on the propensity side,
so the propensity error emerges unweighted; the outcome factor never had weight
removed by that identity, and must therefore be converted separately, at cost
$c^{-1/2}$. The two powers of $c$ have distinct origins: $c^{-1}$ from clipping,
$c^{-1/2}$ from norm conversion.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tangent}]
Throughout, $P_0$ satisfies interiority~\eqref{eq:interior}, and
$\E_0[Y^2\mid A=a,X=x]\le C_Y$ for $P_X$-a.e.\ $x$ and both $a$. Write
\[
  \Lambda_1=\{h_1(X):\E h_1=0\},\quad
  \Lambda_2=\{h_2(A,X):\E[h_2\mid X]=0\},\quad
  \Lambda_3=\{h_3\in L^2(P_0):\E[h_3\mid A,X]=0\}.
\]

\emph{(a) Local saturation.} Factor the law as
\[
  dP(x,a,y)=dP_X(x)\cdot\ez(x)^a\{1-\ez(x)\}^{1-a}
   \cdot dP_{Y\mid A=a,X=x}(y),
\]
so that $P$ is determined by the marginal law $P_X$, the propensity $\ez$,
and the conditional laws of
$Y$ given $(A,X)$, the last constrained only by a finite second moment.

We first show each $\Lambda_i$ consists of scores. For $\Lambda_3$, fix bounded
$b$ with $\E[b\mid A,X]=0$ and set $dP_t=\{1+tb\}\,dP_0$ for $\abs t<1/\Linf b$,
so $1+tb\ge0$. Because $\int b\,dP_{0,Y\mid A=a,X=x}=0$ for each $(a,x)$,
integrating over $y$ leaves the law of $(X,A)$ exactly unchanged, so $P_X$ and
$\ez$ are fixed along the path and $\int dP_t=1$. Interiority is what makes
the path admissible: since $\E_0[Y^2\mid A=a,X=x]\le C_Y$ and $b$ is bounded,
$\E_t[Y^2\mid A=a,X=x]=\E_0[Y^2(1+tb)\mid A=a,X=x]$ remains at most $C_Y$ for
all sufficiently small
$\abs t$, and $V_t>0$ for small $\abs t$ by continuity. Its score at $t=0$ is
$\partial_t\log dP_t\vert_{t=0}=b$. Only bounded $b$ have been exhibited, but the
tangent space is closed and such $b$ are dense in $\Lambda_3$: for $h_3\in\Lambda_3$
put $b_K=h_3\mathbf1\{\abs{h_3}\le K\}-\E[h_3\mathbf1\{\abs{h_3}\le K\}\mid A,X]$,
which is bounded, lies in $\Lambda_3$, and converges to $h_3$ in $L^2(P_0)$.
Hence $\Lambda_3$ is contained in the tangent space.

For $\Lambda_1$, fix bounded $h_1$ with $\E h_1=0$ and set
$dP_{X,t}=\{1+th_1\}\,dP_X$ for $\abs t<1/\Linf{h_1}$, leaving the conditional
laws of $A\mid X$ and $Y\mid A,X$ unchanged; $\int dP_{X,t}=1$ automatically
since $\E h_1=0$, the path remains in $\cP_{c,C_Y}$ because $\ez$, $1-\ez$,
and the conditional second moment of $Y$ are untouched by it, and its score
at $t=0$ is $h_1$. As for $\Lambda_3$, bounded $h_1$ are dense in $\Lambda_1$
by the same truncation argument, so $\Lambda_1$ is contained in the tangent
space.

For $\Lambda_2$, fix bounded $h_2$ with $\E[h_2\mid X]=0$ and perturb only the
conditional law of $A\mid X$, multiplicatively:
$P_t(A=a\mid X=x)=\{1+th_2(a,x)\}P_0(A=a\mid X=x)$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$. Since
$\E[h_2\mid X]=0$, $\sum_aP_t(A=a\mid X=x)=1+t\,\E[h_2\mid X=x]=1$
automatically, and $P_t(A=a\mid X=x)\ge0$ for $\abs t<1/\Linf{h_2}$. This is
exactly where the \emph{uniform} form of~\eqref{eq:interior} is needed:
writing $e_t(x)=\ez(x)\{1+th_2(1,x)\}$,
\[
  1-e_t(x)\ \ge\ 1-\ez(x)-\abs t\Linf{h_2}
  \ \ge\ \operatorname*{ess\,inf}\nolimits_{P_0}(1-\ez)-\abs t\Linf{h_2}\ ,
\]
so $1-e_t\ge c$ for every
$\abs t<\{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{P_0}(1-\ez)-c\}/\Linf{h_2}$, an interval
of positive length precisely because of the uniform gap
in~\eqref{eq:interior}; a merely pointwise gap $1-\ez(x)>c$ at every $x$,
without a common margin, need not bound $\abs t$ away from zero once $\cX$
is not finite, since $\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{P_0}(1-\ez)$ could then equal
$c$. The conditional laws of $X$ and of $Y\mid A,X$ are untouched, so
$\E[Y^2\mid A,X]\le C_Y$ is preserved and $P_t\in\cP_{c,C_Y}$ for $\abs t$ in
that interval. The score at $t=0$ is
$\partial_t\log P_t(A=a\mid X=x)\vert_{t=0}=h_2(a,x)$, i.e.\ $h_2$ itself. As
with $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_3$, bounded $h_2$ are dense in $\Lambda_2$ by
the same truncation argument, so $\Lambda_2$ is contained in the tangent
space.

At a boundary law this construction fails: at $\ez(x_0)=0$ the propensity
admits upward perturbation only, and $m_1$ is undefined on the null set
$\{\ez=0,A=1\}$ --- which is precisely why the lemma is stated at interior
laws, and why interiority must supply a uniform margin rather than a merely
pointwise one once $\cX$ need not be finite.

Conversely, every mean-zero $h\in L^2(P_0)$ decomposes as
\[
  h=\underbrace{\E[h\mid X]}_{\in\Lambda_1}
   +\underbrace{\bigl\{\E[h\mid A,X]-\E[h\mid X]\bigr\}}_{\in\Lambda_2}
   +\underbrace{\bigl\{h-\E[h\mid A,X]\bigr\}}_{\in\Lambda_3},
\]
the memberships being immediate from the definitions and $\E h=0$. The three
subspaces are mutually orthogonal in $L^2(P_0)$, so the tangent space is exactly
$\Lambda_1\oplus\Lambda_2\oplus\Lambda_3=\{h\in L^2(P_0):\E h=0\}$: the model is
locally saturated at $P_0$, imposing no local restriction whatever.

Whether this space is infinite-dimensional is a property of $P_0$, not of the
model: it is infinite-dimensional unless $P_0$ is concentrated on finitely
many points of $\cX\times\{0,1\}\times\R$. When $\cX$ is finite this reduces
to: some $P_{Y\mid A=a,X=x}$ has infinite support; if in addition every
conditional law of $Y$ is finitely supported --- for instance if $Y$ is
binary --- then $L^2(P_0)$ itself is finite-dimensional and so is the
tangent space. Saturation, not dimension, is what makes $V$ a
semiparametric rather than a parametric bound.

\emph{(b) Pathwise differentiability.} Let $P_t$ be a regular one-dimensional
submodel through $P_0$ with score $h=h_1+h_2+h_3$ decomposed as above. The
functional is $\theta(P)=\pi^{-1}\E[\ez(m_1-\mz)]$ with $\pi=\E[\ez]$. Along
the $\Lambda_1$ path constructed in part~(a), $\ez$, $m_1$, and $\mz$ are
themselves unperturbed (only $P_X$ moves, multiplicatively by $h_1$), so for
any fixed, $t$-free function $f$, $\partial_t\E_t[f]\vert_{t=0}=\E[fh_1]$ by
the same calculation as for $\Lambda_3$ in part~(a). The remaining two
ingredients are
\[
  \partial_t\ez(x)=\E\bigl[\{A-\ez(X)\}h_2\bigm|X=x\bigr],\quad
  \partial_tm_1(x)=\E[Yh_3\mid A=1,X=x],
\]
with the analogous expression for $\partial_t\mz(x)$; each follows by
differentiating a ratio of integrals and using $\E[h_2\mid X]=0$ and
$\E[h_3\mid A,X]=0$. Each is well defined for $P_X$-a.e.\ $x$ because
interiority gives $0<\ez(x)<1$ there, so both arms have positive mass.

Write $g=\ez\,(m_1-\mz)$, so $\pi\theta=\E[g]$ and
$\partial_t(\pi\theta)=\E[gh_1]+\E[\partial_t\ez\cdot(m_1-\mz)]
+\E[\ez\{\partial_tm_1-\partial_t\mz\}]$, while
$\partial_t\pi=\E[\ez h_1]+\E[\partial_t\ez]$. Since
$\theta=\pi^{-1}\E[g]$, we have
$\partial_t\theta=\pi^{-1}\{\partial_t(\pi\theta)-\theta_0\,\partial_t\pi\}$.
Collecting the three score blocks:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item the $h_1$ block contributes
  $\pi^{-1}\E[h_1\{g-\theta_0\ez\}]
  =\pi^{-1}\E[h_1\,\ez(m_1-\mz-\theta_0)]=\E[\psi_0h_1]$, using
  $\E[\psi_0\mid X]=\pi^{-1}\ez(m_1-\mz-\theta_0)$;
  \item the $h_2$ block contributes
  $\pi^{-1}\E[\partial_t\ez\cdot(m_1-\mz-\theta_0)]
  =\pi^{-1}\E\bigl[\{A-\ez(X)\}h_2\,\Delta m(X)\bigr]=\E[\psi_0h_2]$, since the
  $\Lambda_2$ component of $\psi_0$ is $\pi^{-1}\{A-\ez\}\Delta m$;
  \item the $h_3$ block contributes
  $\pi^{-1}\E[\ez\{\partial_tm_1-\partial_t\mz\}]
  =\pi^{-1}\E\bigl[\{A-(1-A)\wz\}(Y-\mu_A)h_3\bigr]=\E[\psi_0h_3]$, where
  $\mu_A=Am_1+(1-A)\mz$, using $\ez=(1-\ez)\wz$ to convert the control-arm
  derivative into the ATT weight.
\end{itemize}
Summing, $\partial_t\theta(P_t)\vert_{t=0}=\E[\psi_0h]$, so $\psi_0$ is a
gradient. By part~(a) the tangent space is the whole mean-zero space, and
$\psi_0$ lies in it since $\E[\psi_0]=0$ (shown in part~(c)); a gradient lying in
the tangent space is unique there, hence $\psi_0$ is the canonical gradient. This
agrees with \citet{hahn1998}.

\emph{(c) The variance.} Conditioning on $X$,
\[
  \pi\,\E[\psi_0]
  =\E\bigl[\ez(m_1-\mz-\theta_0)\bigr]-\E\bigl[\{1-\ez\}\wz(\mz-\mz)\bigr]
  =\E[\ez(m_1-\mz)]-\pi\theta_0=0
\]
by the identification identity, so $V=\E[\psi_0^2]$. Expanding, and using
$A^2=A$, $(1-A)^2=1-A$ and $A(1-A)=0$ identically, the cross term vanishes
exactly and
\[
  \pi^2\psi_0^2=A\{Y-\mz-\theta_0\}^2+(1-A)\wz^2\{Y-\mz\}^2 .
\]
Taking conditional expectations,
$\E[A\{Y-\mz-\theta_0\}^2\mid X]=\ez\{\sigma_1^2+(m_1-\mz-\theta_0)^2\}$ and
$\E[(1-A)\wz^2\{Y-\mz\}^2\mid X]=\{1-\ez\}\wz^2\sigma_0^2$. Since
$\{1-\ez\}\wz^2=\ez^2/(1-\ez)$,
\[
  \pi^2V=\E[\ez\sigma_1^2]+\E\bigl[\ez(m_1-\mz-\theta_0)^2\bigr]
   +\E\Bigl[\frac{\ez^2\sigma_0^2}{1-\ez}\Bigr],
\]
which is~\eqref{eq:vdecomp}. All three terms are finite: the first two by the
second-moment condition, the second also using $\abs{\theta_0}\le2\sqrt{C_Y}/\pi$
which needs $\pi>0$; the third because $1-\ez\ge c$ bounds $1/(1-\ez)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The last bound is not the only use of positivity in the document ---
the outcome margin of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}, the clipping step of
Corollary~\ref{cor:convert}, and the Lipschitz constant $c^{-4}$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} all consume it --- and it is worth noting that on a finite
$\cX$ the third term is finite as soon as $\ez(x)<1$ pointwise, so the uniform
constant buys nothing here beyond convenience: a maximum over finitely many
finite values is finite regardless of a common margin. This is special to
finite $\cX$. On a general $\cX$, pointwise $\ez<1$ is not enough:
$\E[\ez^2\sigma_0^2/(1-\ez)]$ can diverge even though $\ez(x)<1$ at every $x$,
if $P_X$ concentrates mass where $1-\ez(x)\to0$ without $\sigma_0^2(x)$
compensating. There the uniform bound $1-\ez\ge c$ is what makes the third
term finite, not merely a convenience.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}]
\emph{Step 1: the characterisation is weight-free.} Let $\nu$ be any weight
with $d\nu=w\,dP$ for a measurable $w$ bounded and bounded away from zero
(the outcome case, $d\nu=\{1-\ez\}dP$, has $c\le w\le1$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}; the propensity case is $\nu=P$, $w\equiv1$), and
let $\tau$ have leaves $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_K$. By definition
$(\Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma)(x)=\bar\gamma_k$ for $x\in\ell_k$, where
$\bar\gamma_k=\nu(\ell_k)^{-1}\int_{\ell_k}\gamma\,d\nu$, well defined once
$\nu(\ell_k)>0$. Because $w$ is bounded and bounded away from zero, $\nu$ and
$P$ are mutually absolutely continuous, so $\nu(\ell_k)>0$ exactly when
$P(\ell_k)>0$, and ``$\nu$-a.s.'' and ``$P$-a.s.'' agree on any leaf.

If $\gamma_0$ is $P$-a.s.\ constant on each leaf, say $\gamma_0\equiv v_k$ on
$\ell_k$, then $\bar\gamma_k=v_k=\gamma_0$ $P$-a.s.\ on $\ell_k$, for every
such $\nu$. Conversely, if $\gamma_0$ is not $P$-a.s.\ constant on some leaf
$\ell_k$, it is not $\nu$-a.s.\ constant there either, by mutual absolute
continuity, so $\bar\gamma_k\ne\gamma_0$ on a set of positive $\nu$-measure,
hence of positive $P$-measure. Hence $\Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_0=\gamma_0$
$P$-a.s.\ if and only if $\gamma_0$ is $P$-a.s.\ constant on every leaf of
$\tau$, a condition that does not mention $\nu$. In particular $\cS_j$ is the
same set whichever strictly positive weight is used, which is the
weight-independence asserted in Definition~\ref{def:sufficient}.

\emph{Step 2: exact cancellation.} Fix
$(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$. By Step~1, $\ez$ is $P$-a.s.\
constant on each leaf of $\tau_e$, so $e_{\tau_e}=\Pi_{\tau_e}\ez=\ez$
$P$-a.s.; since $1-\ez\ge c>0$, dividing gives $w_{\tau_e}=\wz$ $P$-a.s., so
$\{\wz-w_{\tau_e}\}=0$ $P$-a.s. Likewise $\mz$ is $P$-a.s.\ constant on each
leaf of $\tau_\mu$, so $\mu_{\tau_\mu}=\mz$ $P$-a.s.\ and
$\{\mz-\mu_{\tau_\mu}\}=0$ $P$-a.s. Since $1-e_{\tau_e}=1-\ez\ge c$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear} applies at $\eta_\tau$ and gives
\[
  \theta(\eta_\tau)-\theta_0
  =\frac1\pi\E\bigl[\{1-\ez\}\{\wz-w_{\tau_e}\}\{\mz-\mu_{\tau_\mu}\}\bigr]=0 .
\]
Either factor vanishing suffices; here both do, and the two partitions play
independent roles and need not coincide. $\ez$ and $\mz$ are the $P$-a.s.\
defined conditional expectations $\E[A\mid X]$ and $\E[Y\mid A=0,X]$;
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal} guarantees $1-\ez\ge c>0$ $P$-a.s., so the
control-arm conditional mean is well defined $P$-a.s.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}]
Fix $j$ and $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}\setminus\cS_j$. By Step~1 of the previous proof,
$\gamma_{0,j}$ is not constant on some leaf $\ell$ of $\tau$. By
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} the set $\cS_j$ is non-empty, and by Step~1 the
population risk is minimised over $\cT_{\bar L}$ at any $\tau'\in\cS_j$, where the
projection reproduces the truth exactly, so $\min_{\tau'}R^{(j)}(\tau')$ equals
the fully unconstrained optimal risk and the displayed excess-risk identities
below hold.

\emph{The propensity, without positivity.} By
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(a),
\[
  R^{(e)}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'}R^{(e)}(\tau')
  =\Ltwo{\Pi_\tau\ez-\ez}^2
  \ \ge\ \int_\ell\bigl\{\bar e_\ell-\ez\bigr\}^2\,dP ,
\]
restricting the integral to $\ell$, where $\bar e_\ell$ is the $P$-weighted
leaf mean of $\ez$. Writing $P(\ell)=\int_\ell dP$ and $\omega=P\restriction_\ell/P(\ell)$
(the restriction of $P$ to $\ell$, renormalised), the right side equals
$P(\ell)\cdot\Var_\omega(\ez)$. Since $\ez$ is not $P$-a.s.\ constant on
$\ell$ and $\omega$ is mutually absolutely continuous with $P\restriction_\ell$,
$\ez$ is not $\omega$-a.s.\ constant either, so $\Var_\omega(\ez)>0$
directly. And $P(\ell)>0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:finite} together with
$\ell$ being a non-empty union of positive-mass atoms: a $P$-null leaf
cannot carry a non-constant function (it would be constant $P$-a.s.\
vacuously). Hence $R^{(e)}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'}R^{(e)}(\tau')\ge P(\ell)\Var_\omega(\ez)>0$.

\emph{The outcome, with positivity.} The same computation applies with the
control measure $d\nu=\{1-\ez\}dP$ in place of $dP$, giving
\[
  R^{(\mu)}(\tau)-\min_{\tau'}R^{(\mu)}(\tau')
  =\Lw{\Pi^\nu_\tau\mz-\mz}^2
  \ \ge\ \nu(\ell)\cdot\Var_{\omega'}(\mz)>0 ,
\]
with $\nu(\ell)=\int_\ell d\nu$ and $\omega'=\nu\restriction_\ell/\nu(\ell)$,
by the same argument: non-constancy of $\mz$ on $\ell$ gives
$\Var_{\omega'}(\mz)>0$ directly, and $\nu(\ell)>0$ follows from $P(\ell)>0$
together with $1-\ez\ge c>0$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}), which is what
rules out a control-weight-null leaf carrying non-constant $\mz$.

\emph{Finiteness.} The displayed excess risk is strictly positive for every
$\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}\setminus\cS_j$, and that set is finite by
Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}. A minimum of finitely many strictly positive
numbers is strictly positive, so $\Delta_j>0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The asymmetry is worth recording: $\Delta_e$ uses only $P(\ell)>0$,
whereas $\Delta_\mu$ additionally uses the lower bound on $1-\ez$. Were
positivity to degenerate, so that $1-\ez=0$ on a set of positive $P$-measure
within some leaf, that region would be invisible to the control-weighted norm
and the leaf could appear $\nu$-constant while $\mz$ still varied on it, $P$-a.s.
This is what Assumption~\ref{ass:causal} rules out, and it is required for
the outcome margin only.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}]
\emph{Step 1: reduction to finitely many atom statistics.} Write
\[
  \widehat P(a)=\Pn\mathbf1\{X\in a\},\quad
  \widehat S^A(a)=\Pn[A\mathbf1\{X\in a\}],\quad
  \widehat S^0(a)=\Pn[(1-A)\mathbf1\{X\in a\}],\quad
  \widehat S^Y(a)=\Pn[(1-A)Y\mathbf1\{X\in a\}]
\]
for each grid atom $a\in\cA$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}). By
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} every leaf $\ell$ of every $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$
is a union of atoms, so
\[
  \widehat p_\ell=\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}\widehat P(a),\qquad
  \bar A_\ell=\frac{\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}\widehat S^A(a)}{\widehat p_\ell},\qquad
  \widehat q_\ell=\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}\widehat S^0(a),\qquad
  \bar Y_{\ell,0}=\frac{\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}\widehat S^Y(a)}{\widehat q_\ell}
\]
are exact linear aggregations of the atom statistics followed by a single
division. For a leaf $\ell$ of $\tau$ the refit value minimises the
within-leaf empirical squared error, so by the analysis-of-variance identity
\[
  R^{(e)}_n(\tau)=\Pn[A]-\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat p_\ell\,\bar A_\ell^2,
  \qquad
  R^{(\mu)}_n(\tau)=\Pn\bigl[(1-A)Y^2\bigr]
   -\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat q_\ell\,\bar Y_{\ell,0}^2 ,
\]
using $A^2=A$. Both risks are on the \emph{unnormalised} control-arm scale, matching
$\Lw\cdot^2=\E[\{1-\ez\}(\cdot)^2]$ as used in Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}; writing the
leading term as the control-conditional average $\Pn[Y^2\mid A=0]$ would put it on
a different scale from $\widehat q_\ell$, so that $R^{(\mu)}_n$ would converge to
$R^{(\mu)}/(1-\pi)$ and the comparison against $\Delta_\mu$ in Step~4 would be off
by that factor. The leading terms $\Pn[A]$ and $\Pn[(1-A)Y^2]$ are
common to every $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$, so in any difference
\[
  R^{(j)}_n(\tau)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau')
  =\sum_{\ell'\in\tau'}\widehat\omega_{\ell'}\bar Z_{\ell'}^2
   -\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\widehat\omega_{\ell}\bar Z_{\ell}^2
\]
they cancel exactly. The right-hand side is a fixed rational function ---
exact linear aggregation followed by one quotient --- of the fixed
$(4\card\cA+2)$-vector of atom and scalar statistics above, with $\card\cA$
fixed by Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}. The population risks admit the
identical representation with $P,\ez,\mz$ in place of their empirical
counterparts, and with $P(a)$ in place of $\widehat P(a)$, etc.

\emph{Step 2: uniformity is free.} By Assumption~\ref{ass:iid} and the weak law
of large numbers, each of the $4\card\cA+2$ atom and scalar statistics converges
in probability to its population counterpart; only a finite second moment is used,
supplied by Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}, and no tail condition is invoked. Every
atom has $P(a)>0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}, and every control atom has mass
at least $c\,P(a)>0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, so every leaf denominator
$\widehat p_\ell,\widehat q_\ell$ is bounded away from zero on an event of
probability tending to one, uniformly over the finitely many leaves of the
finitely many $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}). Since each risk
difference is a fixed continuous (indeed rational) function of finitely many
convergent statistics, a maximum over finitely many $o_p(1)$ terms gives
\[
  \max_{\tau,\tau'\in\cT_{\bar L}}
  \Bigl\lvert\bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau')\bigr\}
   -\bigl\{R^{(j)}(\tau)-R^{(j)}(\tau')\bigr\}\Bigr\rvert=o_p(1).
\]
No covering number, entropy integral, or exponential inequality appears, because
the index set is fixed and finite.

\emph{Step 3: the penalty.} By Assumption~\ref{ass:global}, $\lambda_n\to0$, and
$\card\tau\le\bar L$ for every candidate, so
$\sup_{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}}\lambda_n\card\tau\le\lambda_n\bar L=o(1)$.

\emph{Step 4: conclusion.} Let $\tau^\star\in\cS_j$ minimise $R^{(j)}$ over
$\cT_{\bar L}$, and fix $\delta<\Delta_j/2$.

First, $\tau^\star$ is feasible with probability tending to one. Every leaf of
$\tau^\star$ is a non-empty union of atoms, so has population mass at least
$q_{\min}:=\min_{\ell\in\tau^\star}P(\ell)>0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}
(finiteness of $\cT_{\bar L}$ makes this a minimum over finitely many positive
numbers, hence itself a well-defined constant), and control mass at least
$cq_{\min}>0$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}; its observed count is therefore
$n q_{\min}\{1+o_p(1)\}$, while $m_n/n\to0$ because $\bar Lm_n\lesssim n$ with
$\bar L$ fixed. Hence $\PP(\tau^\star\in\cT^{(j)}_n)\to1$. Restricting the argmin
to $\cT^{(j)}_n$ can only remove competitors, so the argument below is unaffected
by the restriction once $\tau^\star$ is available for comparison.

On the event
\[
  E_n=\Bigl\{\max_{\tau,\tau'}
   \bigl\lvert\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)-R^{(j)}_n(\tau')\}
   -\{R^{(j)}(\tau)-R^{(j)}(\tau')\}\bigr\rvert+\lambda_n\bar L<\delta\Bigr\}
  \cap\bigl\{\tau^\star\in\cT^{(j)}_n\bigr\},
\]
which has probability tending to one by Steps~2 and~3 together with the
preceding paragraph, every $\tau\in\cT^{(j)}_n\setminus\cS_j$ satisfies
$R^{(j)}(\tau)-R^{(j)}(\tau^\star)\ge\Delta_j$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}, hence
\[
  \bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau)+\lambda_n\card\tau\bigr\}
  -\bigl\{R^{(j)}_n(\tau^\star)+\lambda_n\card{\tau^\star}\bigr\}
  \ \ge\ \Delta_j-2\delta\ >\ 0 ,
\]
so no such $\tau$ can be a global minimiser. Since Assumption~\ref{ass:global}
specifies $\that_j$ as a \emph{global} minimiser, $\that_j\in\cS_j$ on $E_n$, and
$\PP(\that_j\in\cS_j)\ge\PP(E_n)\to1$.

\emph{Step 5: the exponential rate.} Suppose $\abs Y\le C$. Hoeffding's
inequality states that for independent $Z_i\in[a_i,b_i]$ and $t>0$,
$\PP(\abs{n^{-1}\sum_i(Z_i-\E Z_i)}>t)\le2\exp\{-2n^2t^2/\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2\}$.
Each of the $4\card\cA+2$ statistics of Step~1 is an i.i.d.\ average of a bounded
variable --- $\mathbf1\{X_i\in a\}$ and $A_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in a\}$ lie in $[0,1]$ and
$(1-A_i)Y_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in a\}$ in $[-C,C]$ --- so its hypotheses hold by
Assumption~\ref{ass:iid} and the outcome bound, giving a deviation bound
$2e^{-c_2nt^2}$ with $c_2$ depending only on $C$. On the region where the
denominators exceed the fixed constants $q_{\min}$ and $cq_{\min}$, the leaf
means are Lipschitz functions of these statistics, so composing preserves
exponential concentration; a union bound over the fixed set $\cT_{\bar L}$ and
absorption of $\lambda_n\bar L=o(1)$ give
$\PP(\that_j\notin\cS_j)\le\card{\cT_{\bar L}}e^{-c_1n\Delta_j^2}$ for large $n$,
with $c_1$ depending only on $C$, $c$ and $q_{\min}$ through $c_2$ and those
Lipschitz constants.

\emph{Step 6: scope.} Boundedness of $Y$ entered only in Step~5. The convergence
$\PP(\that_j\in\cS_j)\to1$ rests on Steps~1--4 and uses
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} alone.
\end{proof}

\noindent Assumption~\ref{ass:global} is used exactly once, in Step~4, and cannot
be weakened there. The argument compares objective values only; a greedy or
otherwise local search could halt at some $\tau\notin\cS_j$ whose immediate
neighbours are worse even though a distant $\tau'\in\cS_j$ has smaller penalised
risk. Proposition~\ref{prop:greedy} exhibits precisely that configuration.

\section{Proofs for Section~\ref{sec:main}}\label{app:main}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regular}]
\emph{Step 1: contiguity.} Fix $h$ in the tangent space at $P_0$, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tangent} is the entire mean-zero space --- saturation, not
dimension, is what the argument uses --- and let
$P_{n,h}=P_{1/\sqrt n,h}$ along a path differentiable in quadratic mean.
Differentiability in quadratic mean gives, under $P_0^n$,
\[
  \log\frac{dP_{n,h}^n}{dP_0^n}
  =\sqrt n\,\Pn h-\tfrac12\E_{P_0}[h^2]+o_{P_0}(1),
\]
with $\sqrt n\Pn h\rightsquigarrow N(0,\E_{P_0}[h^2])$ by the central limit
theorem under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid} and~\ref{ass:moments}. This is local
asymptotic normality \citep{vandervaart1998}, and by Le Cam's first lemma it
implies mutual contiguity $P_{n,h}^n\triangleleft\triangleright P_0^n$.

\emph{Step 2: transfer of the expansion.} Theorem~\ref{thm:main} at $P_0$ gives
$R_n:=\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)-\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0=o_{P_0}(1)$: one sequence of
statistics, with fixed centring $\theta_0$ and fixed $\psi_0$, established at
$P_0$ only. For $\epsilon>0$ set $A_n=\{\abs{R_n}>\epsilon\}$, so
$P_0^n(A_n)\to0$. Contiguity states precisely that events asymptotically null
under $P_0^n$ remain asymptotically null under $P_{n,h}^n$, so
$P_{n,h}^n(A_n)\to0$ and $R_n=o_{P_{n,h}}(1)$. Only the algebraic definition of
$R_n$, valid pointwise on the sample space irrespective of the generating law, and
Step~1 are used; no part of the development is re-derived at $P_{n,h}$.

\emph{Step 3: Le Cam's third lemma.} The pair $(\sqrt n\Pn h,\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0)$
is asymptotically bivariate normal under $P_0^n$, both coordinates being averages
of i.i.d.\ mean-zero functions with finite variance by
Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid} and~\ref{ass:moments}, with asymptotic covariance
$\E_{P_0}[h\psi_0]$. Together with the expansion of Step~1, Le Cam's third lemma
gives, under $P_{n,h}^n$,
$\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0\rightsquigarrow N(\E_{P_0}[\psi_0h],V)$.

\emph{Step 4: the drift of the target.} Pathwise differentiability at $P_0$ with
canonical gradient $\psi_0$, from Lemma~\ref{lem:tangent}, means exactly that
$\sqrt n\{\theta(P_{n,h})-\theta_0\}\to\E_{P_0}[\psi_0h]$ along the path; this is
\citet{hahn1998} and is not rederived.

\emph{Step 5: subtraction.} Under $P_{n,h}^n$,
\[
  \sqrt n\bigl\{\thetahat-\theta(P_{n,h})\bigr\}
  =\bigl[\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0+R_n\bigr]
   -\sqrt n\bigl\{\theta(P_{n,h})-\theta_0\bigr\}
  \rightsquigarrow N\bigl(\E_{P_0}[\psi_0h],V\bigr)-\E_{P_0}[\psi_0h]=N(0,V),
\]
by Slutsky, using $R_n=o_{P_{n,h}}(1)$ from Step~2 and cancellation of the
deterministic shift. The limit is free of $h$, which is regularity. By the
convolution theorem the limit law of any regular estimator is $N(0,V)$ convolved
with some distribution; here that distribution is degenerate, so $\thetahat$
attains the bound and is locally asymptotically efficient in $\cP$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Routing the efficiency claim through the convolution theorem rather
than a local asymptotic minimax bound is deliberate: the minimax supremum form
would additionally require a uniform worst-case control such as
Assumption~\ref{ass:margin-floor}, and is not attempted.

The crux of the argument is that Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} is a property of
the fixed $P_0$ at which Theorem~\ref{thm:main} was established. Steps~1--5 never
evaluate Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the decomposition of
Lemma~\ref{lem:skeleton}, or selection consistency at $P_{n,h}$; the perturbed
laws may leave the sparse class for every $n$ without disturbing anything,
because Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is invoked once, as a statement about $P_0$, and
transferred by the measure-theoretic device of contiguity.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse-bound}]
Restricting to $\cP_\tau$ constrains only the conditional law of $A$ given $X$ to
be leaf-constant; the law of $X$ and the conditional laws of $Y$ given $(A,X)$
remain unrestricted under Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}. Hence the tangent space
is $\Lambda_\tau=\Lambda_X\oplus\Lambda^\tau_{A\mid X}\oplus\Lambda_{Y\mid A,X}$,
differing from $\Lambda$ only in
\[
  \Lambda^\tau_{A\mid X}
  =\bigl\{a(X)\{A-\ez(X)\}:a\text{ constant on the leaves of }\tau\bigr\}
  \subsetneq\Lambda_{A\mid X}.
\]
The efficient influence function in $\cP_\tau$ is the $L^2(P_0)$ projection of
$\psi_0$ onto $\Lambda_\tau$, since gradients in a submodel are projections of any
full-model gradient. The component of $\psi_0$ along $A\mid X$ has Riesz
representer $\pi^{-1}\Delta m(X)\{A-\ez(X)\}$. Since
$\E[\{A-\ez(X)\}^2\mid X]=\ez(1-\ez)$, the inner product of two such terms with
coefficients $c_1,c_2$ equals $\int c_1(x)c_2(x)\,dv(x)$ with
$dv=\ez(1-\ez)\,dP$, so projecting $c=\pi^{-1}\Delta m$ onto
leaf-constant functions in that inner product gives the $v$-weighted leaf
average $\pi^{-1}\Pi^v_\tau\Delta m$. Therefore
\[
  \psi_\tau=\psi_0-\pi^{-1}\{A-\ez(X)\}
   \bigl\{\Delta m(X)-\Pi^v_\tau\Delta m(X)\bigr\},
\]
and by orthogonality
$V=\E[\psi_\tau^2]+\pi^{-2}\E[\ez(1-\ez)\{\Delta m-\Pi^v_\tau\Delta m\}^2]$,
which rearranges to the stated formula. Equality holds if and only if
$\Delta m=\Pi^v_\tau\Delta m$ almost surely, so the inequality is strict whenever
the treated--control contrast varies within a leaf of $\tau$.

If $\ez$ is known then $\Lambda^\tau_{A\mid X}=\{0\}$, so
$\Pi^v_\tau\Delta m\equiv0$ and
$V^{\mathrm{known}\text{-}e}=V-\pi^{-2}\E[\ez(1-\ez)\Delta m^2]$, matching
\citet{hahn1998}. Since
$\{0\}\subseteq\Lambda^\tau_{A\mid X}\subseteq\Lambda_{A\mid X}$, the removed
variance is sandwiched and $V^{\mathrm{known}\text{-}e}\le V_\tau\le V$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Two consequences must be stated carefully. First, refining $\tau$
within the budget \emph{enlarges} $\Lambda^\tau_{A\mid X}$, because a function
constant on a coarser partition is constant on any refinement; projection onto a
larger subspace leaves a smaller residual, so $V_\tau$ increases towards $V$
under refinement. The tangent set of $\bigcup_\tau\cP_\tau$ at $P_0$ is the union
of the $\Lambda_\tau$ over $\tau\in\cS_e(P_0)$, and the relevant bound therefore
corresponds to the largest such subspace, that is to the \emph{finest} sufficient
partition within the budget. Hence
$V_{\mathrm{sparse}}=\max_{\tau\in\cS_e}V_\tau$ depends on $\bar L$ as well as on
$P$, and a gloss referring to the minimal sufficient partition is wrong, since
that would minimise rather than maximise $V_\tau$.

Second, with $\tau$ fixed and known, $\cP_\tau$ has a fixed tangent space and the
known-structure construction of \citet{hahn1998} yields a regular estimator
attaining $V_\tau$ at every $P\in\cP_\tau$. The irregularity arises solely
because $\tau=\tau(P)$ is an unknown and discontinuous functional of $P$:
$\cS_e(P)$ can empty out as $P$ leaves the sparse class, so $\tau(P)$ and
$V_{\tau(P)}$ are discontinuous in $P$. The correct conclusion is that no
estimator attains $V_{\tau(P)}$ at every $P\in\bigcup_\tau\cP_\tau$ while
remaining regular throughout --- not that any estimator attaining
$V_{\mathrm{sparse}}$ must be irregular.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform}]
Fix $\tau=(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$. On $\{\pihat>0\}$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:skeleton} gives identically
$\pihat\{\thetahat(\tau)-\theta_0\}=\pi\Pn\psi_0+U-T$, using
$\phi(\cdot;\eta_0)=\pi\psi_0$. Dividing by $\pihat$ and multiplying by
$\sqrt n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unif-exact}
  \sqrt n\{\thetahat(\tau)-\theta_0\}
  =\frac\pi{\pihat}\sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0+\frac{\sqrt n(U-T)}{\pihat},
\end{equation}
exactly, for each fixed $\tau$. Since $\PP(\pihat>0)\to1$, all statements below
are on that event, which suffices for $o_p(1)$ conclusions.

\emph{The remainder.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:U}, $\abs U=O_p(\bar L/n)$ uniformly over
$\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:cross},
$\max\abs T=O_p(\sqrt{\bar L}/n)$ over $\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$. Hence
\[
  \max_{(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu}\sqrt n\,\abs{U-T}
  \le\sqrt n\max_{\tau_\mu}\abs U+\sqrt n\max_{\tau_e,\tau_\mu}\abs T=o_p(1),
\]
and since $\pihat\to_p\pi>0$ gives $1/\pihat=O_p(1)$, the second term
of~\eqref{eq:unif-exact} is $o_p(1)$ uniformly.

\emph{The leading term.} Write, exactly and with no series expansion,
$\pi/\pihat-1=-(\pihat-\pi)/\pihat$. By Assumption~\ref{ass:iid} and the central
limit theorem for the Bernoulli mean $\pihat=\Pn A$, together with $\pi>0$ from
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, we have $\pihat-\pi=O_p(n^{-1/2})$ and
$1/\pihat=O_p(1)$, so $\pi/\pihat-1=O_p(n^{-1/2})$. Since $V<\infty$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}, $\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0=O_p(1)$, and therefore
\[
  \Bigl(\frac\pi{\pihat}-1\Bigr)\sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0=O_p(n^{-1/2})=o_p(1).
\]
Neither $\pihat$ nor $\psi_0$ depends on $(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)$, so this is a single
scalar sequence rather than a family: uniformity over $\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$ is
vacuous for this term, and the same bound applies verbatim to every $\tau$
simultaneously.

\emph{Combining.} Subtracting $\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0$
from~\eqref{eq:unif-exact}, taking the maximum of the absolute value over the
family and applying the triangle inequality bounds the first summand by the
display above, constant in $\tau$, and the second by the uniform remainder bound.
The family is finite by Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}, so this is a maximum of
finitely many $o_p(1)$ terms, hence $o_p(1)$ by a union bound over an index set
whose cardinality does not grow with $n$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} uses Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid},
\ref{ass:causal}, \ref{ass:moments}, \ref{ass:budget}, and
\ref{ass:construct}(c) through Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}. It uses neither
Assumption~\ref{ass:global} nor Assumption~\ref{ass:margin-floor}, and asserts
nothing uniform in $P$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $E_n=\{\that_e\in\cS_e\}\cap\{\that_\mu\in\cS_\mu\}$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} applied to each $j$ and a union bound,
$\PP(E_n)\to1$.

The bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} concerns the deterministic,
data-independent family $\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$: it asserts that a single random
quantity, the maximum over that family, is $o_p(1)$. On $E_n$ the realised pair
$(\that_e,\that_\mu)$ is some member of the family --- which member may depend on
the data --- and since the bound already covers every member, it applies to
$\thetahat=\thetahat(\that_e,\that_\mu)$ with no conditioning on $E_n$ and none
on any event $\{\that=\tau\}$. Formally, on $E_n$,
\[
  \bigl\lvert\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)-\sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0\bigr\rvert
  \le\max_{\tau\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu}
   \bigl\lvert\sqrt n\{\thetahat(\tau)-\theta_0\}-\sqrt n\,\Pn\psi_0\bigr\rvert ,
\]
so for any $\epsilon>0$,
$\PP(\text{left side}>\epsilon)\le\PP(E_n^c)+\PP(\text{right side}>\epsilon)\to0$
and $\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)=\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0+o_p(1)$.

The summands of $\Pn\psi_0$ are i.i.d.\ by Assumption~\ref{ass:iid}, have mean
zero by Assumption~\ref{ass:causal} directly --- the two-line computation in
part~(c) of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tangent}, which uses only the
identification identity and needs no interiority, so that cells with $\ez(x)=0$
remain harmless here --- and have
variance $V\in(0,\infty)$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}. The classical central
limit theorem gives $\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0\rightsquigarrow N(0,V)$, and the
conclusion follows.
\end{proof}

\noindent Three routes that appear to serve in place of
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} do not. First, Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} states that the
population estimand is $\theta_0$ for every member of the sufficient class, which
is invariance of first-order bias; a central limit theorem requires the
\emph{influence function} to agree across members, and invariance is silent on
the linearisation. Second, writing
$\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)=\sum_\tau\mathbf1\{\that=\tau\}
\sqrt n\{\thetahat(\tau)-\theta_0\}$ does not deliver normality from finiteness
of $\cT_{\bar L}$, because the indicators are correlated with the components,
both being functions of the same data; what is required is asymptotic equivalence
to a \emph{fixed} statistic, which is what Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform} supplies.
Third, conditioning on $\{\that_\mu=\tau_\mu\}$ is not a repair: leaf membership
is a function of $X$, while which cells are merged is determined through the fit
criterion by the control outcomes, so conditioning on the selection event
distorts the conditional law of $Y$ given $X$ and leaves $\mhat$ conditionally
biased.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:variance}]
\emph{Step 1: sup-norm consistency by a union bound.} Fix $j$. For each fixed
$\tau\in\cS_j$, Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} gives
$\max_\ell\abs{\widehat\gamma_j\restriction_\ell-\gamma_{0,j}\restriction_\ell}
=O_p(n^{-1/2})$, and since $\tau\in\cS_j$ the leaf-constant population fit is
$\gamma_{0,j}$ itself, so $\Linf{\widehat\eta_\tau-\eta_0}=O_p(n^{-1/2})$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} every $\tau\in\cS_j$ has the same population limit,
which is what prevents a union over $\cS_j$ from producing a selection-weighted
mixture of distinct limits. As $\cS_j$ is finite,
$\max_{\tau\in\cS_j}\Linf{\widehat\eta_\tau-\eta_0}=O_p(n^{-1/2})$. Decomposing
on the selection event,
\[
  \Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}
  \le\mathbf1\{\that_j\in\cS_j\}\max_{\tau\in\cS_j}\Linf{\widehat\eta_\tau-\eta_0}
   +\mathbf1\{\that_j\notin\cS_j\}\cdot O_p(1),
\]
the second bound using boundedness of the clipped fits under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:causal} and~\ref{ass:construct}(c). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} the second term is $o_p(1)$, so
$\Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}=o_p(1)$.

\emph{Step 2: the plug-in expansion.} Write $\Vhat=\pihat^{-2}\Pn[\widehat g^2]$
where $\widehat g_i$ is the bracketed summand of~\eqref{eq:vhat}, and note that at
$(\eta_0,\theta_0)$ we have $g_0=\pi\psi_0$ pointwise. By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
  \bigl\lvert\Pn[\widehat g^2]-\Pn[g_0^2]\bigr\rvert
  \le\bigl\{\Pn[(\widehat g-g_0)^2]\bigr\}^{1/2}
   \bigl\{\Pn[(\widehat g+g_0)^2]\bigr\}^{1/2} .
\]
Because $\wz$ and the clipped $\what$ are bounded under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:causal} and~\ref{ass:construct}(c), the first factor is
bounded by a constant multiple of
$\{\Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}+\abs{\thetahat-\theta_0}\}$ times an $O_p(1)$ term
controlled by Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}. Only \emph{consistency}
$\thetahat-\theta_0=o_p(1)$ is needed here, and it does not require
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: on the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} the identity
\eqref{eq:skeleton} gives
$\pihat(\thetahat-\theta_0)=\Pn\phi(\cdot;\eta_0)+U-T$, where
$\Pn\phi(\cdot;\eta_0)=o_p(1)$ by the weak law of large numbers, while $U$ and $T$
are each $o_p(1)$ by Step~1 together with boundedness of the clipped weights ---
crude bounds that need neither Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}'s rate nor
Assumption~\ref{ass:rate}. Hence the first factor is $o_p(1)$; the second factor
is $O_p(1)$. Thus $\Pn[\widehat g^2]=\Pn[g_0^2]+o_p(1)$, and
$\Pn[g_0^2]\to_p\E[g_0^2]=\pi^2V$ by the weak law of large numbers. Since
$\pihat\to_p\pi>0$, Slutsky gives $\Vhat\to_pV$.

\emph{Step 3: coverage.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:main},
$\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)/\sqrt V\rightsquigarrow N(0,1)$; with
$V/\Vhat\to_p1$ and Slutsky the same holds with $\Vhat$ in place of $V$, giving
asymptotic coverage $1-\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The argument requires only $\Linf{\widehat\eta-\eta_0}=o_p(1)$, and in
particular uses neither Assumption~\ref{ass:rate} nor Lemma~\ref{lem:cross};
since the cross-term lemma is the harder argument, this isolates the risk in the
development. Two arguments that suggest themselves are invalid and are not used.
Conditioning on a sufficient partition and then appealing to finite-dimensional
theory fails because Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} is a fixed-$\tau$ statement, silent
about the data-dependent $\that$; and conditioning on $\{\that=\tau\}$ fails
because that event is data-dependent, so the conditional sample is not i.i.d.\
from $P$ and no fixed-$\tau$ limit theorem applies.

Two finite-sample properties follow from the same algebra. The estimator
$\thetahat$ solves the estimating equation whose summands are exactly the
brackets appearing in~\eqref{eq:vhat}, so those summands have sample mean exactly
zero and $\Vhat$ is simultaneously the uncentred second moment and the centred
sample variance; no centring ambiguity arises. And because the structure and the
leaf values are fitted on the same $n$ observations that form the residuals, the
residuals are in-sample: counting up to $\bar L$ leaf values for each of $\ehat$
and $\mhat$ together with $\thetahat$ gives $2\bar L+1$ fitted parameters, and
the familiar residual-variance calculation makes $\Vhat$ downward biased by a
relative factor of order $(2\bar L+1)/n$. This vanishes only because $\bar L$ is
fixed by Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}. Finally, there is no tension with
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance} licensing only distributional claims:
$V=\E[\psi_0^2]$ is a second moment of a fixed population function, a functional
of $P$ alone containing no selection, and $\Vhat\to_pV$ is a statement about a
probability limit.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-cover}]
Each ingredient of Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} is re-derived with constants
depending only on $(\Delta_{\min},c,C_4,v_0)$.

\emph{Uniform selection.} By Definition~\ref{def:uniformity},
$\min_j\Delta_j\ge\Delta_{\min}$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}'s bound is at most
$\card{\cT_{\bar L}}e^{-c_1n\Delta_{\min}^2}$, a bound free of $P$ that tends to
zero; hence $\sup_P\PP_P(\that_j\notin\cS_j)\to0$.

\emph{Uniform moments.} The constraint $1-\ez\ge c$ bounds $\wz$ and the clipped
$\what$ uniformly, and $\E[Y^4\mid A,X]\le C_4$ then bounds $\E[\psi_0^4]$
uniformly. Consequently the third absolute moment
$\rho_3=\E\abs{\psi_0}^3\le\{\E\psi_0^2\}^{1/2}\{\E\psi_0^4\}^{1/2}$ is bounded
uniformly over the class.

\emph{Uniform central limit theorem.} The Berry--Esseen bound for i.i.d.\ sums
gives
\[
  \sup_x\bigl\lvert\PP\bigl(\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0/\sqrt V\le x\bigr)-\Phi(x)\bigr\rvert
  \le C\,\rho_3\,n^{-1/2}V^{-3/2},
\]
whose hypotheses --- independence from Assumption~\ref{ass:iid}, a finite third
absolute moment, and non-degeneracy --- hold uniformly, the last because
$V\ge v_0$. The right side is therefore at most $C\rho_3v_0^{-3/2}n^{-1/2}\to0$
uniformly.

\emph{Uniform variance consistency and coverage.} Steps~1 and~2 of
Corollary~\ref{cor:variance} go through with the union bound replaced by its
uniform version and the Cauchy--Schwarz remainder controlled by $C_4$ and $c$, so
$\sup_P\PP_P(\abs{\Vhat/V-1}>\epsilon)\to0$. Combining with the uniform
Berry--Esseen rate,
$\sup_P\bigl\lvert\PP_P\bigl(\theta_0\in\thetahat\pm
z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\Vhat/n}\bigr)-(1-\alpha)\bigr\rvert\to0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Each of the four constants is consumed at a definite point.
$\Delta_{\min}$ uniformises Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}. The constant $c$
uniformises boundedness of $\wz$ and $\what$, which feeds both the moment bounds
and Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c). The fourth moment $C_4$ is required twice:
for the third absolute moment in the Berry--Esseen bound, and because
uniform-in-$P$ selection consistency rests on a Chebyshev step applied to a
squared-error risk, whose variance is itself a fourth-moment quantity, so
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} does not suffice uniformly. And $v_0$ keeps $V$, and
hence the Berry--Esseen denominator $V^{3/2}$, away from zero; without it, or
without $C_4$, the variance may be unbounded or degenerate along a sequence in
the class and uniform coverage cannot hold at all. No tail condition is needed
for the pointwise results; the uniform version requires a uniform fourth moment
or a bounded outcome.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:skeleton}]
Work on $\{\pihat>0\}$. Multiplying the estimating equation
$\Pn\psi(\cdot;\thetahat,\widehat\eta)=0$ by $\pi>0$, which is a fixed constant
by Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, gives
\[
  \Pn\Bigl[A\{Y-\mhat(X)-\thetahat\}
   -(1-A)\what(X)\{Y-\mhat(X)\}\Bigr]=0 .
\]
The left side is affine in $\thetahat$ with coefficient $-\Pn A=-\pihat\ne0$, so
the solution is unique, and isolating it gives
\[
  \pihat\,\thetahat=\Pn\bigl[A\{Y-\mhat(X)\}\bigr]
   -\Pn\bigl[(1-A)\what(X)\{Y-\mhat(X)\}\bigr].
\]
Subtracting $\pihat\theta_0$ and comparing with the definition of $\phi$ yields
$\pihat(\thetahat-\theta_0)=\Pn\phi(\cdot;\widehat\eta)$, exactly. The treated
share is not substituted anywhere: $\pihat=\Pn A$ arises as the coefficient of
$\theta$, which is why no further correction for estimating $\pi$ is required.

At the truth, $\psi_0=\pi^{-1}[A\{Y-\mz-\theta_0\}-(1-A)\wz\{Y-\mz\}]$, so
$\phi(\cdot;\eta_0)=\pi\psi_0$ identically.

For the decomposition, substitute $\what=\wz+\Delta w$ and
$\mhat=\mz+\Delta\mu$ in the control-arm term:
\[
  \what\{Y-\mhat\}
  =(\wz+\Delta w)(Y-\mz-\Delta\mu)
  =\wz\{Y-\mz\}-\wz\Delta\mu+\Delta w\{Y-\mz\}-\Delta w\Delta\mu .
\]
Since $\{Y-\mz\}-\Delta\mu=Y-\mhat$, the last two terms combine to
$\Delta w\{Y-\mhat\}$, giving
$\what\{Y-\mhat\}-\wz\{Y-\mz\}=-\wz\Delta\mu+\Delta w\{Y-\mhat\}$. Hence
\[
  \phi(\cdot;\widehat\eta)-\phi(\cdot;\eta_0)
  =-A\Delta\mu+(1-A)\wz\Delta\mu-(1-A)\Delta w\{Y-\mhat\}
  =h\,\Delta\mu-(1-A)\Delta w\{Y-\mhat\},
\]
with $h=\wz(1-A)-A$. Applying $\Pn$ gives $U-T$ and
therefore~\eqref{eq:skeleton}. Finally, for every $x$,
$\E[h\mid X=x]=\wz(x)\{1-\ez(x)\}-\ez(x)=\ez(x)-\ez(x)=0$, using
$\wz\{1-\ez\}=\ez$, which is available because $1-\ez\ge c>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:findim}]
A leaf-constant function on $\tau$ is described by one number per leaf, so the
refit is determined by a vector in $\R^{\card\tau}$ with
$\card\tau\le\bar L$.

\emph{Occupancy.} The lemma's own hypothesis gives $\PP(X\in\ell)>0$ for
every $\ell\in\tau$, and $\tau$ has at most $\bar L$ leaves by
Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}, so $\min_{\ell\in\tau}\PP(X\in\ell)>0$ is a
minimum over finitely many strictly positive numbers, a constant free of
$n$. Writing
$N_\ell=\sum_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in\ell\}$, Assumption~\ref{ass:iid} makes $N_\ell$
binomial with mean $n\PP(X\in\ell)$, so $N_\ell\asymp n$. For $j=\mu$, the
control count satisfies
$\PP(X\in\ell,A=0)=\int_\ell\{1-\ez\}\,dP\ge c\,\PP(X\in\ell)>0$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, so $N^0_\ell\asymp n$ likewise.

\emph{The propensity leaf.} Conditionally on $\{X_i\in\ell\}$, the $A_i$ are
independent with $\E[A_i\mid X_i]=\ez(X_i)$, so $\ehat\restriction_\ell$ has
conditional mean $(\Pi_\tau\ez)\restriction_\ell$ and, by the law of total
variance, conditional variance
$N_\ell^{-1}\{\E[\ez(1-\ez)\mid X\in\ell]+\Var(\ez\mid X\in\ell)\}
\le N_\ell^{-1}\{1/4+1/4\}$, using $\ez\in[0,1]$ for both terms. Chebyshev
and $N_\ell\asymp n$ give $O_p(n^{-1/2})$. If $\tau\in\cS_e$, $\ez$ is
constant on $\ell$, the heterogeneity term $\Var(\ez\mid X\in\ell)$ vanishes,
and $(\Pi_\tau\ez)\restriction_\ell=\ez\restriction_\ell$, recovering the
sharper single-Bernoulli variance bound $1/(4N_\ell)$.

\emph{The outcome leaf.} Conditionally on $\{X_i\in\ell,A_i=0\}$, the
control-arm outcomes $Y_i$ are independent with conditional mean
$(\Pi^\nu_\tau\mz)\restriction_\ell$ and, by the law of total variance,
conditional variance at most $2C_Y$: $\Var(Y\mid A=0,X)\le C_Y$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} directly, and $\Var(\mz\mid X\in\ell,A=0)\le C_Y$
since $\mz(x)^2\le\E[Y^2\mid A=0,X=x]\le C_Y$ by Jensen. Chebyshev with
$N^0_\ell\asymp n$ gives $O_p(n^{-1/2})$. If $\tau\in\cS_\mu$, $\mz$ is
constant on $\ell$, the heterogeneity term vanishes,
$(\Pi^\nu_\tau\mz)\restriction_\ell=\mz\restriction_\ell$, and the bound
sharpens to $C_Y/N^0_\ell$ as before.

\emph{Maximum over leaves.} There are at most $\bar L$ leaves by
Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}, so a union bound over them preserves the order.
\end{proof}

\noindent Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:finite} and~\ref{ass:causal} the positive-mass
hypothesis is automatically satisfied by \emph{every} $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$, since a
leaf is a non-empty union of atoms, every atom has $P(a)>0$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}, and control mass is at least $c\,P(a)$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}. It is therefore not load-bearing here, and is
retained only because the lemma is stated for a generic $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$
independent of this document's own standing assumptions: were the
positive-atom-mass half of Assumption~\ref{ass:finite} dropped, a leaf built
entirely of null atoms would give $N_\ell=o_p(n)$, possibly vanishing with
non-negligible probability, leaving the leaf mean undefined or based on
$o_p(n)$ observations, and the conclusion would fail. The occupancy step
should be read as deriving the qualifier from Assumption~\ref{ass:finite},
not as consuming it as a hypothesis.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:U}]
Fix $\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$. The refit $\mhat$ is leaf-constant on $\tau_\mu$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(b), and $\mz$ is leaf-constant because
$\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$; hence so is $\Delta\mu$, with
$\Delta\mu(X)=\sum_{\ell}(\mhat\restriction_\ell-\mz\restriction_\ell)
\mathbf1\{X\in\ell\}$, exactly one indicator being active. Substituting into
$U=\Pn[h\Delta\mu]$ and using linearity,
\[
  U=\sum_{\ell\in\tau_\mu}
   \bigl(\mhat\restriction_\ell-\mz\restriction_\ell\bigr)\cdot
   \Pn\bigl[h\,\mathbf1\{X\in\ell\}\bigr].
\]
For the second factor, Lemma~\ref{lem:skeleton} gives $\E[h\mid X]=0$, so
$\E[h\mathbf1_\ell]=0$. Since $h=-1$ when $A=1$ and $h=\wz(X)$ when $A=0$,
\[
  \E[h^2\mid X]=\ez+\{1-\ez\}\wz^2\le1+\bigl\{(1-c)/c\bigr\}^2<\infty
\]
by Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, so the summands are i.i.d.\ with mean zero and
finite variance and $\Pn[h\mathbf1_\ell]=O_p(n^{-1/2})$. For the first factor,
Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} applied at $j=\mu$ gives $O_p(n^{-1/2})$. No independence
between the factors is needed: a product of two $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ terms on a common
probability space is $O_p(n^{-1})$. Summing over at most $\bar L$ leaves gives
$\abs U=O_p(\bar L/n)$, and since $\bar L$ is fixed by
Assumption~\ref{ass:budget} this is $o_p(n^{-1/2})$. The bound holds for each
fixed $\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$, and $\cS_\mu$ is contained in the fixed finite set
$\cT_{\bar L}$, so the maximum over it satisfies the same bound.
\end{proof}

\noindent Two features of Lemma~\ref{lem:U} are worth isolating. The bound does
not involve $\tau_e$ or $\cS_e$ at any point, because $h=\wz(1-A)-A$ is built
from the \emph{true} weight and never from $\ehat$; this is in contrast with $T$,
which involves $\Delta w$ and hence $\tau_e$. And since
$\abs U=O_p(\bar L/n)$ while $\abs T=O_p(\sqrt{\bar L}/n)$, it is $U$ that would bind
first as the leaf budget grows, which is why the budget enters the theory through
this term.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}]
For a fixed pair $(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)\in\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$ we show that
$T=n^{-1}\sum_{i:A_i=0}a_i\varepsilon_i$ \emph{exactly}, where the $a_i$ are
$\sigma(X_{1:n},A_{1:n})$-measurable and centred within the leaves of $\tau_\mu$.
Conditioning on $(X_{1:n},A_{1:n})$ then gives conditional mean zero and a
variance controlled by $\sum a_i^2$, which is bounded by $c^{-4}L_e/4$ using the
leaf-constancy of $\ez$ on $\tau_e$. Chebyshev gives the rate for the fixed pair,
and the maximum over the fixed finite index set preserves it.

\emph{Step 1: exact recentring.} Fix $\tau_e\in\cS_e$ and $\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$;
no maximisation over, or conditioning on, an estimated partition occurs until the
final step. Group $T$ by the leaves $\ell$ of $\tau_\mu$. For any
$\tau_\mu$-leaf-constant $c_\ell$, replacing $\Delta w(X_i)$ by
$\Delta w(X_i)-c_\ell$ within leaf $\ell$ changes the leaf sum by
$-c_\ell\sum_{i\in\ell,A_i=0}\{Y_i-\mhat\restriction_\ell\}=0$, by the exact
identity supplied by Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(b). Choosing $c_\ell$ to be
the control-observation average $\overline{\Delta w}_\ell$ and setting
$a_i=\Delta w(X_i)-\overline{\Delta w}_{\ell_\mu(i)}$ therefore gives
\[
  T=n^{-1}\sum_{i:A_i=0}a_i\bigl\{Y_i-\mhat\restriction_{\ell_\mu(i)}\bigr\},
  \qquad
  \sum_{i\in\ell,A_i=0}a_i=0\ \text{ for every leaf }\ell\text{ of }\tau_\mu.
\]

\emph{Step 2: the exact identity.} Since $\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$, $\mz$ is
$P$-a.s.\ constant on each leaf, so
$Y_i-\mhat\restriction_\ell
=\varepsilon_i-(\mhat\restriction_\ell-\mz\restriction_\ell)$ for $i\in\ell$.
Substituting,
\[
  T=n^{-1}\sum_{i:A_i=0}a_i\varepsilon_i
   -n^{-1}\sum_{\ell}\bigl(\mhat\restriction_\ell-\mz\restriction_\ell\bigr)
    \sum_{i\in\ell,A_i=0}a_i ,
\]
and the inner sum vanishes for every $\ell$ by Step~1. Hence
$T=n^{-1}\sum_{i:A_i=0}a_i\varepsilon_i$ exactly.

\emph{Step 3: conditional mean and variance.} For fixed $\tau_e,\tau_\mu$ each
$a_i$ is a function of $\Delta w(X_i)$, hence of $\ehat$, which is
$\sigma(X_{1:n},A_{1:n})$-measurable, and of the within-leaf control average
$\overline{\Delta w}_{\ell_\mu(i)}$, also $\sigma(X_{1:n},A_{1:n})$-measurable.
So $\{a_i\}$ is $\sigma(X_{1:n},A_{1:n})$-measurable. By
Assumption~\ref{ass:iid}, conditionally on $(X_{1:n},A_{1:n})$ the variables
$\{Y_i:A_i=0\}$ are independent with $\E[\varepsilon_i\mid X_i,A_i=0]=0$ by the
definition of $\mz$ and $\Var(\varepsilon_i\mid X_i,A_i=0)\le C_Y$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}. Therefore
\[
  \E[T\mid X,A]=0,\qquad
  \Var(T\mid X,A)\le C_Y\,n^{-2}\sum_{i:A_i=0}a_i^2 .
\]
This is where fixing $\tau_\mu$ is load-bearing: were $\tau_\mu$ replaced by
$\that_\mu$, the coefficients $a_i$ would depend on $\{Y_i:A_i=0\}$ and the
cancellation above would fail. Fixing $\tau_e$ is what keeps the binomial
variance bound of Step~5 valid, since that step treats $A_{1:n}$ as random given
$X_{1:n}$.

\emph{Step 4: bounding $\sum a_i^2$.} Centring within a leaf is the $\ell_2$
projection onto the orthogonal complement of the constants, so
$\sum_{i\in\ell,A_i=0}a_i^2\le\sum_{i\in\ell,A_i=0}\Delta w(X_i)^2$ for each
$\ell$, and summing over leaves,
$\sum_{i:A_i=0}a_i^2\le\sum_{i:A_i=0}\Delta w(X_i)^2$. Because
$\tau_e\in\cS_e$, $\ez$ is $\tau_e$-leaf-constant, so for $i$ in the
$\tau_e$-leaf $\ell'$ we have
$\Delta w(X_i)=\what\restriction_{\ell'}-\wz\restriction_{\ell'}$. Both
$\ehat\restriction_{\ell'}$, by Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c), and
$\ez\restriction_{\ell'}$, by Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, lie in $[0,1-c]$, on
which $u\mapsto u/(1-u)$ has derivative $(1-u)^{-2}\le c^{-2}$; the mean value
theorem gives
$\abs{\what\restriction_{\ell'}-\wz\restriction_{\ell'}}
\le c^{-2}\abs{\ehat\restriction_{\ell'}-\ez\restriction_{\ell'}}$. Hence
\[
  \sum_{i:A_i=0}\Delta w(X_i)^2
  =\sum_{\ell'}n_{0,\ell'}
   \bigl(\what\restriction_{\ell'}-\wz\restriction_{\ell'}\bigr)^2
  \le c^{-4}\sum_{\ell'}n_{0,\ell'}
   \bigl(\ehat\restriction_{\ell'}-\ez\restriction_{\ell'}\bigr)^2 .
\]

\emph{Step 5: no leaf-mass condition is required.} Let
$\ehat^{\mathrm{pre}}\restriction_{\ell'}$ denote the unclipped leaf mean of
$A_i$. Given $X_{1:n}$, and hence given the leaf counts $n_{\ell'}$, the value of
$\ez$ on $\ell'$ is the single number $\ez\restriction_{\ell'}$, because
$\tau_e\in\cS_e$; so by Assumption~\ref{ass:iid} the $A_i$ with $i\in\ell'$ are
independent Bernoulli$(\ez\restriction_{\ell'})$ and
\[
  \E\bigl[(\ehat^{\mathrm{pre}}\restriction_{\ell'}
   -\ez\restriction_{\ell'})^2\bigm| X_{1:n}\bigr]
  =\frac{\ez\restriction_{\ell'}\{1-\ez\restriction_{\ell'}\}}{n_{\ell'}}
  \le\frac1{4n_{\ell'}} .
\]
This is exactly where $\tau_e\in\cS_e$ enters: without it the leaf mean of $A_i$
does not have mean $\ez\restriction_{\ell'}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:clip} clipping is
non-expansive towards $\ez\restriction_{\ell'}$, so the same bound holds after
clipping. Since $n_{0,\ell'}\le n_{\ell'}$ \emph{deterministically},
\[
  \E\bigl[n_{0,\ell'}
   (\ehat\restriction_{\ell'}-\ez\restriction_{\ell'})^2\bigm| X_{1:n}\bigr]
  \le n_{\ell'}\cdot\frac1{4n_{\ell'}}=\frac14 ,
\]
irrespective of $n_{\ell'}$, and trivially zero if $n_{\ell'}=0$. Summing over
the $L_e$ leaves and taking expectations,
$\E\bigl[\sum_{i:A_i=0}\Delta w(X_i)^2\bigr]\le c^{-4}L_e/4$.

\emph{Step 6: the rate, then the maximum.} By Step~3,
$\E[T^2]=\E[\Var(T\mid X,A)]\le C_Yc^{-4}L_e/(4n^2)$, so Chebyshev applied to
$T^2$ gives $\abs{T(\tau_e,\tau_\mu)}=O_p(\sqrt{\card{\tau_e}}/n)$ for the fixed pair.
Since $\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$ is a subset of the fixed finite set
$\cT_{\bar L}\times\cT_{\bar L}$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}, and its
cardinality does not grow with $n$, the maximum over it satisfies the same
bound; no logarithmic factor arises, because none is needed to control a maximum
over a fixed finite family, and $\card{\tau_e}\le\bar L$ throughout, giving the
stated $O_p(\sqrt{\bar L}/n)$. As $\bar L$ is fixed this is $o(n^{-1/2})$. Note that
$L_e=\card{\that_e}$ is reserved for the \emph{selected} tree and does not appear
here: the bound is over fixed pairs and must not be indexed by a data-dependent
leaf count.
\end{proof}

\noindent The index set cannot be enlarged. If $\tau_e\notin\cS_e$ then $\ez$ is
not leaf-constant on $\tau_e$ and $\Delta w$ converges to a non-zero limit
$b=w_*-\wz$; centring within the leaves of $\tau_\mu$ does not annihilate $b$, so
$\sum_{i:A_i=0}a_i^2=\Theta_p(n)$ and $\sqrt n\,T=O_p(1)$ rather than $o_p(1)$.
Sufficiency is thus required on both sides, for different reasons:
$\tau_\mu\in\cS_\mu$ makes the substitution of Step~2 exact, while
$\tau_e\in\cS_e$ licenses the collapse to one value per leaf in Steps~4--5.

Cauchy--Schwarz is not merely lossy here but fatal. It would give
$\abs T\le\{\Pn(1-A)\Delta w^2\}^{1/2}\{\Pn(1-A)(Y-\mhat)^2\}^{1/2}$, whose
second factor converges to $\E[\{1-\ez\}\sigma_0^2]^{1/2}$, bounded away from
zero because it is irreducible outcome noise rather than an estimation error. The
resulting bound is $O_p(\sqrt{L_e/n})\ge O_p(n^{-1/2})$ for every $L_e\ge1$, so
no leaf budget repairs it: the failure is at the scale of the central limit
theorem. The argument above instead exploits $\E[\varepsilon\mid X,A=0]=0$, a
cancellation unavailable to Cauchy--Schwarz, which is the reason
Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} is proved through conditional mean-zeroness and never
through smallness.

\section{Proofs for Sections~\ref{sec:crossfit} and~\ref{sec:honest}}
\label{app:honest}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit}]
\emph{Neyman orthogonality.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear}, for any
$\eta=(e,\mu)$,
$R(\eta):=\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)]
=\pi^{-1}\E[\{1-\ez\}\{\wz-w\}\{\mz-\mu\}]$, exactly. Fix a direction and set
$\eta_t=\eta_0+t(\eta-\eta_0)$, so that $\mu_t-\mz=-t(\mu-\mz)$ exactly and
\[
  R(\eta_t)/t=-\pi^{-1}\E\bigl[\{1-\ez\}\{\wz-w_t\}(\mu-\mz)\bigr].
\]
As $t\to0$ we have $w_t\to\wz$ pointwise on $\cX$, by
continuity of $e\mapsto e/(1-e)$ on $[0,1-c]$, and the integrand is uniformly
bounded, so dominated convergence gives $R(\eta_t)/t\to0$. Hence the Gateaux
derivative of $\eta\mapsto\E[\psi(O;\theta_0,\eta)]$ at $\eta_0$ vanishes in
every direction, which is Neyman orthogonality. It is immediate from the exact
bilinear form: a product of two factors each vanishing at $t=0$ has vanishing
derivative there, whatever the nonlinear dependence of $w$ on $e$.

\emph{The rate condition.} Corollary~\ref{cor:convert} bounds $\abs{R(\eta)}$ by
$(\pi c^{3/2})^{-1}\Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}\Lw{\mhat-\mz}$, the pairing being unweighted
for the propensity and control-weighted for the outcome, as forced by the factor
structure. Assumption~\ref{ass:rate} makes this $o_p(n^{-1/2})$ at each fold-wise
fit, and since $K$ is fixed the weighted sum of the $K$ fold contributions
preserves the rate.

\emph{Moments.} Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} gives $V<\infty$. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal}, $\wz\le(1-c)/c$, and under
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c) the clipped $\what$ obeys the same bound;
together with Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} this gives uniformly bounded second
moments of $\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\widehat\eta)$ and $L_2$ convergence to $\psi_0$,
which is the integrability package required by \citet{chernozhukov2018}.

\emph{The empirical-process term.} For fold $k$ write
\[
  \sqrt{n_k}\bigl\{\mathbb{P}_{n_k}-P\bigr\}
   \Bigl[\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\widehat\eta^{(-k)})-\psi_0\Bigr],
\]
where $\psi_0$ is subtracted inside the bracket: it is the \emph{differenced} term
that is negligible, the undifferenced empirical process converging to $N(0,V)$
rather than to zero. Since $\widehat\eta^{(-k)}$ is a function of observations
outside fold $k$, it is independent of the fold-$k$ observations, so conditionally
on $\widehat\eta^{(-k)}$ this is a centred average of i.i.d.\ terms with conditional
variance bounded by
$\lVert\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\widehat\eta^{(-k)})-\psi_0\rVert_{2}^2\to_p0$; hence
it is $o_p(1)$. \emph{No entropy or complexity condition on $\cT_{\bar L}$ is
required}, precisely because splitting removes the need for uniform control over
the candidate class. The conditional-mean term is $o_p(1)$ after scaling by the
previous step, and with $K$ fixed the $K$ fold contributions do not accumulate.
Applying the central limit theorem to $n^{-1/2}\sum_i\psi_0(O_i)$ and Slutsky,
with the coefficient of $\theta$ again producing the empirical treated fraction,
gives the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rate-verify}]
\emph{The rate.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}, $\Delta_j>0$ for each $j$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}, $\PP(\that_j\in\cS_j)\to1$, and on that event
$\gamma_{0,j}$ is leaf-constant, so each fitted nuisance is a leaf mean of a
leaf-constant truth on leaves of positive mass. Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} gives
$O_p(n^{-1/2})$ per leaf, and aggregating over the at most $\bar L$ leaves of
$\that_j$, with $\bar L$ fixed by Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}, gives
$\Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}=O_p(\sqrt{\bar L/n})=O_p(n^{-1/2})$
and likewise $\Lw{\mhat-\mz}=O_p(n^{-1/2})$. The product is
$O_p(n^{-1})=o_p(n^{-1/2})$, with headroom.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:anchor}]
By definition $\thetahat=\widehat\delta+\thetatil$, whence the identity
$\thetahat-\widehat\delta=\thetatil$: subtracting $\widehat\delta$ returns the
cross-fitted estimator exactly, so $\widehat\delta$ cannot recentre $\thetahat$
without discarding it, and functions only as a width term.

Rearranging, $\thetahat-\theta_0=\widehat\delta+(\thetatil-\theta_0)$, so the
triangle inequality gives, deterministically for every $n$ and every realisation,
\[
  \abs{\thetahat-\theta_0}\le\abs{\widehat\delta}+\abs{\thetatil-\theta_0}.
\]
No property of $\widehat\delta$ beyond its being a real number is used, so no
condition on $\delta_e,\delta_\mu,\Dw,\Dm$ and no smoothness condition enters.
Let $E_n=\{\abs{\thetatil-\theta_0}\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\widetilde\sigma/\sqrt n\}$;
on $E_n$ the display gives
$\abs{\thetahat-\theta_0}\le\abs{\widehat\delta}
+z_{1-\alpha/2}\widetilde\sigma/\sqrt n$, which is exactly $\theta_0\in C_n$.
Hence $E_n\subseteq\{\theta_0\in C_n\}$ and
$\PP(\theta_0\in C_n)\ge\PP(E_n)$ for every $n$. Since
$\sqrt n(\thetatil-\theta_0)\rightsquigarrow N(0,\widetilde V)$ with
$\widetilde V>0$ and $\widetilde\sigma^2\to_p\widetilde V$, Slutsky gives
$\sqrt n(\thetatil-\theta_0)/\widetilde\sigma\rightsquigarrow N(0,1)$ and
$\PP(E_n)\to1-\alpha$. Therefore
$\liminf_n\PP(\theta_0\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The conclusion is an inequality, not an equality: $C_n$ over-covers
whenever $\widehat\delta\ne0$. Since $\abs{\widehat\delta}\ge0$, the interval
contains the anchor's own interval recentred at $\thetahat$, so its length is
never below $2z_{1-\alpha/2}\widetilde\sigma/\sqrt n$. The hypothesis
$\widetilde V>0$ is what the Slutsky step consumes, and it is inherited from the
anchor's own regularity rather than imposed here; if the anchor's central limit
theorem or the consistency of $\widetilde\sigma$ fails, coverage is not
guaranteed. Theorem~\ref{thm:anchor} buys robustness against an unquantified
bias, given a valid anchor, and none against a broken one.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:width}]
\emph{The bias of $\thetahat$.} Decompose
$\thetahat-\theta_0=\{\thetahat-\theta(\widehat\eta)\}
+\{\theta(\widehat\eta)-\theta_0\}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear} at
$\eta=\widehat\eta$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:biasbound} give
$\abs{\theta(\widehat\eta)-\theta_0}\le\pi^{-1}\Dw\Dm$, while the plug-in
remainder $\thetahat-\theta(\widehat\eta)$ is $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ by the same
union bound over the finite class $\cT_{\bar L}$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:budget})
and leaf-mass floor (Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}) used in the proofs of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} and Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} --- applied here
uniformly over $\cT_{\bar L}\times\cT_{\bar L}$ rather than only
$\cS_e\times\cS_\mu$, since $\that_e,\that_\mu$ need not be sufficient in
this section --- matching the $\Pn\psi_0$ term underlying Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Hence
$\thetahat-\theta_0=O_p(\Dw\Dm)+O_p(n^{-1/2})$, and since
$\thetatil-\theta_0=O_p(n^{-1/2})$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-order}
  \widehat\delta=O_p(\Dw\Dm)+O_p(n^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}

\emph{Realised versus approximation error.} Fix $j$. The fitted tree estimates at
most $\bar L$ leaf constants, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} each leaf estimate
deviates from its population leaf value by $O_p(n^{-1/2})$; aggregating the at
most $\bar L$ leaf-wise discrepancies in the relevant norm gives a total
estimation contribution of order $\sqrt{\bar L/n}$. Applying the triangle
inequality in both directions between the realised and approximation errors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D-vs-delta}
  D_j=\delta_j+O_p\bigl(\sqrt{\bar L/n}\bigr)=\delta_j+O_p(n^{-1/2}),
\end{equation}
the last step using that $\bar L$ is fixed by Assumption~\ref{ass:budget}.

\emph{Combining.} Since $\delta_e,\delta_\mu=O(1)$, \eqref{eq:D-vs-delta} gives
$\Dw\Dm=\delta_e\delta_\mu+O_p(n^{-1/2})$, and substituting
into~\eqref{eq:delta-order} yields
$\abs{\widehat\delta}=O_p(\delta_e\delta_\mu+n^{-1/2})$. The width of $C_n$ is
$2\{\abs{\widehat\delta}+z_{1-\alpha/2}\widetilde\sigma/\sqrt n\}$, whose second
term is $\Theta_p(n^{-1/2})$ because $\widetilde\sigma\to_p\widetilde V^{1/2}>0$,
so $\mathrm{width}(C_n)\asymp_p\max\{n^{-1/2},\delta_e\delta_\mu\}$. This
contracts at the parametric rate if and only if
$\delta_e\delta_\mu=O(n^{-1/2})$, and otherwise plateaus at
$\delta_e\delta_\mu$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Relation~\eqref{eq:D-vs-delta} is the reason results proved for one
error notion do not transfer to the other. The two agree only up to
$O_p(\sqrt{\bar L/n})$, which is immaterial in Corollary~\ref{cor:width}, where
the leading term is a product of $O(1)$ quantities, but decisive in
Section~\ref{sec:realised}, where the allowance is required to dominate the
realised error of the reported fit rather than the best available approximation.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:spectest}]
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity}, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives
$\sqrt n(\thetahat-\theta_0)=\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0+o_p(1)$, and under
Assumption~\ref{ass:rate}, Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit} gives
$\sqrt n(\thetatil-\theta_0)=\sqrt n\Pn\psi_0+o_p(1)$ with the \emph{same}
$\psi_0$, the efficient influence function being a property of the model rather
than of the estimator. Subtracting, the leading terms cancel identically, term by
term, and $\sqrt n\widehat\delta=o_p(1)$.

\emph{Size.} With $\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}\asymp n^{-1/2}$, the quantity
$\sqrt n\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}$ is bounded away from zero and infinity with
probability tending to one, so
\[
  \PP\bigl(\abs{\widehat\delta}>z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}\bigr)
  =\PP\bigl(\sqrt n\abs{\widehat\delta}
   >z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt n\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}\bigr)\to0 .
\]

\emph{Consistency.} Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo} holds and consider a
fixed alternative law with $b\ne0$. The proof technique of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}, applied with the pseudo-true margin
$\Delta_j^\dagger>0$ of Assumption~\ref{ass:pseudo} in place of the
sufficient-class margin $\Delta_j$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}, gives
$\PP(\that_j=\tau_j^\dagger)\to1$ for each $j\in\{e,\mu\}$: no property of
$\Delta_j^\dagger$ beyond strict positivity is used in that argument. On that
event the selected partitions are the fixed pseudo-true partitions
$\tau_e^\dagger,\tau_\mu^\dagger$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:findim} (evaluated at
$\tau=\tau_e^\dagger,\tau_\mu^\dagger$, its general form covering
$\tau\notin\cS_j$) gives $\ehat-e_*=O_p(n^{-1/2})$ in $\Ltwo\cdot$ and
$\mhat-\mu_*=O_p(n^{-1/2})$ in $\Lw\cdot$, hence $\widehat\eta\to_p\eta_*$. By
\eqref{eq:exact-pi}, $\pihat(\thetahat-\theta_0)=\Pn\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\widehat\eta)$;
decompose
$\Pn\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\widehat\eta)=\Pn\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_*)
+\{\Pn\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\widehat\eta)-\Pn\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_*)\}$. The
second term is, exactly as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:findim}, a sum over
the fixed, finitely many leaves of $\tau_e^\dagger,\tau_\mu^\dagger$ of
leaf-value differences bounded by a fixed multiple of $\widehat\eta-\eta_*$, hence
$O_p(n^{-1/2})\to_p0$. The first term satisfies
$\Pn\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_*)\to_pP\phi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_*)=\pi b$ by
the law of large numbers, since $\eta_*$ is a fixed pair not depending on $n$
and $\phi$ is bounded under Assumptions~\ref{ass:causal}
and~\ref{ass:moments}. Combining with $\pihat\to_p\pi>0$ gives
$\thetahat-\theta_0\to_pb\ne0$, while $\thetatil-\theta_0=O_p(n^{-1/2})\to_p0$
by Theorem~\ref{thm:crossfit}, so $\widehat\delta\to_pb\ne0$ while
$\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}\to_p0$, and the rejection probability tends to one.
\end{proof}

\noindent The cancellation is exact, so no non-degenerate limiting variance
exists: the null distribution of $\sqrt n\widehat\delta$ is degenerate and the
test is conservative rather than asymptotically exact. A claim that
$\sqrt n\widehat\delta$ converges to a non-degenerate normal law with a
computable variance would be false. The same degeneracy is why
$\widehat\delta$ cannot serve as a widening term on its own: it is $o_p(n^{-1/2})$
under sparsity, hence small precisely when the bias is zero, and by
Corollary~\ref{cor:width} it is $O_p(\Dw\Dm+n^{-1/2})$ away from
sparsity, so it neither centres on nor dominates the bias. This is why
Theorem~\ref{thm:anchor} adds the anchor's full sampling width on top of
$\abs{\widehat\delta}$.

\subsection*{Status of the realised-error refinement}

The realised-error construction discussed in Section~\ref{sec:realised} is not
proved here; the four open obstacles are listed there. What is established is the
following computation, which disqualifies the plug-in~\eqref{eq:vhat} as a
conservative variance estimator away from sparsity.

Write $a=\mu_*-\mz$ and $b=w_*-\wz$ for the pseudo-true discrepancies and expand
$\psi(\cdot;\theta_0,\eta_*)$ about $\eta_0$:
\[
  \psi(\eta_*)-\psi_0
  =\pi^{-1}\Bigl[-Aa-(1-A)\bigl\{b(Y-\mz)-\wz a\bigr\}\Bigr]+O(ab).
\]
Squaring and taking expectations, the cross term is $2\E[\psi_0\{\psi(\eta_*)-\psi_0\}]$.
The treated arm contributes $-\pi^{-2}\E[\ez a\Delta m]$, using
$\E[Y\mid A=1,X]-\mz-\theta_0=\Delta m$; the control arm contributes
$+\pi^{-2}\E[\{1-\ez\}\wz b\sigma_0^2]$, using
$\E[(Y-\mz)^2\mid A=0,X]=\sigma_0^2$, while the term in $\wz^2a(Y-\mz)$ vanishes
because $\E[Y-\mz\mid A=0,X]=0$. Hence
\[
  \plim\Vhat=V+2\pi^{-2}\bigl\{\E[\{1-\ez\}\wz b\,\sigma_0^2]
   -\E[\ez\,a\,\Delta m]\bigr\}+O\bigl(\Ltwo a^2+\Ltwo b^2\bigr).
\]
Taking $b\equiv0$ and $a=\epsilon\{1-\ez\}\Delta m$ makes the bracket
$-\epsilon\E[\ez\{1-\ez\}\Delta m^2]$, strictly negative at order $\epsilon$
whenever $\Delta m\not\equiv0$ on a cell with $0<\ez<1$, against a remainder of
order $\epsilon^2$. So $\plim\Vhat<V$ for all small $\epsilon>0$. Note that
$b\equiv0$ forces $\theta(\eta_*)=\theta_0$ exactly by
Proposition~\ref{prop:bilinear}, so the centring in~\eqref{eq:vhat} is harmless in
this construction.

\section{Counterexamples}\label{app:counterex}

\subsection*{The margin is not optional in Proposition~\ref{prop:rate-verify}}

Proposition~\ref{prop:rate-verify} routes through Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}. The
following computation shows that the apparently simpler route, which uses only
finiteness of the candidate class, fails --- and fails at the boundary, which is
why the failure is easy to miss.

Suppose one argues from finiteness of $\cT_{\bar L}$ and a union bound alone,
without invoking the margin. Absent any separation between the best candidate and
its competitors, the generic guarantee for empirical risk minimisation over a
finite class is the slow rate: Hoeffding or Bernstein together with a union bound
give concentration of each candidate's empirical risk to within $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ of
its population value, and with no gap $\Delta_j>0$ nothing sharper follows for the
selected candidate. So the excess risk of $\widehat\gamma_j$ is $O_p(n^{-1/2})$.
By the loss-to-norm identity of Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(a), excess risk
equals squared $L_2$ distance, so
\[
  \bigl\lVert\widehat\gamma_j-\gamma_{0,j}\bigr\rVert
  =O_p\bigl(n^{-1/4}\bigr),\qquad j\in\{e,\mu\},
\]
and therefore
\[
  \Ltwo{\ehat-\ez}\cdot\Lw{\mhat-\mz}
  =O_p(n^{-1/4})\cdot O_p(n^{-1/4})=O_p\bigl(n^{-1/2}\bigr).
\]
This is \emph{exactly} $O_p(n^{-1/2})$, not $o_p(n^{-1/2})$, and
Assumption~\ref{ass:rate} requires the latter. The margin-free route therefore
lands precisely on the boundary and is \emph{insufficient to verify}
Assumption~\ref{ass:rate}, with no room to spare. It does not establish a
violation: that would require a matching lower bound, which is not claimed.

The margin repairs this by replacing the slow rate with exact recovery of a
sufficient structure: on the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} the truth is
leaf-constant, each nuisance converges at $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ rather than
$O_p(n^{-1/4})$, and the product gains a full factor of $n^{-1/2}$. The assertion
that the candidate set is finite and the matter therefore settled is false.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:greedy}]
Let $\cX=\{0,1\}^2$ with $X$ uniform on the four cells, and set
\[
  \ez(0,0)=\ez(1,1)=a,\qquad \ez(0,1)=\ez(1,0)=b,\qquad a\ne b,
\]
with $a,b\in(0,1-c]$, so that $\ez$ takes values in $[0,1-c]$ and
Assumption~\ref{ass:causal} holds with all four cells of positive probability.
Thus $\ez$ depends on $X$ only through $\mathbf1\{x_1=x_2\}$.

\emph{The root.} By uniformity the best constant is
$\E[\ez]=\tfrac14(a+a+b+b)=\tfrac12(a+b)$, and the excess risk is
\[
  \Var(\ez)=\tfrac14\Bigl[2\bigl(a-\tfrac{a+b}2\bigr)^2
   +2\bigl(b-\tfrac{a+b}2\bigr)^2\Bigr]
  =\tfrac14\Bigl[2\bigl(\tfrac{a-b}2\bigr)^2+2\bigl(\tfrac{b-a}2\bigr)^2\Bigr]
  =\frac{(a-b)^2}4 .
\]

\emph{Splitting on $x_1$.} The children are $\{(0,0),(0,1)\}$, carrying values
$a$ and $b$, and $\{(1,0),(1,1)\}$, carrying values $b$ and $a$; each is uniform
on its two cells. In each child the best constant is again $\tfrac12(a+b)$ and
the within-child variance is again $(a-b)^2/4$, by the identical computation. The
post-split risk is the equally weighted average of the two child variances, hence
$(a-b)^2/4$, equal to the root risk. The reduction is exactly zero.

\emph{Splitting on $x_2$.} The construction is symmetric under
$x_1\leftrightarrow x_2$, since it depends on $X$ only through
$\mathbf1\{x_1=x_2\}$, so the identical computation applies and the reduction is
again exactly zero.

\emph{Exhaustiveness.} On $\{0,1\}^2$ the only admissible first splits are on
$x_1$ and on $x_2$, and both have zero population risk reduction.

\emph{The global optimum recovers $\ez$.} The singleton partition
$\tau^*=\{\{(0,0)\},\{(0,1)\},\{(1,0)\},\{(1,1)\}\}$ has $\card{\tau^*}=4\le\bar L$
and $\ez$ is trivially constant on each singleton leaf, so $\tau^*\in\cS_e$ and
$\cS_e\ne\emptyset$ at $\bar L=4$; Lemma~\ref{lem:margin} then gives
$\Delta_e>0$. Note that the two-leaf partition
$\{\{x_1=x_2\},\{x_1\ne x_2\}\}$, which also has zero excess risk, is not
obtainable by a single axis-aligned split, being the parity function; it is
therefore not available to a depth-one tree.

Consequently a greedy recursive procedure that splits only on strictly positive
immediate risk reduction halts at the root, while the global optimiser over
$\cT_4$ attains $\tau^*\in\cS_e$ with $\Delta_e>0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The mechanism is that the first split is uninformative while the pair of
splits together is exactly informative, so a procedure that requires strictly
positive immediate gain has no signal to follow. Two caveats keep the conclusion
honest. The zero gain makes the first split a \emph{tie}, so a greedy variant that
breaks ties arbitrarily and grows to the budget does reach $\tau^*$ here: split on
$x_1$ for zero gain, then on $x_2$ in each child for gain $(a-b)^2/4>0$ each.
Cost-complexity pruning likewise succeeds, since the four-leaf tree has risk
$4\lambda_n$ against the root's $(a-b)^2/4+\lambda_n$ and $\lambda_n\to0$. So what
is established is the failure of the positive-improvement stopping rule and the
absence of any guarantee for greedy growth, not the failure of every greedy
variant. And the witness sits at $\bar L=4=M$, where
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} is vacuous; embedding the same parity block in a
larger space, for instance $\{0,1\}^2\times\{0,1,2\}$ with $\ez$ ignoring the
third coordinate, would place it in the regime $\bar L<M$ where sparsity has
content, at no cost to the computation.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:pinwheel}]
Let $d=2$ and $\cX=\{0,1,2\}^2$, with every one of the nine cells carrying
positive probability, and put
\[
  A=\{0\}\times\{0,1\},\quad
  B=\{0,1\}\times\{2\},\quad
  C=\{2\}\times\{1,2\},\quad
  D=\{1,2\}\times\{0\},\quad
  E=\{1\}\times\{1\} .
\]

\emph{Step 1: each region is a box.} Each of $A,\dots,E$ is displayed above in
the form $B_1\times B_2$ with $B_j\subseteq\{0,1,2\}$, hence each is an
axis-aligned box.

\emph{Step 2: they partition $\cX$.} Assigning the nine cells,
\[
\begin{array}{lllll}
 (0,0)\in A, & (0,1)\in A, & (0,2)\in B, & (1,0)\in D, & (1,1)\in E,\\
 (1,2)\in B, & (2,0)\in D, & (2,1)\in C, & (2,2)\in C. &
\end{array}
\]
Every cell appears exactly once, and
$\card A+\card B+\card C+\card D+\card E=2+2+2+2+1=9=\card{\cX}$. Let $\gamma_0$
take five pairwise distinct values $v_A,\dots,v_E$ on $A,\dots,E$; it is then
well defined, and $\{A,B,C,D,E\}$ is a box partition of cardinality five on whose
blocks $\gamma_0$ is constant.

\emph{Step 3: the minimal box complexity is five.} Since $v_A,\dots,v_E$ are
pairwise distinct, no box on which $\gamma_0$ is constant can meet two of the
five value classes, so any box partition on whose blocks $\gamma_0$ is constant
has at least five blocks. Step~2 exhibits one with exactly five. Hence the
minimal box complexity is five, and we set $\bar L=5$.

\emph{Step 4: no tree partition in $\cT_5$ has $\gamma_0$ leaf-constant.} Each
coordinate carries three ordered levels, so the admissible binary splits on a
coordinate are the two threshold cuts, giving four possible first splits. Each
severs one of the regions:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item $x_1\in\{0\}\mid x_1\in\{1,2\}$ severs $B$, since $(0,2)\in B$ lies left
  and $(1,2)\in B$ lies right;
  \item $x_1\in\{0,1\}\mid x_1\in\{2\}$ severs $D$, since $(1,0)\in D$ lies left
  and $(2,0)\in D$ lies right;
  \item $x_2\in\{0\}\mid x_2\in\{1,2\}$ severs $A$, since $(0,0)\in A$ lies left
  and $(0,1)\in A$ lies right;
  \item $x_2\in\{0,1\}\mid x_2\in\{2\}$ severs $C$, since $(2,1)\in C$ lies left
  and $(2,2)\in C$ lies right.
\end{itemize}
Let $\tau\in\cT_5$. If $\card\tau=1$ the single leaf is $\cX$, on which $\gamma_0$
takes five distinct values, so $\gamma_0$ is not leaf-constant. If
$\card\tau\ge2$ then $\tau$ has a root split, which is one of the four above and
severs some region $R$ into two non-empty pieces lying in the two children of the
root. Subsequent splits refine within a child and never rejoin points from
different children, so the cells of $R$ on the two sides lie in disjoint sets of
leaves, and $R$ occupies at least two leaves. Since the five regions carry
pairwise distinct values, no leaf may contain cells from two regions, so each of
the remaining four regions requires at least one leaf of its own. Therefore
\[
  \card\tau\ \ge\ \underbrace{2}_{R}
  +\underbrace{1+1+1+1}_{\text{the other four regions}}\ =\ 6\ >\ 5=\bar L,
\]
a contradiction. Hence no $\tau\in\cT_5$ has $\gamma_0$ leaf-constant and
$\cS=\emptyset$.

\emph{Step 5: unordered coordinates.} If the levels are treated as unordered, two
further first splits become admissible. The split
$x_1\in\{1\}\mid x_1\in\{0,2\}$ severs both $B$, through $(1,2)$ against
$(0,2)$, and $D$, through $(1,0)$ against $(2,0)$; symmetrically
$x_2\in\{1\}\mid x_2\in\{0,2\}$ severs both $A$ and $C$. Each forces
$\card\tau\ge2+2+1+1+1=7>5$, so admitting them only strengthens the bound. The
conclusion is unchanged: the ordered-coordinate hypothesis restricts the menu of
splits but does not rescue leaf-constancy.
\end{proof}

\noindent At $\bar L=5$ a refinement reading of sufficiency, under which it
suffices that $\bar L$ be at least the cardinality of a minimal partition into
boxes on which the truth is constant, would declare the budget adequate; yet
$\cS=\emptyset$. Every conclusion downstream then fails silently:
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection} asserts membership in an empty set, an event of
probability zero, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} loses its route.
Definition~\ref{def:sufficient} is immune because it never refers to a minimal
box partition. Positivity of every cell matters only in that it equates
$P$-a.s.\ constancy with constancy, so the construction is not an artefact of
null cells; the pinwheel symmetry, in which each of $A,B,C,D$ is severed by
exactly one axis cut around the central cell $E$, is what makes every root choice
fail at once.
