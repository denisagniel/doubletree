## 09:42 — 2026-09-16_finite-x-legibility-registries

**Goal.** Audit the manuscript's finite-$X$ framing and settle an outline/notation/claims
registry for a proposed reframing before touching `manuscript.tex` prose. Author's original
intent: state that finite $X$ is required because legibility matters, and that when $X$ is only
made finite by discretizing continuous covariates, (a) the anchor interval already adjusts
inference for this, and (b) the discretization gap is a specific, bounded form of "residual
confounding."

**What happened.**

1. **Audit.** Read `manuscript.tex` (all sections) and the relevant parts of `theory.tex`.
   Found the manuscript's *existing, deliberate* position is the opposite of the proposed
   framing: `manuscript.tex:79` states "$\cX$ need not be finite," and `theory.tex:594–618`
   explicitly calls genuinely-finite $\cX$ an "escape hatch... special to a genuinely finite
   $\cX$" that "does not survive" relaxation to general/continuous $\cX$. Also found an
   orphaned, superseded `discretization-theory-section.tex` (Besov/Hölder smoothness framework,
   marked superseded 2026-07) that looked relevant but turned out not to be reusable.

2. **Oracle consultation** (`bg_2394d715`, ~3.5 min) on whether the proposed reframing is
   technically sound. Verdict, in order of how much it changed the plan:
   - **Do not state "finite $X$ required."** It contradicts `:79` outright and would gut the
     motivation for the anchor interval (whose best use case *is* continuous $X$). Replacement:
     a **three-rung regime ladder** (audit-complete / sparse / general) as *motivation*, not a
     new assumption.
   - **"Residual confounding" is a real term (epidemiology: categorizing a continuous
     confounder — Becher; Brenner & Blettner) but the wrong mechanism here.** The decisive test
     is what fixes it: unmeasured confounding breaks identification and no second estimator
     repairs it; here `ass:causal` conditions on the *full* $X$, so $\theta_0$ stays identified —
     coarsening only enters the estimating-equation remainder (`prop:bilinear`), and
     `thm:anchor` genuinely *removes* it from the coverage guarantee. Adopted term: **coarsening
     bias / leaf-constancy misspecification**, with one disclaiming sentence citing the epi
     usage so a referee's "isn't this just confounding?" is pre-empted rather than invited.
   - **Don't resurrect the superseded Besov lemma** for "amenable to bounding" — its rate is
     inert once $\bar L$ and the grid are fixed in $n$. Use a three-line Lipschitz bound instead
     ($\delta_e\lesssim L_eh$, $\delta_\mu\lesssim L_\mu h$ for mesh $h$), explicitly labeled a
     **sensitivity device local to one proposition**, not a standing assumption. Oracle flagged
     a gap neither of us had caught: `manuscript.tex:515` already warns the bias is controlled
     by the *realized* $D_w,D_\mu$, not the population $\delta_e,\delta_\mu$ — so bounding
     $\delta$ alone doesn't bound the anchor-interval width; needs a short finite-class ERM
     excess-risk step to bridge them (cheap, since $\log|\cT_{\bar L}|=O(1)$ is already
     available, but it is the one piece of genuinely new technical work in the whole plan).

3. **Registries created/updated** in `inst/paper/`, scope resolved with the author across two
   rounds (first pass scoped only to the touched sections; author asked for the whole paper;
   clarified `theory.tex` is out of scope, matching `claims.md`'s own existing precedent):
   - **`outline.md`** (new) — full backfill of the section plan for every section/subsection in
     `manuscript.tex` (Intro through Discussion + the empty appendix), with an explicit
     "Owns (pending)" column showing where the new material would go without duplicating what
     `:79` and `:128` already say about finite/general $\cX$ (two *different* existing
     finite-$X$ discussions — positivity's uniform-vs-pointwise bound at `:128` is not the same
     point as the sparsity/legibility one being added, and the outline flags this so they don't
     get conflated later).
   - **`notation.md`** (new) — full backfill of `manuscript.tex`'s symbols (~45 rows) and
     assumptions. Found two things worth a second look later: (a) **structural sparsity**
     ($\cS_e,\cS_\mu\ne\emptyset$) is the single most load-bearing condition in the paper and has
     no numbered `\begin{assumption}` of its own in `manuscript.tex` (only `theory.tex` promotes
     it to formal `ass:sparsity`) — flagged in §2b, not fixed; (b) $\delta_e,\delta_\mu$ are used
     at `:515` but never actually defined in `manuscript.tex` itself, only in `theory.tex` —
     matters if `prop:coarsen-bound` (below) ever gets drafted. §3 "Identification status" is
     where the actual technical resolution of the coarsening-vs-confounding question now lives
     as a checkable table (which assumption governs identification vs. which governs the

     items *before* any prose exists, per the project's hard gate 2: four settled, prose-only

     `sec:trees`; the coarsening-bias remark + terminology disclaimer after `cor:width`; two
     small amendments to existing "future work" sentences, conditional on the fifth item below);
     one **explicitly not yet decided** item (`prop:coarsen-bound`, the Lipschitz/mesh width
     bound — the only piece with real technical work and no simulation coverage if adopted);
