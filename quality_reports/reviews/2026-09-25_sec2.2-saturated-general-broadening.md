#### Issue 1.1 — Strictness claim has a missing hypothesis (grid-measurability of the truth) — BLOCKING
- **Location:** `:115`, `rem:saturated-general`, sentence 3
- **Severity:** MAJOR
- **Claim in text:** "The containment is strict whenever the truth departs from additivity on the working model's link scale: no choice of coefficients reproduces such a departure, while a tree with enough leaves does."
- **Problem:** The second half is an existence claim about representing *the truth*, and it is false on the covariate space §2.2 actually permits. §2.2 opens "We do not put a restriction on $\cX$", and the companion is explicit that on a general $\cX$ the nuisances "need not be constant within a grid atom" (`theory.tex:605–606`) and that "enlarging $\bar L$ only permits finer unions of the *fixed* atoms in $\cA$, so it cannot make an otherwise-varying $e_0$ or $\mu_0$ exactly constant within a single atom" (`:608–611`). If the truth's departure from link-additivity lives *within* an atom, then no tree in $\cT_M$ reproduces it either, and the containment — while still strict as a statement about classes — is not witnessed by "the truth" at all. The sentence conflates a **class-level** strictness (true unconditionally given $\ge2$ active coordinates with $\ge2$ groups) with a **truth-level** representability claim (needs grid-measurability of $e_0,\mu_0$).
- **Why now:** the 2026-09-18 pass reportedly fixed the adjacent error ("any function on the grid" → "grid-measurable functions"); this is the same scope slip surviving one sentence later, and today's broadening makes the sentence carry more weight.
- **Suggested fix:** "The containment is strict: there are grid-measurable functions — any departure from additivity on the working model's link scale across two or more active coordinates — that no choice of coefficients reproduces and that a tree with enough leaves represents exactly."

#### Issue 1.2 — Unstated reference measure for $\Pi^\nu_\tau$; change (B) entrenches the problematic reading — BLOCKING
- **Location:** `:94–98` (the new sentence) and `:103` (the projection definition); downstream `:226–232`, `:212–214`

But the sentence now supplies a *reason for the $p_{\bx}$ factor* that the companion document shows is unnecessary, and it does so without the definition that makes the factor correct:

Two stated results then fail: `:232`'s "These projections are exactly the squared-error minimizers" (the minimizer of $R^{(e)}(\tau)=\E[\{A-\Pi_\tau e_0(\bX)\}^2]$ is the *unweighted* leaf mean), and the alignment between $\Pi_\tau e_0$ and the probability limit of the plain leaf proportion $\hat e_\tau$ in `\eqref{eq:refit}`.

The manuscript has inherited a finite-$\cX$ notation into a general-$\cX$ setting, and (B) is the first sentence to assert the general-$\cX$ reading out loud.

If $p_{\bx}$ is kept, add one clause naming $\lambda$ (the atom-normalized measure) as the integrator, so that "$\nu$-weighted projection" means $\argmin_g\int\nu\{f-g\}^2d\lambda$.

#### Issue 2.1 — The coefficient-tie robustness claim is FALSE in the regime (D) newly introduces — BLOCKING
- **Location:** `:115`, final clause of the leaf-cost sentence
- **Severity:** MAJOR
- **Claim in text:** "representing it exactly generally requires a separate leaf for each one — even under coefficient ties that collapse some of those values together"
- **Problem:** Two kinds of tie behave differently, and only one supports the claim.
  - **Cross-coordinate ties** (the claim holds). Two binary coordinates, $\beta_1=-\beta_2$: values at $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ coincide. The tied level set $\{(0,0),(1,1)\}$ is a union of atoms but is not a box — the smallest box containing both points is the whole square — so any axis-aligned tree must still use 4 leaves. ✓
  - **Within-coordinate ties between adjacent groups (the claim fails).** Let $c_1=3$ with ordered groups $1<2<3$ and $\beta_{1,2}=\beta_{1,3}$. The merged level set is the interval $\{2,3\}$ on coordinate 1, which **is** a box, hence realizable as a single leaf. The exact representation then needs $2\cdot c_2\cdots c_k<\prod_i c_i$ leaves.
  Under the previous all-binary $2^k$ statement this second case was vacuous: a within-coordinate tie on a binary coordinate means $\beta_i=0$, i.e. the coordinate is inactive and drops out of $k$. **The generalization to $c_i\ge3$ is precisely what makes the counterexample reachable, and the tie clause was carried over unchanged.** Note also that whether the merged set is realizable depends on Issue 1.3's unspecified split family — for an unordered coordinate whose only permissible splits are the pre-specified groupings, $\{2,3\}$ may not be available, so the clause's truth value is currently underdetermined by the text.
- **Suggested fix:** state the count as an upper bound with a generic matching lower bound, and scope the tie robustness: "...takes at most $\prod_{i=1}^k c_i$ distinct values, exactly that many for generic coefficients ... Ties *across* coordinates do not reduce the leaf count, since the level sets they induce are not axis-aligned; ties *within* a coordinate between adjacent groups do."

#### Issue 2.2 — The stated reason for tie-robustness is a false mathematical assertion — BLOCKING
- **Location:** `:115`, "since the level sets they induce are typically not themselves unions of grid atoms"

- **Location:** `:115`, "for coordinates with $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ categories each"
- **Severity:** MINOR
- **Problem:** §2.2 admits "permissible cutpoints **or** category groupings on each coordinate, such as age brackets, income bands, or clinical cutoffs" — i.e. a coordinate may be continuous with $c_i$ inter-cutpoint *bins*, not categories. #### Verified correct (no issue) — the $\prod_i c_i$ count itself
I re-derived this independently and it holds:
- Model $g(\hat f(\bx))=\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^k\beta_i^\top d_i(\bx)$ with $d_i$ the group-indicator encoding of coordinate $i$. The linear predictor takes the value $\alpha+\sum_i\beta_{i,j_i}$ on the combination $(j_1,\dots,j_k)$; there are $\prod_i c_i$ combinations. Two combinations collide only on a proper hyperplane in coefficient space, so for generic coefficients all $\prod_i c_i$ values are distinct; a strictly monotone link preserves distinctness. ✓ ("generic coefficients" is stated, which is the right hedge.)
- $2^k$ in the all-binary case: $\prod_i 2=2^k$. ✓
- **Leaf count, both directions.** Upper: a tree splitting each active coordinate fully and no inactive coordinate has exactly $\prod_i c_i$ leaves, each a box. Lower: any tree partition on whose leaves the function is constant has at least as many leaves as the function has distinct values. So $\prod_i c_i$ is exact, generically — a statement *stronger* than `theory.tex:621`'s "at most $\prod_{j\le s}J_j$", and correctly so.
- "This is a genuine cost only once $\Lbar$ falls below that product; it does not arise at the saturated budget": ✓, since $\prod_{i\in\text{active}}c_i\le\prod_{i\le p}c_i=M=\Lbar$ at saturation.
- **Direction of the fix is right:** the old $2^k$ was inconsistent with the companion's product form; the new $\prod_i c_i$ agrees with it.

#### Issue 5.1 — The broadening is sound but the *strictness* does not generalize; the resulting antecedent ambiguity is the real risk — BLOCKING
- **Location:** `:115`, sentences 2–3
- **Severity:** MAJOR
- **First, the part that is correct.** I looked specifically for a hidden GLM-specific step in the containment argument, as instructed, and there is none. The GLM argument is: fitted value $=g^{-1}(\hat\alpha+\hat\beta^\top d(\bx))$ with $d$ grid-derived $\Rightarrow$ the fitted value factors through the atom map $\bx\mapsto a(\bx)$ $\Rightarrow$ it is constant on atoms $\Rightarrow$ it is a leaf-constant function on the saturated $\tau\in\cT_M$ (which exists and has exactly $M$ leaves, reachable by splitting every coordinate fully). Every step is pure measurability; none uses linearity, the link, the loss, or the coefficient count. So **(C)(i) is sound**, and the added caveat "restricted to the same grid-measurable inputs" is the correct and sufficient hypothesis, modulo one edge case: the fitted value must be a function of the declared inputs *alone*. A learner that touches raw $\bx$ anywhere else — a nearest-neighbour tie-break on the raw coordinate, distance weights computed pre-binning — is not grid-measurable, and the remark's phrasing "restricted to the same grid-measurable inputs" would let a reader through. The stated condition earlier in the sentence ("whose fitted value is itself grid-measurable") is the right one; the later paraphrase is the looser one.
- **Now the problem.** The sentence that follows — "The containment is strict whenever the truth departs from additivity on the working model's link scale: no choice of coefficients reproduces such a departure" — has an antecedent that the new sentence has just made ambiguous. Bound to the GLM, it is (modulo §1.1) fine. Bound to the broadened claim, it is false twice over: a grid-restricted random forest has neither a link scale nor coefficients, so the criterion is undefined for it; and more importantly the containment against such a class is generically an **equality**, not strict — a forest of grid-restricted trees grown to purity reproduces exactly the atom-wise fit, which is precisely what the saturated tree $\cT_M$ delivers. So the broadening preserves the remark's *title* claim ("representationally maximal") while silently voiding its *comparative* claim, which is the only part that does argumentative work elsewhere in the paper.
- **Net effect on traceability:** the remark is cited downstream (`:152` in `rem:legibility-not-complete`, `:788` in the Discussion) for a comparative point — trees give up nothing in representation relative to the working model. That point survives only against the GLM. The broadened sentence adds a true statement with no comparative content, and one false reading.
- **Suggested fix:** either place the new generalization sentence *after* the strictness sentence, or name the GLM in the strictness sentence explicitly, and add the honest one-clause consequence: against a grid-restricted flexible learner the containment is an equality, not strict.
